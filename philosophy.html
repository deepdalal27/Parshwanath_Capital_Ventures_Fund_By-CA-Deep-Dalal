Strategy & Philosophy

Buy quality businesses at sensible prices, and let time do the heavy lifting.

A flexible multi-strategy mandate across the Indian capital markets, applied through a research-first process and a long-term ownership mindset.

01Philosophy

What we believe before we invest anything.

Strategy is downstream of belief. These are ours, stated plainly enough that you can hold us to them.

The Fund Manager is a firm believer in long-term and value investing: buy quality businesses at sensible prices, hold with conviction, and let time and compounding do the heavy lifting.

Every investment is treated as a long-term ownership stake rather than a trade. That prioritises durable fundamentals, a margin of safety and patient capital over short-term market noise — and it applies equally to a position entered through an anchor allocation in a primary issue as to one bought in the secondary market.

It also sets the standard for what we will not own. We are sector-agnostic, but we look for scalable businesses, clean governance and credible promoter track records, and the absence of any one of those is sufficient reason to decline.

I

A share is a fraction of a business

Value comes from the cash a business will produce for its owners. Sentiment, flows and narrative affect the price you pay — never the value you receive.

II

Price paid is the risk taken

Quality bought without regard to valuation is a deferred loss. We insist on a margin of safety between what a business is worth and what is being asked for it.

III

Governance is not a soft factor

Clean governance and a credible promoter record are gating criteria. No valuation is attractive enough to compensate for a management team we do not trust.

02Mandate

Four ways the mandate is expressed.

Flexible by structure, selective by discipline. The same selection standard applies whichever route an opportunity arrives through.

01

Primary markets

Anchor investor participation in mainboard and SME IPOs, pre-IPO placements and qualified institutional placements of quality issuers. Each issue is underwritten on business fundamentals and our own view of intrinsic value, formed before the price band is considered.

02

Listed equities

High-conviction positions in listed companies, held with an ownership mindset and sized by conviction and downside. The Category III structure permits long and short exposure, used where the risk-reward justifies it rather than as a default.

03

Special situations

Rights issues, buybacks, open offers and other event-driven opportunities across market capitalisations — situations where a corporate action, rather than a change in the underlying business, has created the mispricing.

04

Sector coverage

The mandate is sector-agnostic. Research depth is currently concentrated in capital market players, gems and jewellery, gold lenders, specialty chemicals and waste management, and we extend coverage as the work is done rather than in advance of it.

03Process

How an opportunity becomes a position.

A swift decision cycle, but never a shortened one. Every stage has an explicit reason to stop.

01

Origination

Opportunities reach us from issuers, lead managers and intermediaries, and from our own screening of the listed universe and the corporate-action calendar. We are willing to look early — at DRHP and pre-filing stages, under non-disclosure agreement where required.

02

Fundamental analysis

Financial statements, cash conversion, capital allocation history and the durability of the business are examined before any view is taken on price. The gap between reported profit and collected cash is where most losses begin, so it is where we begin.

03

Governance & promoter review

We assess the promoter's track record, the quality and consistency of disclosure, related-party behaviour and the history of how minority shareholders have been treated. A business we like on the numbers is still declined when this review fails.

04

Valuation & margin of safety

Intrinsic value is assessed as a range under conservative assumptions, across sectors and market capitalisations. In a primary issue that assessment is formed independently, and only then compared against the price band on offer.

05

Decision & sizing

Size follows conviction and downside rather than enthusiasm, and reflects the liquidity and any lock-in attaching to the instrument. The internal decision cycle is deliberately short so that issuers and lead managers receive a clear answer within their timetable.

06

Ownership & review

Positions are held with a long-term orientation and reviewed against the thesis that justified them. We exit on a broken thesis, on valuation that no longer offers a margin of safety, or when a better idea competes for the capital — not on price movement alone.

04Risk

Risk management is a system, not an instinct.

The mandate's flexibility is there to manage risk, not to manufacture returns.

Control

Concentration

High-conviction positions are sized against how much could be permanently impaired, and against how correlated that impairment is with the rest of the book.

Control

Liquidity & lock-in

Sizing accounts for the days required to exit at normal volumes and for any mandatory lock-in — a particular discipline for anchor allocations and SME issues.

Control

Governance veto

Documented governance concerns are an absolute bar to investment, irrespective of the valuation or the size of the allocation on offer.

Control

Cash as a position

When the opportunity set is unattractive we hold cash rather than deploy for the sake of being invested. Doing nothing is a permitted outcome.

An honest statement about risk. A Category III AIF investing in listed equities and primary issues can and will experience periods of significant drawdown. Anchor allocations carry lock-ins that prevent an immediate exit, and allotment in an oversubscribed issue may be partial or nil. Our framework is designed to make drawdowns survivable and recoverable — not to eliminate them. Any investor who cannot tolerate a material temporary decline in capital should not invest in this Fund.
