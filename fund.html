Why Category III

Alternative Investment Funds in India are regulated under the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012 and fall into three categories. Category III covers funds that employ diverse or complex strategies, including investment in listed and unlisted derivatives.

We selected it because the mandate genuinely requires that breadth. A single vehicle has to be able to take an anchor allocation in a primary issue, hold a listed business for years, and participate in a buyback or open offer when a corporate action creates an opportunity.

Why open-ended

The Fund is constituted as an open-ended scheme. Subscription and redemption mechanics, including any applicable notice periods, lock-in and permitted windows, are set out in the private placement memorandum. Investors should read those provisions carefully rather than assume daily liquidity.

On leverage. Category III AIFs are permitted to employ leverage subject to SEBI limits and disclosure requirements. Read the PPM for the Fund's specific policy, and understand that any use of leverage or derivatives magnifies losses as well as gains.

03Onboarding

How an investment is made.

Five steps. We move at the pace of your diligence, not ours.

01

Introductory conversation

A direct discussion with the Fund Manager about your objectives, horizon and liquidity needs. If the Fund is not a suitable fit, we will say so at this stage.

02

Documentation

On confirmation of eligibility we share the private placement memorandum, contribution agreement and risk disclosure documents for review by you and your advisers.

03

KYC & compliance

Know-your-customer verification, PAN and bank validation, FATCA and CRS declarations and, for non-resident investors, applicable FEMA documentation.

04

Commitment & funding

You execute the contribution agreement recording your commitment, and capital is funded in accordance with the mechanics set out in the fund documents.

05

Reporting & access

Units are allotted and recorded. You receive periodic statements, NAV reporting, audited annual financial statements and direct access to the Fund Manager.

AIFs are intended for investors with the financial capacity and sophistication to evaluate and bear the risks of the strategy, and the regulation sets the floor accordingly.

No. Open-ended describes the Fund's constitution, not a promise of on-demand liquidity. The strategy also holds instruments that carry their own lock-ins, such as anchor allocations in IPOs. Treat a commitment to the Fund as long-term capital and read the PPM's liquidity provisions before investing.

A mutual fund is a highly regulated, daily-liquid pooled vehicle open to retail investors, with tight limits on concentration and derivatives. A portfolio management service holds securities in your own demat account and is managed on an individual basis. A Category III AIF is a pooled vehicle for eligible investors that permits concentrated positions, anchor participation in primary issues, long and short exposure, and meaningful cash holdings, with taxation applied at the fund level. Each suits a different objective.

Anchor allocations carry a mandatory lock-in, so the position cannot be exited immediately even if the thesis changes or the price falls sharply on listing. Allotment in an oversubscribed issue may be partial or nil, so capital earmarked for an issue may not be deployed as intended. Newly listed companies have shorter public track records and can be more volatile and less liquid than established ones. SME issues carry additional liquidity risk. These risks sit alongside ordinary equity market risk.

The Fund is a trust overseen by Axis Trustee Services Limited, an independent trustee, and portfolio securities are held with a SEBI-registered custodian rather than by the investment manager. The Fund's financial statements are audited annually by independent auditors. These safeguards address operational and custodial risk; they do not protect against market losses.

Under prevailing Indian law, income of a Category III AIF is generally taxed at the fund level rather than passed through to investors, and distributions are made net of applicable taxes. Treatment depends on the nature of the income, the residential status of the investor and the law in force at the time. This is a general description and not tax advice — obtain independent tax advice before investing.

A full statement of risk factors is in the private placement memorandum, which every prospective investor must read.
