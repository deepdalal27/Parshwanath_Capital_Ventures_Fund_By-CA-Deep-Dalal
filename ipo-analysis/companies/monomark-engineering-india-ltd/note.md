## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a Chittorgarh (Rajasthan) industrial services contractor, incorporated in 2005 (p. 185). It earns its revenue on large customers' plant sites by supplying:
  - trained manpower;
  - an owned equipment fleet;
  - consumables.
- **Three service lines (pp. 185-188):**
  - **Industrial operations and maintenance (O&M):** running and maintaining smelters, mines, refineries and port terminals for the plant owner, on 2-5 year contracts priced on bills of quantities and KPI score cards (pp. 27, 35). This was 80.1% of FY26 revenue and 89.8% of Q1 FY27.
  - **Industrial project execution:** mechanical erection, installation and commissioning of plant and structures, either as on-site fabrication-erection-installation-commissioning (FEIC) or as a fuller EPC scope. This was 16.9% of FY26 revenue.
  - **Metal fabrication:** structural steel at its own 4,000 MT a year plant in Kapasan. This was 2.3% of FY26 revenue.

- **Who pays:**
  - **Metals:** 83.7% of FY26 revenue, through Hindustan Zinc's mines and smelters, Vedanta's aluminium and copper units and Kutch Copper.
  - **Cement:** 9.0%.
  - **Ports:** 4.0% (Adani Ports, from FY25).
  - **Engineering/OEMs:** 3.2% (p. 189).
  - **Concentration:** the Vedanta group, including Hindustan Zinc, was **77.9% of FY26 revenue and 83.3% of Q1 FY27** (p. 190). Hindustan Zinc alone was 35.4% of FY26 revenue and has been a customer since 2010 (p. 27).
- **How it earns its margin:**
  - **Labour-heavy cost base:** employee cost was Rs 295.7 cr, 52.6% of FY26 revenue. The workforce was 6,339 at Mar-26 and 6,823 at Jun-26 (pp. 86, 138).
  - **Thin but steady margins:** gross margin is about 14% (O&M 14.4%, projects 13.6%, fabrication 5.3% in FY26), and EBITDA margin has held at 8.2-8.7% for three years (p. 138).
  - **What it owns:** a fleet of 232 cranes, hydras, excavators, trucks and loaders (p. 189).
  - **What it hires:** further machines, Rs 15.65 cr in FY26 (p. 189), and subcontracted work, Rs 64.89 cr in FY26 (p. 428).
- **Order book:** Rs 1,761.92 cr unexecuted at 30-Sep-2026, which is 3.1x FY26 revenue. O&M is Rs 1,696.47 cr of it (p. 189). New orders were Rs 331.5 / 422.9 / 499.9 cr in FY24-26 (p. 138).

### Stage of the business

**Scaling.** Revenue has grown about 21% a year since FY23. Over FY24-26 the mix has moved towards multi-year O&M:
- **O&M:** up from 64% to 80% of revenue.
- **Fabrication:** shrank as the plant ran at 22% of capacity in FY26 and 3% in Q1 FY27 (p. 210).
- **Ports:** a new line from FY25, Rs 22.43 cr in FY26 at a 17.6% gross margin (p. 400).

For valuation this argues for steady growth with low capital intensity, offset by thin margins and working capital that grows with revenue. The order book gives visibility, but it is almost all with one group of customers.

## 2. Industry outlook

- **Market size:** the Indian industrial O&M services market grew from USD 2.74 bn in FY22 to USD 3.46 bn in FY26, a 6% CAGR (p. 168). The drivers are:
  - higher capacity use: RBI's survey shows 77.4% in Q4 FY26 (p. 166);
  - ageing plants in metals, cement and power;
  - outsourcing by plant owners;
  - stricter safety and energy-efficiency rules (pp. 166-168).
- **Structure:** highly fragmented. Many small contractors compete on price, and skilled site manpower is scarce (p. 172). Clients are large groups (Vedanta, Adani, UltraTech, Shree Cement), which hold pricing power.
- **Competitors:** the RHP compares the company with:
  - **Power Mech Projects:** erection and O&M for power and industrial plants. Revenue Rs 6,062 cr FY26, +15.8%, EBITDA margin 11.6%.
  - **Thejo Engineering:** maintenance services for mining, cement and steel. Revenue Rs 632 cr, +14.4%, EBITDA margin 12.2%.
  - **ANI Integrated Services:** O&M manpower. Revenue Rs 254 cr, +11.8%, EBITDA margin 3.4%.
  - **Thermax:** capital goods (p. 139).
- **The issuer against peers:** its growth (+18.3%) is faster than these peers; its margin (8.7%) sits between ANI and the larger peers.
- **Customer capex:** metals and cement capex is a tailwind (p. 175). The base business is recurring plant upkeep, and it is less cyclical than new-project erection.
- **Industry report:** D&B (Dun & Bradstreet Information Services India), commissioned and paid for by the company for the issue (p. 8).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Incumbency and switching costs:** the company has served Hindustan Zinc since 2010 and Vedanta since 2020 (p. 196). 87-94% of revenue came from repeat clients in FY24-26 (p. 138). Mid-contract changes of O&M contractor at a running smelter are costly for the owner. This is the main source of durability.
- **Scale in its niche:** about 6,800 site staff and a 232-unit owned fleet let it mobilise quickly for large sites (pp. 138, 189). Capital needs are low: fixed-asset turnover was 8.5x in FY26 (p. 138).
- **Limits:**
  - **No pricing power:** the contracts are re-tendered every 2-5 years against many competitors (p. 172).
  - **One group:** the moat is a relationship moat with one group.
  - **No technology:** there is no proprietary technology or brand.

The moat is moderate and depends on renewals with the Vedanta group.

### Is the reported P/E right?

The test finds one recurring item that the profit and loss account does not carry. Reported FY26 earnings therefore **flatter** the P/E by about 16%:

- **Gratuity remeasurement losses (included):** actuarial losses on the gratuity plan of Rs 7.02 / 6.79 / 5.45 cr pre-tax in FY24-26 (and Rs 0.65 cr in Q1 FY27) go to other comprehensive income every year (p. 86).
  - **Why it counts:** a loss in all three years, on a workforce of 6,000-7,300, indicates that the service cost charged to profit understates the true cost of the obligation.
  - **Effect:** after tax, FY26 PAT falls from Rs 25.90 cr to **Rs 21.83 cr**. Total comprehensive income was Rs 21.84 cr.
- **Loss on sale of fleet assets (not included):** Rs 1.42 / 1.07 / 2.04 cr (p. 87). It recurs with routine replacement of trucks and cranes, so it is a running cost. Shown so the test stays two-sided.
- **Interest on fixed deposits (not included):** Rs 2.29 cr in FY26 (p. 87), mostly margin money for bank guarantees, which is operational.

Checked with no adjustment needed:
- other income is under 0.6% of total income (p. 417);
- no capitalised development cost, goodwill or revaluation;
- tax is paid in cash at 20-32% of PBT.

**Sensitivity (Neutral DCF, Rs per share):**
- **WACC and growth:** WACC of 12.5% to 16.5% gives Rs 47.5 to Rs 33.9 (at g = 4.0%). At WACC 14.46%, g of 3.5% to 5.5% gives Rs 39.0 to Rs 40.6.
- **Margin and working capital:** a 1-point higher margin (9.4%) adds about Rs 5.4. Each 3 points of working capital costs about Rs 2.5. At 9.4% margin and 15.4% working capital, the DCF is Rs 47.7.

**Why the methods differ (DCF 27% below relative):** the business earns an 8.4% EBITDA margin and must carry working capital equal to about 18% of revenue. So most of the extra operating profit goes back into receivables and unbilled revenue, and free cash flow stays small relative to earnings. Listed peers are priced on earnings, at a median of 18.8x. Giving the market's pricing of peers its due weight, the 50:50 blend is used.

**Reverse DCF in plain words:**
- **Current cash flow:** the price asks for about 344 times current free cash flow in perpetuity, which is not meaningful because FY26 FCF was close to zero after the receivables build.
- **What it means:** the price assumes either margins well above the 8.2-8.7% of the last three years, or working capital falling well below 18% of revenue. The objects plan points the other way.
- **Objects floor:** the cap and the objects floor coincide at about Rs 65. That is the price the company needs to fund Rs 120 cr of working capital plus GCP from 2.70 crore shares, and it sits above the fair value range.

### What would change our view

- **Upwards:** the call would turn Subscribe at about Rs 52.5 or below. Above our Neutral, these would also move it: EBITDA margin sustained above 9.5%, or working capital kept near 18% of revenue after the issue, with CFO above 80% of PAT for a full year.
- **Downwards:** the call would turn Avoid above about Rs 67.4 (with Bull below the price). Non-renewal of a major Hindustan Zinc or Vedanta contract would also move it down.
- **A new customer:** order wins taking non-Vedanta revenue above 35% would reduce the concentration discount implied in the Bear case.

### Risks

- loss or re-pricing of Vedanta-group contracts;
- site accidents (KPI deductions, p. 35);
- wage and labour-law cost increases;
- inventory deployed at client sites with limited control (Rs 49.3 cr of consumables, p. 41);
- the recourse obligation under the SBI vendor-finance line.

## 9. Conclusion

**Call: Neutral**, judged at the upper band of **Rs 65**.
- **The rule:** the Neutral fair value of **Rs 47.2** is 27.4% below the price, which is inside the -10% to -30% band of the Fund's rule (ROUTINE.md hard rule 6), so the Neutral limb applies.
- **Range:** fair value is Rs 38.6 / 47.2 / 52.2 (Bear / Neutral / Bull).
- **At the floor:** at Rs 60 the Neutral value is 21.3% below.
- **No material issue** was found under the Fund's rules.

What decides it:
- **Quality against price:** a steady, scaling O&M contractor with a large order book and 20%+ ROCE, priced at 24.1x reported (28.6x adjusted) FY26 earnings against an 18.8x peer median.
- **Cash and customers:** thin margins, working capital that absorbs most of the profit, and dependence on one customer group for about four-fifths of revenue.

The issue money mainly funds faster supplier payment, so it does not add earnings by itself.
