## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** mines and sells non-coking (thermal) coal from the Talcher and Ib Valley coalfields in Odisha. It is a wholly owned subsidiary of Coal India and the largest single coal producer in India, with **21.0% of domestic production in FY26** (p. 230).
- **Mines:** 17 operational mines, 14 opencast and 3 underground. Opencast mines produced 99.85% of FY26 output (pp. 39, 124).
- **Volumes (MT):**

  | | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 | H1 FY27 |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | Production | 206.10 | 225.17 | 218.31 | 87.54 |
  | Dispatch | 199.02 | 210.46 | 210.74 | 111.24 |
  | Stripping ratio | 1.34 | 1.50 | 1.50 | 1.31 |

  Source: p. 124. H1 FY27 dispatch ran at 127% of production, drawing down pithead stock.
- **Reserves:** audited reserves of 9,840.31 MT under the JORC Code, about **45 years** at FY26 output, or about 25 years if output grows 4.7% a year. Geological resources are 40.34 bn tonnes, 9.8% of India's (p. 230; DMT and CRISIL reports).
- **Customers (p. 229):**
  - Power generators (state, central and independent) took 68.62% of FY26 sale of products; captive power plants 16.57%; others (sponge iron, aluminium, traders) 14.81%.
  - **82.12% of sales are under long-term fuel supply agreements (FSAs)** at prices notified by Coal India. The rest goes through e-auctions, at a premium that moves with the market.
  - Customers are in 16 states. NTPC is the largest customer.
- **Logistics:** rail carried 65.28% of FY26 dispatch (58.70% in FY24) as first-mile connectivity projects came on stream. Sea-borne coal goes through Paradip, Dhamra, Gopalpur, Vizag and Gangavaram (p. 229).
- **People:** 20,526 employees at Sep-26, down from 21,493 in FY24 (p. 125). Most overburden removal and much of the mining is done by contractors: contract expenses were 22.34% of FY26 revenue (p. 28).
- **Diversification so far:** 57.21 MW of solar capacity (p. 229). This is small next to the coal business.

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures come from the CRISIL report commissioned and paid for by the company (p. 18). We have not checked them independently.

- **Demand:** India's coal demand (at a standard grade) rose from 896 MT in FY20 to 1,176 MT in FY25. CRISIL projects **1,495 MT by FY31**, about 4% a year, driven by power demand (p. 167).
- **Supply:**
  - India produces over a billion tonnes a year. Coal India's subsidiaries produce about 74% of it (768 MT in FY26).
  - Captive and commercial mines auctioned since 2020 are taking a growing share.
  - Imports fill the gap in coking coal and some thermal grades.
- **Pricing:**
  - FSA prices are administered by Coal India and change rarely.
  - E-auction premiums swing with international coal prices and domestic stock levels. They fell sharply in FY25-26, which is the main reason MCL's revenue fell (pp. 511-512).
- **Long-term question:** coal still generates most of India's power. India has a 2070 net-zero target and is adding solar and wind fast. Most forecasts, CRISIL's included, see coal demand growing through the 2030s and then levelling off.
- **Read for an investor:** a steady, regulated, cash-rich industry. Volume growth is low-single-digit. Prices are set by policy as much as by markets. The long-term demand peak is a real but distant risk.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Resource position:** about 45 years of reserves in two of India's largest coalfields, held under statute (p. 230).
- **Cost:** opencast mining at a stripping ratio of about 1.5 makes MCL among the lowest-cost producers in India. It has the highest EBITDA and PAT margins among Indian coal peers (p. 121).
- **Contracts:** long-term FSAs with large power utilities, and rail links to their plants.
- **Limits:**
  - Prices are administered, so the company cannot capture all of a rise in market prices.
  - Commercial miners are adding competing supply.

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- India's largest single coal producer, 21% of output (p. 230)
- About 45 years of audited reserves (p. 230)
- Operating EBITDA margin about 42%; RoNW 54% (pp. 121, 124)
- No debt; about Rs 16,085 cr of net cash at Mar-26 (pp. 86-87)

**Weaknesses**
- Revenue and profit have fallen for two years (p. 88)
- Prices are set by Coal India and the government, not by the company
- No insurance cover on assets (auditor's emphasis, pp. 67-69)
- No independent directors on the board (p. 42)

**Opportunities**
- Coal demand growing about 4% a year to FY31 (p. 167)
- More rail-led dispatch lowers cost and raises volume (p. 229)
- Coal gasification and solar diversification

**Threats**
- Energy transition reduces coal demand after the 2030s
- Wage revisions and contractor costs rise faster than prices
- Delays in paying by state power utilities (p. 24)
- Tax demands (contingent liabilities 56% of net worth, p. 24)

## 4. Management analysis and governance

- **Ownership:** Coal India holds 100% before the offer, with six nominee holders (p. 110). It will hold 90% after the offer. The President of India, acting through the Ministry of Coal, is the other named promoter. Coal India's cost is Rs 0.26 a share (cover).
- **Board:**
  - Uday Anantrao Kaole is Chairman-cum-Managing Director. Sanjay Kumar Jha is Director (Technical) and has more than 35 years in coal mining (p. 229).
  - Directors are appointed by the President of India through the Ministry of Coal.
  - **There are no independent directors at the RHP date** (p. 42). The SEBI Listing Regulations require at least six, including a woman. The company wrote to SEBI on 31-Aug-2026 asking for exemptions from the board and committee composition rules. Until the Ministry appoints independent directors, it may be penalised for non-compliance. This is a known pattern in PSU listings, not a sign of wrongdoing.
- **Audit:**
  - The statutory auditor, Kadmawala & Co, is appointed by the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG).
  - The opinion is **unmodified**. Its emphasis-of-matter and other-matter paragraphs cover (pp. 67-69):
    - no insurance on the company's assets;
    - Rs 76.55 cr of unplanned depreciation flagged by the C&AG;
    - the non-appointment of independent directors;
    - the new labour codes;
    - CARO remarks on reconciling MSME dues and GST records.
- **Related parties:** executives are seconded from Coal India, and the company pays Coal India for shared services. Full related-party tables were not compiled in this run.
- **Read:** a government company run as part of the Coal India group. The governance gaps are the usual ones for a PSU. None is a material issue under the Fund's rules. The main point for a minority shareholder is that dividends, prices, wages and capital spending are decided within the group and by the Ministry.

### Is the reported P/E right?

**Verdict: the reported FY26 P/E of 8.4x understates the multiple. On recurring earnings it is 9.7x.**

FY26 profit includes two items the company itself lists as non-recurring (pp. 512-513):

| Item (pre-tax, Rs cr) | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest on income-tax and indirect-tax refunds | 19.25 | 1,163.92 |
| Liabilities written back | 154.16 | 695.38 |

- The write-back includes a **Rs 604.8 cr reversal of a water-charges provision**, which is now shown as a contingent liability instead.
- Taking both out after tax at 25.17% lowers FY26 PAT to about **Rs 9,286 cr**, EPS Rs 14.03 and **P/E 9.7x**.
- On the FY24-26 average PAT, the P/E is 8.1x reported and 8.5x adjusted.

**For information only:**
- **The stripping-activity reversal in revenue** (Rs 489.70 cr in FY26) is a non-cash credit. It recurs every year under the company's stripping-ratio accounting, so we keep it in the adjusted P/E. We take it out of the margin used in the DCF.
- **Other income excluding the one-offs** is about Rs 1,500-1,950 cr a year (FY24-26), largely interest on Rs 15,000-20,000 cr of bank deposits (our reading). It is real but depends on the cash staying in the company. The DCF values the cash directly instead.

**Checked, no adjustment:**
- Profit is taxed in cash, at 22-24% of PBT.
- There is no goodwill or acquisition accounting.
- The audit opinion is unmodified.

### What would change our view

- **Towards Neutral:**
  - Margin falling below about 38% for a full year.
  - A cut in the dividend payout.
  - A large tax demand crystallising.
- **Firmer Subscribe:** a revision of notified FSA prices, or sustained dispatch growth above 5%.
- **Price:** the call stays Subscribe up to about Rs 156 (Neutral value within 10%).

### Risks

- Lower coal prices or demand, including from the energy transition (p. 20 onwards).
- Wage revisions and contractor costs.
- Land acquisition, forest and environmental clearances for new mines.
- Mining accidents; the assets are uninsured (pp. 67-69).
- Tax and land-compensation disputes (p. 24).
- Government control of prices, dividends and board appointments (p. 42).

## 9. Conclusion

**Subscribe at Rs 136 (upper band). The Neutral fair value is 3.4% above the cap.** The band was confirmed by the company on 16-Nov-2026.

**The investment case.**
- India's largest single coal producer: 21% of national output and about 45 years of reserves.
- Operating EBITDA margin of about 42%, no debt and Rs 16,000 cr of net cash.
- It pays out about 80% of profit, a 9.3% trailing yield at the cap.
- Priced at 8.4x reported (9.7x recurring) earnings, in line with Coal India and below the 9.9x peer median.

**What holds the value back.**
- Revenue and recurring profit fell in FY25 and FY26 as coal prices eased.
- FY26 profit includes about Rs 1,859 cr of one-off income.
- Margins have slipped from 46% to 40%.
- The board has no independent directors, and the government sets prices, wages and dividends.
- Coal's long-term demand is capped by the energy transition.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 127 / **Rs 141** / Rs 151 (Bear / Neutral / Bull).

**Call rule.** At Rs 136 the Neutral value is 3.4% above the upper band, better than the -10% limit, so the call is **Subscribe**. On recurring earnings the Neutral value is about Rs 130 (-4%), still within the Subscribe limb. No material governance or accounting issue was found. This is a yield-and-value call, not a growth call: the upside is modest and most of the return is expected to come as dividends.
