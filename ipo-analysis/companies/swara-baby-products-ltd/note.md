## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a Pithampur (Madhya Pradesh) maker of disposable hygiene products, set up in 2016 and making baby diapers since 2018 (p. 262). It runs four plants with 20 production lines on 24 acres at Pithampur and Indore (p. 263).
- **Two ways of selling:**
  - **Contract manufacturing** for other brands, priced on purchase orders that customers can cancel (p. 25): 76.1% of FY26 revenue and 85.9% of Q1 FY27 (p. 263).
  - **Its own brands, Shields and Cuddles:** 20.9% of FY26 revenue but only 9.7% in Q1 FY27 (p. 263).
- **Product mix (share of product sales, p. 262):**

| Baby diapers | 88.0% | 84.1% | 79.1% | 68.2% |
| Adult incontinence | 10.2% | 14.4% | 16.1% | 17.0% |
| Feminine hygiene (KAEHPL, from 25-Dec-2025) | - | - | 2.7% | 8.9% |

| **Sale of products (Rs cr)** | **741.08** | **933.38** | **1,153.30** | **320.34** |

- **Who buys:**
  - **Brainbees Solutions (FirstCry):** the 76.59% promoter and the largest customer, 27.0% / 23.5% / 22.6% of revenue FY24-26 and 26.2% in Q1 FY27 (p. 24).
  - **Top 10 customers:** 85.6% / 84.5% / 80.3% FY24-26 and 72.7% in Q1 FY27. Named customers include Piramal Pharma and Himalaya Wellness (pp. 24, 263).
  - **Exports:** 1.5% of FY26 revenue (p. 263).
- **How it earns its margin:** a converter of nonwovens, pulp and super-absorbent polymer.
  - **Gross margin:** 32.3% / 34.2% / 30.7% FY24-26 and 28.3% in Q1 FY27 (p. 172).
  - **Government subsidies:** a large part of profit, tied to its Madhya Pradesh investments (part 5).
- **Scale and use:**
  - **Units produced:** 1,237 / 1,492 / 2,033 million FY24-26.
  - **Lines:** 12, 15 and 20 at the year-ends.
  - **Utilisation:** 63.7% / 55.4% / 64.9%, against 70% that the company treats as full (p. 172).

### Stage of the business

**Scaling, with heavy investment ahead of demand.**
- **Evidence:**
  - revenue up 25.7% and 23.4% in FY25 and FY26;
  - lines up from 12 to 20 in two years;
  - two acquisitions in Dec-2025;
  - a Rs 554.85 cr new plant of which Rs 234.39 cr is already spent (p. 138).
- **Valuation consequence:** cash flow over the next two years is absorbed by capex and working capital, so value rests on what the new capacity earns from FY28 on.

## 2. Industry outlook

- **Market size (TKC report, commissioned for the offer):**
  - **Hygiene market:** Rs 26,850 cr in FY26, projected at Rs 43,840 cr by FY30, a 13.0% CAGR (p. 208).
  - **Diapers:** Rs 15,330 cr in FY26 growing to Rs 25,160 cr by FY30.
  - **Mix:** baby diapers 51.7% of hygiene spend, feminine hygiene 40.4%, adult incontinence 6.4% (p. 262).
- **Drivers:**
  - low penetration;
  - urbanisation and rising income;
  - online and D2C brands that outsource manufacturing and want short runs and fast changes (p. 168).
- **Structure:**
  - **Brands:** a few multinationals and many new D2C brands.
  - **Swara's position:** the largest Indian contract manufacturer, with an estimated **38% share of baby-diaper contract manufacturing and 36% of adult diapers** in FY26. Sanitary napkins are much smaller at 4.3% (p. 168).
- **Risks to the outlook:**
  - large brands can make in-house;
  - customers face no switching contract;
  - resin and pulp prices pass through only with a lag.
- **Listed peers:** none comparable. The RHP says there are no listed companies in India or abroad with a comparable model and scale (p. 170).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Scale and cost:** the largest contract maker, with 20 lines clustered in central India and SAP-based operations. Scale lowers unit cost and allows short runs for many brands. Durability is **moderate**: lines can be bought by any well-funded rival or brand, and the moat is not contractual.
- **Customer embedding:** a "land-and-expand" record from single products to multi-category supply (p. 169). It is helped by a promoter that is itself the largest customer, which secures volume but limits independence.
- **Subsidy position:** Madhya Pradesh capital and interest subsidies lower the effective cost of new lines (p. 41). This is policy-based, not a business moat.

### Is the reported P/E right?

The test finds that **government subsidies make up much of reported profit**, so reported earnings **flatter** the P/E by about 32%.

- **Government grant income (included):** Rs 11.38 / 15.03 / 38.14 cr pre-tax FY24-26 (p. 90), 47% of FY26 profit (p. 42). The schemes are:
  - MPIDC capital or investment assistance for each plant;
  - TUFS;
  - a textile PLI of Rs 31.98 cr (p. 419).
- **Why it counts:** the money is real over time (grants received Rs 51.79 / 18.69 / 77.59 cr), but it is scheme-bound and time-limited:
  - MPIDC assistance runs 7 years and PLI 5 years;
  - disbursement depends on approvals, and Rs 18.05 cr was overdue at Jun-26;
  - FY26 includes Rs 13.98 cr accrued on a Swara Hygiene sanction with nothing received by Mar-26 (p. 419).
- **Interest income on subsidy (included):** Rs 2.21 / nil / 2.70 cr is accounting interest on the discounted value of future subsidy receivables (p. 42).
- **Effect:** after tax, FY26 PAT falls from Rs 95.59 cr to **Rs 65.03 cr**.
- **Interest subsidy netted in finance cost (not included):** Rs 2.42 / 1.58 / 4.40 cr (p. 42). It is tied to specific term loans; shown for information.
- **Licence fee from Brainbees (not included):** Rs 5.50 cr in FY26 (p. 44), recurring while the agreement runs.

Checked with no adjustment needed:
- tax at 21-22% of PBT, paid in cash;
- share-based payment charged to profit;
- gratuity remeasurement losses under Rs 0.14 cr;
- goodwill not amortised.

## 7. Valuation: DCF and peer comparison (P2P)

### Peer comparison (P2P)

**No listed peer is reported from the offer document.** The RHP states that no listed company in India or abroad is comparable in business model and scale, and gives no industry P/E (p. 170). The relative method is therefore not used. Fair value is the DCF alone, as for other no-peer issuers on this desk.

For context only, two reference points:
- **Dec-2025 share swaps at Rs 22.50:** the company's own shares were issued at Rs 22.50 (adjusted) for two acquisitions from the promoters (pp. 175-176). The cap is 4.9x that price eleven months later.
- **Earnings multiple:** FY26 P/E of 33.8x reported and 49.7x excluding subsidies.

### DCF and fair value

**Inputs (FY26; Rs crore):**
- Revenue 1,163.90 and **operating EBITDA 144.23** (excluding other income).
- D&A 51.04, net worth 553.22.
- Net debt **443.88**: borrowings 443.06 plus leases 5.09, less cash and bank balances 4.27. It includes the Brainbees loan (p. 88).
- Trade working capital 218.56.

**Treatment of the issue and capex:**
- **Fresh issue:** Rs 500 cr credited net of 6% assumed expenses (Rs 470 cr), because the RHP prints the expenses as [●].
- **IPO capex:** Rs 198.21 cr, 55% in Y1 and the rest in Y2 per the schedule (p. 136).
- **Organic capex:** D&A plus 15% of each rupee of revenue growth. FY26 net capex implies 38%, but the Project carries the next capacity step and the existing lines have headroom to 70% utilisation.
- **Capital grants:** treated as a reduction of capex. Grant receivables of Rs 103.49 cr are not added.
- **OFS:** it does not change the share count. ESOP options of 20.49 lakh are included in the 29.14 crore diluted count.

**Cost of capital (house CAPM):**
- Risk-free 6.5%, beta 1.10, ERP 7.0%, size premium 2.0%: cost of equity 16.2%.
- Pre-tax cost of debt 9.0%; debt weight 20%.
- **WACC 14.31%**; terminal growth 4.50%.
- Terminal value is about 97% of enterprise value in the Neutral case, because the explicit years carry the IPO capex and working capital. **The DCF is mostly a terminal estimate.**

| Rs per share | Bear | Neutral | Bull |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DCF (= fair value; no peers) | 2.3 | 23.2 | 34.9 |
| Against Rs 111 (upper band) | -97.9% | -79.1% | -68.5% |

- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF):**
  - WACC 12.3-16.3% gives Rs 32-18.
  - Margin 12-16% gives Rs 16-31.
  - Working capital 12.8-24.8% of revenue gives Rs 27-20.
  - **No plausible single input bridges the gap to Rs 111.** Each point of margin is worth about Rs 4 a share, so the price implies an operating margin well above the FY24 peak of 18.3%, sustained with much lower reinvestment.
- **Objects floor:** not applicable; the fresh issue is amount-based.
- **Reverse DCF:** the price asks the business to deliver 86.9 times its current free cash flow, in perpetuity. **In plain words: the price assumes the new capacity is filled at margins well above recent levels and that subsidies, or something like them, last.**
- **Why the gap is so wide:**
  - A market cap of Rs 3,212 cr is set against operating EBITDA of Rs 144 cr and subsidy-assisted PAT of Rs 96 cr.
  - In the Neutral case free cash flow is negative by about Rs 130 cr in FY27-28 while the new plant and working capital are funded.

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

### Growth drivers to track

- Commissioning of the new Pithampur plant on schedule (FY28) and its utilisation.
- Feminine hygiene growth through KAEHPL (8.9% of Q1 FY27 product sales).
- Recovery of own-brand revenue (20.9% in FY26, 9.7% in Q1 FY27).
- New contract customers beyond Brainbees (top 10 down from 85.6% to 72.7%).

### Points to monitor

- Operating EBITDA margin: 12.4% FY26 and 9.5% in Q1 FY27.
- Government grant income and disbursement: Rs 38.14 cr in FY26, with Rs 103.49 cr receivable at Mar-26 and Rs 18.05 cr overdue.
- Brainbees' share of revenue (26.2% in Q1 FY27) and the terms of the Rs 102.0 cr on-demand loan.
- Cash conversion: CFO 71% of PAT FY24-26.
- CARO remarks on audit trail and on bank statements not matching the books.

### What would change our view

- Operating EBITDA margin back above 16% for two consecutive quarters with utilisation rising.
- Free cash flow after capex turning positive before the Project is complete.
- A price nearer our value: the Neutral call would apply at about Rs 35 or below, and Subscribe at about Rs 26.

### Risks

- A large customer insourcing or switching, given cancellable purchase orders (p. 25).
- Delay or reversal of subsidies (p. 42).
- Raw material inflation (pulp, nonwovens, super-absorbent polymer) not passed through.
- Cost or time overrun on the Project, which has not been independently appraised (p. 137).

## 9. Conclusion

- **Call: Avoid**, judged at the upper band of Rs 111.
- **Fair value:**
  - Neutral Rs 23.2 (79.1% below), Bear Rs 2.3 (-97.9%), Bull Rs 34.9 (-68.5%).
  - On the DCF alone, because there are no listed peers.
- **Limb applied (hard rule 6):** Neutral is more than 30% below the price and Bull is also below it, so the Avoid limb applies. The result is the same at the Rs 105 floor.
- **What decides it:**
  1. **Price against earnings:** the price is about 50x the profit the plants earn without government subsidies, and the operating margin has fallen from 18.3% in FY24 to 9.5% in Q1 FY27.
  2. **Investment ahead:** the business has a large investment programme to fund before it generates free cash, and depends on its promoter Brainbees for a quarter of its sales.
- **Material issue:** none found under the Fund's rules. The call follows from valuation.
