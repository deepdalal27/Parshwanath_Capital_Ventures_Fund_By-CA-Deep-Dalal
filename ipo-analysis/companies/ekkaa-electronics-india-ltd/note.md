# Ekkaa Electronics (India) Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 24-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **196 days old** at the date of this note. **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> **FY26 is consolidated; FY24 and FY25 are standalone.** The company bought its subsidiary Ekkaa Electronics Industries Private Limited (EEIPL) on 29-Aug-2025 (p. 362). Manufacturing began only in 2024, so FY24 revenue is ₹0.71 m. Growth between the restated years is therefore not like-for-like. The DRHP supplies unaudited proforma figures that treat the subsidiary as owned throughout (pp. 375–388); this note uses them for growth and margin comparisons and says so each time.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹182.63**, the price at which an outside investor (Abakkus Four2eight Opportunities Fund) subscribed to Series B preference shares on 17-Jul-2026, adjusted for the 3:1 bonus and 1:2 split (pp. 99, 100). It is a private placement price, not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The RHP must carry financials no more than six months old and should therefore include H1 FY27. No capital should be committed until the RHP is read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths: very fast, profitable scale-up with capacity to spare.**
- Revenue from operations rose from ₹0.71 m (FY24) to ₹4,965.14 m (FY25) to ₹12,217.63 m (FY26). On proforma figures it grew 63.6% in FY25 and 72.3% in FY26 (p. 137).
- PAT was ₹653.79 m in FY26 (5.35% margin); proforma PAT rose from ₹20.51 m (FY24) to ₹353.77 m to ₹706.94 m.
- Installed capacity of 5.2 m units ran at 37.80% utilisation in FY26 (p. 247), so volume can grow before more capital is spent.
- Tax is paid at the concessional 17.16% rate: current tax was 18.3% of FY26 PBT (p. 350).
- The last outside placement priced the equity at ₹182.63, 73.5% above the ₹105.25 Series A price of Sep–Oct 2025 (pp. 94–99).

**Concerns: no operating cash, heavy debt, concentration and related-party origins.**
- Operating cash flow was negative in all three years: −₹152.83 m, −₹641.12 m and −₹286.59 m, against cumulative PAT of ₹848.81 m (p. 75).
- Borrowings were ₹3,087.54 m at 31-Mar-2026 and ₹3,961.43 m by 31-Jul-2026; promoters guarantee ₹3,833.93 m of them (p. 43).
- LED televisions are 71.78% of revenue; the top 10 customers (unnamed) are 65.77%; one supplier is 46.94% of raw-material purchases (pp. 22, 23, 29).
- 42.9% of the fresh issue repays debt, and 28.6% funds working capital; none funds new capacity (p. 120).
- The subsidiary was owned by two promoter-directors until August 2025. It was the issuer's largest customer in FY25 (21.01% of revenue) and was bought from them for ₹490.00 m (pp. 78, 274, 362).

At the ₹182.63 reference the model's neutral blended value is ₹169.15 (−7.4%). Its DCF value is ₹76.21 and its relative value ₹308.54: **the two methods disagree by 75%, and that gap is the finding.** The relative method prices FY26 earnings against two very large listed peers; the DCF prices cash, and the cash is negative. **H1 FY27 operating cash flow and receivables in the RHP will show which reading holds.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** finished consumer electronics and appliances made to a customer's design or its own: LED televisions (up to 98 inches), semi-automatic washing machines, personal and commercial air coolers, multimedia speakers, induction cooktops and smart displays. It also presses metal back covers for televisions (pp. 242–243).
- **To whom:** more than 450 customers in 20 states and union territories, described as retail brands, online platforms and other businesses, some of which sell the products under their own brand. No customer is named (pp. 23, 243).
- **How it earns:** a manufacturing margin on bought-in components. FY26 gross margin was 16.98% and EBITDA margin 9.37% (p. 137). The business is a pass-through of imported parts (open cells, PCB parts, optical parts, motors) with design, assembly and pressing added; raw material purchases were 91.7% of total expenses (p. 28). Supplier credit is short: payable days were 46 against 44 receivable days (p. 127).
- **What it owns vs outsources:** plants at Noida (four SMT lines, robotic press shop), Ganaur (coolers, commissioned January 2026) and, through the subsidiary, Sonipat (televisions). A 13-engineer R&D team in Noida designs products. Some land is held on lease (pp. 243, 273).
- **Origin:** the company was incorporated in March 2021 and began manufacturing in March 2024. The subsidiary EEIPL has made televisions since 2019 (p. 243). The promoter family (Sagar, Chandra Prakash and Madhuri Gupta) held 98.27% of the equity before the preference shares convert (p. 108).

## Industry analysis

Source: a report by **Frost & Sullivan, commissioned and paid for by the issuer** under an engagement letter of 18-Mar-2026 (p. 242). Treat its estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** the Indian consumer appliances market is put at ₹2,055 bn in FY26, growing at a 13.7% CAGR to ₹3,433 bn by FY30. The LED television market was ₹469 bn in FY25, projected to reach ₹859 bn by FY30 (12.9% CAGR from FY24). Washing machines (₹163 bn, 7.9% CAGR), coolers (₹120 bn, 6.8%) and induction cooktops (₹9 bn, 7.5%) grow more slowly (pp. 168–184).
- **Structure:** brand owners increasingly outsource television and appliance assembly to ODM and EMS firms. Large listed names include Dixon Technologies, PG Electroplast, Amber Enterprises and Syrma SGS; Radiant Appliances is named as a television partner for a large brand (pp. 134, 219–225).
- **Unnamed competitors:** smaller domestic assemblers and imported finished goods.
- **Regulation and cyclicality:** component import duties and trade policy, BIS certification, and state electronics subsidies (the issuer receives an interest subsidy under the Uttar Pradesh Electronics Manufacturing Policy 2020, ₹13.96 m in FY26, p. 411). Coolers and air-conditioning products are seasonal.

## Valuation

Neutral drivers: revenue growth 25% / 20% / 15% / 12% / 10% over five years, EBITDA margin 8.5%, trade NWC 22% of revenue, terminal ROIC 19%, tax 17.16%, cost of debt 8.3% (facility rates 7.25–9.35%, pp. 122), WACC about 16.6%, terminal growth 4.5%. Each driver and its cite is in the workbook (Assumptions sheet). Peers are two of the issuer's three named listed names: Dixon Technologies and PG Electroplast, per share; Amber Enterprises is excluded as an air-conditioner component and EMS business (p. 134). A 55% discount to peer median is applied for size and cash flow, and DCF is weighted 60:40 against relative. Net debt is adjusted for the ₹1,000 m of Series B proceeds received after the balance-sheet date, to match the share count.

Use of proceeds funds no capex in any case, so the Bear and Neutral cases do not differ on that. **DCF and relative diverge by 75% in the neutral case.** The relative method applies 48.6x and 121.5x earnings multiples of two large listed groups; the DCF discounts free cash flow that is negative today. Terminal value is above 75% of enterprise value, so the DCF is mostly a terminal assumption. The relative figure rests on two peers and should not be relied on alone.

**At the ₹182.63 reference:** the price is 20.2x FY26 basic EPS of ₹9.04, 23.5x EPS on the fully diluted pre-issue count (₹7.77) and 5.3x NAV of ₹34.79 per share. Issuer-named peers trade at 48.6x to 139.5x earnings (p. 134). The price is 9.2x and 10.4x the promoters' average costs of ₹19.87 and ₹17.53. Post-money ROE on FY26 PAT is about 7.7% (assuming 5% offer expenses, which the DRHP does not state).

**Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple can be stated and the entire price rests on a turn that has not yet begun. The turn would come from converting ₹2,520 m of inventory and ₹2,186 m of receivables to cash and from the 9.4% EBITDA margin holding as volumes grow.

## What would change our view

- **Cash conversion:** H1 FY27 operating cash flow turning positive and above 50% of PAT (FY26: −₹286.59 m).
- **Working capital:** trade working capital back below 15% of revenue; receivable days under 50 on a closing basis (FY26: 65).
- **Price band:** a floor price below about ₹164, the neutral blended value on current inputs.
- **Customer concentration:** top customer below 15% and top-10 below 55% of revenue, with customers named and confirmed as unrelated.
- **Supplier concentration:** top supplier below 35% of raw-material purchases (FY26: 46.94%).
- **Leverage:** borrowings at or below ₹2,500 m after the debt object, with guarantees released or reduced from the ₹3,833.93 m now guaranteed.
- **Governance:** confirmation that the independent director who is an IBBI valuer is not the person who valued the August 2025 share swap, and a board with independent directors serving a full audited year.
- **Accounting:** one treatment of the subsidiary purchase across the restated and proforma statements.
- **Margins:** FY26 other expenses (6.39% of revenue) explained between volume-linked and one-time items.

## Risks

- Dependence on one product (LED televisions, 71.78%) and a small group of unnamed customers.
- Imported components and foreign-exchange exposure; supplier concentration at 46.94%.
- Leverage and interest cost: debt rose by ₹873.89 m in four months to 31-Jul-2026; promoters guarantee almost all of it.
- Related-party history: the subsidiary, its loans to the issuer and the acquisition were all with promoter-directors.
- A short operating record: manufacturing started in March 2024; FY24 and FY25 figures are special-purpose accounts.
- Execution of a ramp-up from 37.80% utilisation and new product lines (coolers, speakers, cooktops at 2–16%).
- Market-size and share claims rest on a report the issuer commissioned and paid for.
