# Ekkaa Electronics (India) Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 24-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Audited (restated) financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **177 days old** at the DRHP date (204 days at the date of this note); there is no H1 FY27 data. **No price band has been announced.** The offer is up to ₹7,250 m (₹725 cr): a fresh issue of up to ₹5,250 m and an offer for sale of up to ₹2,000 m by two promoters. Lead managers: Motilal Oswal Investment Advisors and Shannon Advisors. Holding period assumed: 2 to 3 years. Grey market premium is not used.
>
> Upside is measured against a **range of two assumed prices, not a band**: a floor of **₹182.63**, the price paid in cash for the Series B CCPS on 17-Jul-2026 (₹100.0 cr, after split and bonus; p. 142), and a cap of **₹226.00**, the price of the promoters' secondary transfers on 10 to 21-Sep-2026, the latest priced transactions in the DRHP (pp. 102-104). The peer-median P/E of 121.5x is not used to set the reference (see part 7).
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. FY26 is the first year that includes the subsidiary Ekkaa Electronics Industries (acquired 29-Aug-2025), and FY24 revenue was ₹0.07 cr, so the RHP with H1 FY27 will be the first like-for-like read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths**
- Revenue rose from ₹0.07 cr (FY24) to ₹496.5 cr (FY25) and ₹1,221.8 cr (FY26); on the issuer's unaudited pro-forma basis FY26 revenue is ₹1,263.1 cr, up 78.1% on ₹709.3 cr (pp. 74, 137).
- FY26 EBITDA margin 9.37%, PAT margin 5.35%, ROCE 18.0% and interest cover 3.6x (EBIT ₹100.9 cr over finance cost ₹27.9 cr) (pp. 74, 137).
- Installed capacity is 5.20 m units (LED TVs 2.82 m); the Noida LED TV line ran at 55.6% in FY26, so volume can grow before more capex (pp. 247, 250).
- The last priced equity was institutional: a Category I/II AIF paid ₹100 cr for 6.5% of the fully diluted capital in July 2026 (pp. 106, 142). Litigation is a ₹93,433 wage claim and ₹0.99 m of tax matters (pp. 434-435).

**Points to monitor**
- Operating cash flow was negative in all three years: ₹-15.3 cr, ₹-64.1 cr, ₹-28.7 cr, ₹-108.1 cr in total against cumulative PAT of ₹84.9 cr. FY26 absorbed ₹97.3 cr in receivables and ₹38.9 cr in inventory (p. 75).
- Top 5 customers were 52.4% and top 10 65.8% of FY26 revenue; the top supplier was 46.9% of raw-material purchases, and imports were 76.2% (pp. 23, 29, 41).
- FY25's largest customer (21.0% of revenue, ₹104.3 cr) was Ekkaa Electronics Industries, then owned by two of the promoters; it was still 6.2% of FY26 revenue before the 29-Aug-2025 acquisition (pp. 23, 78-80, 274).
- Net debt is ₹296.0 cr (2.58x EBITDA); borrowings rose from ₹308.8 cr at 31-Mar-2026 to ₹396.1 cr at 31-Jul-2026 (pp. 73, 42).

The cash-flow method and the peer-multiple method are far apart: the DCF gives a Neutral ₹102 per share, the peer-median relative method ₹630. The DCF alone is 55% below the ₹226 cap; the blend is 62% above it only because of the peer multiples. **The price band, the peer set and H1 FY27 cash conversion decide this case.**

## 1. Business of the company

Ekkaa is a business-to-business original design and original equipment manufacturer of consumer electronics and appliances. It makes LED televisions (24 to 98 inch; QLED, 2K, 4K), semi-automatic washing machines, personal and commercial air coolers, multimedia speakers, induction and infrared cooktops, and smart displays, to the order of retail brands, online platforms and regional players who sell them under their own labels (pp. 242-243). It also presses metal back covers and moulds plastic cabinets in-house and runs four SMT lines at Noida, each rated at 250,000 products a month (p. 243). It offers samples in seven days and customised product in 15 to 35 days (p. 243). The R&D centre at Noida has 13 engineers (p. 243).

The company was incorporated in March 2021, started production at Noida in 2024, and commissioned a cooler plant at Ganaur in January 2026. Its subsidiary, Ekkaa Electronics Industries Pvt Ltd (EEIPL), began TV assembly at Sonipat in 2019 and was bought from the promoters on 29-Aug-2025 in a share swap (81.14% for new shares valued at ₹888 per ₹10 share, then the balance in tranches) (pp. 243, 274).

## 2. Industry outlook

The industry data come from a Frost & Sullivan report dated 22-Sep-2026 that the issuer **commissioned and paid for** exclusively for this offer (p. 242); it is treated as the issuer's estimate. It sizes India's consumer-durables ODM market at ₹24,900 cr in FY26, growing at 16.9% a year over FY21-26 and projected at 19.9% a year to ₹51,380 cr in FY30. The wider consumer electronics and appliances market it covers is ₹46,900 cr in FY25 and ₹85,900 cr in FY30 (p. 245). Ekkaa puts its share of the LED TV market by volume at about 5.5% in FY26 (p. 245).

Structure: brands outsource design and assembly to ODM/EMS firms to cut overheads and time to market; the report lists reliance on imported open cells, PCBs and chipsets among the limits on local value-added, and exposure to exchange rates and import policy (industry chapter, pp. 156-241). The DRHP's listed peers are Dixon Technologies, Amber Enterprises and PG Electroplast (pp. 134-135); each is between 4 and 40 times the issuer's revenue. Demand for TVs and white goods is discretionary and cyclical, and the report notes that brands may adopt multi-vendor strategies (industry chapter).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

**Moat: narrow and unproven.** The evidence for an edge is scale (5.2 m units of capacity), partial backward integration (metal pressing, injection moulding, SMT), speed (samples in seven days) and licences with global smart-TV operating-system providers (pp. 243-244). The evidence against a durable moat is that the product is built to a customer's order, customers can multi-source, the top 5 are 52.4% of revenue, there are no long-term contracts with suppliers, and gross margin is flat at 17% while revenue rose 146% (pp. 23, 30, 137). Switching costs for customers are low and the cost edge depends on volume. Over two to three years this reads as an execution advantage, not a moat; the RHP's customer-retention and repeat-order data would test it.

The chief executive is Praveen Goyal (p. 245). The DRHP gives the promoters' collective industry experience as over 25 years (p. 245).

None has served a full audited year, and two of the three also sit on the board of Skyways Air Services, an IPO managed by Shannon Advisors, one of this offer's lead managers (pp. 286, 457).

**Pay.** FY26 remuneration was ₹0.875 cr (Sagar Gupta), ₹1.05 cr (Chandra Prakash Gupta), ₹0.28 cr (Madhuri Gupta) and ₹0.18 cr to two relatives, 3.6% of PAT in all (pp. 78-80).

Between 10 and 21-Sep-2026 the promoters also sold about 0.8% of the capital in small lots at ₹226 per share to named buyers, including Awa Endeavor LLP, which holds Series A CCPS (pp. 102-104). The basis for offer price lists no secondary transaction (p. 143).

**Related parties.** The material dealings are with EEIPL while it was promoter-owned: in FY25 sales ₹104.3 cr (21.0% of revenue), purchases ₹89.4 cr (18.0%), loans taken ₹170.9 cr and interest ₹8.4 cr; in FY26 sales ₹75.8 cr (6.2%) and purchases ₹36.3 cr (3.0%) (pp. 78-80). These are disclosed, and the proceeds do not repay any promoter or related-party loan.

**Auditor and banking.** M S K A & Associates LLP was appointed on 27-Nov-2025 after the death of the previous auditor's proprietor (p. 83). It restated all three years. FY24 and FY25 were first audited on a special-purpose basis by the earlier auditor (p. 311). Lenders in the repayment list are Axis, HDFC, ICICI, Kotak, Yes Bank and Bajaj Finance (pp. 122-124). The audit trail was enabled throughout FY26, but backup logs were kept only from 18-Feb-2026 (p. 372).

**Governance read.** The family controls the board and the majority; the independent directors are new. Disclosure is detailed and the litigation record is clean. The points to follow are the related-party history with EEIPL and how the new independent directors act on the audit committee.

### Is the reported P/E right?

The test looked for costs capitalised, provisions released, expenses deferred, policy changes and one-offs. Reported FY26 PAT is ₹65.38 cr. One item changes recurring earnings: ₹0.85 cr of creditors written back, which reduces PAT by ₹0.64 cr after tax (p. 346). Adjusted PAT is ₹64.74 cr, a 1.0% change, so **the reported P/E is fair on a one-year basis**: 37.1x reported and 37.5x adjusted on post-offer capital at ₹226. Three items are shown but not applied: the ₹1.40 cr interest subsidy (recurring while the policy runs), the ₹10.1 cr unrealised exchange loss (a non-cash charge that raises earnings if it reverses, but exchange movements are part of an importer's cost) and capitalised interest (₹1.15 cr in FY26; ₹7.25 cr in FY24, standard for plants under construction) (pp. 75, 332, 346). The three-year average P/E is 85.8x reported and 86.5x adjusted, which reflects a loss in FY24 and a small profit in FY25 and is not a basis for pricing.

## 6. Future outlook and projections

**What the DRHP says about the future.** No order book is disclosed. Growth levers named are new categories (blower coolers, fully automatic and larger semi-automatic washers), more capacity use at Noida and Ganaur, and the ODM market's growth (pp. 243, 253, 257). The only capital plans in the objects are debt repayment and working capital; no capex is funded (p. 120). **Why DCF and relative differ (-84%).** The relative method applies a median P/E of 121.5x and P/B of 5.7x to a company that earns a 5.35% PAT margin; the peers' trailing multiples are between 1.7 and 6 times the 23.5x to 29.1x at which the issuer's own investors have recently bought. Narrowing the peers to Dixon alone leaves the relative value at ₹623, because Dixon trades at 17.7x book. The DCF prices the cash a 9.3% EBITDA-margin business with a working-capital intensity of 18% can release, and 88.4% of its enterprise value is the terminal value, so it is mostly a view on long-run margin and returns. The two methods have separated on whether the market will pay for the growth and the scale that peers have shown, or for the cash this business has yet to produce. **The relative value is not reliable here** and the Fund does not rest a view on it: it measures what listed peers fetch, not what a new, thin-margin ODM would. The DCF does not rest on the market's pricing of peers at all. The truth lies between, and the price band will show where the offer sits.

**Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple of it can be stated: the price assumes cash generation improves from here. Holding the Neutral growth path, justifying ₹182.63 needs a steady-state EBIT margin of 25.1%, and ₹226 needs 30.1%, against 8.3% in FY26; it is unreachable through working capital alone (the 22.4% intensity would have to turn negative). Three of the four levers are not meaningful or unreachable, and the fourth asks for three to four times today's margin. A lower price or evidence of a higher through-cycle margin would change this.

**Objects floor.** None: the fresh issue is amount-based, so no share count fixes a floor price. Identified objects are ₹375 cr (71.4% of ₹525 cr); expenses and general corporate purposes (up to 25% of gross proceeds, p. 120) take the remaining ₹150 cr.

**Guards.** Terminal value share 88.4% (above the 75% amber line); WACC less terminal growth 9.8% (clear); terminal ROIC 18% above WACC (clear); divergence of -84% (above the 25% line, explained above).

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

**Growth drivers to track:** LED TV volume against the Noida line's 55.6% utilisation; the ramp of coolers (₹25.6 cr of revenue in FY26) and of cooktops and speakers, which are at 10.5% and 2.3% utilisation; new customers beyond the top ten; the share of revenue outside LED TVs (28.2% in FY26).

**Points to monitor** (normal-course items, each reported once):
- *Cash conversion.* Operating cash flow ₹-108.1 cr over three years while revenue grew 17,000-fold from a near-nil base; receivables 65 days with 52.8% past due (pp. 75, 337).
- *Customers.* Top customer 16.9%, top 5 52.4%, top 10 65.8% (80.4% in FY25); names withheld (p. 23).
- *Suppliers and imports.* Top supplier 46.9% of purchases (31.9% in FY25) with no contract; imports ₹802.6 cr, 76.2% of purchases; unrealised exchange loss ₹10.1 cr (pp. 29, 41, 75).
- *Related-party history.* EEIPL sales were 21.0% of FY25 revenue and 6.2% of FY26; the Sonipat lease rises to ₹0.50 m a month (pp. 78-80, 304).
- *Tax rate.* Current tax 18.3% of PBT in FY26 and 15.6% cumulative against 25.17%; the reconciliation was not located (p. 74).
- *Diluted EPS.* The printed ₹9.04 excludes the CCPS shares; ₹7.77 on the full count (pp. 70, 133).
- *Debt.* Borrowings ₹396.1 cr at 31-Jul-2026 against ₹308.8 cr at 31-Mar-2026; the Bajaj Finance term loan of ₹65 cr disbursed 17-Jun-2026 for land and general purposes carries no prepayment right for 24 months, then 2% (pp. 42, 122).
- *Purchase accounting.* The restated accounts book a ₹2.04 cr bargain-purchase capital reserve on EEIPL; the pro-forma shows ₹8.50 cr of goodwill (pp. 340, 381).
- *Eligibility.* The offer uses Reg 6(2): 75% to QIBs or it fails (p. 448).

**What would change our view** (measurable, from the RHP and H1 FY27 results):
1. Operating cash flow positive for H1 FY27, or cumulative CFO/PAT above 50% for the full year.
2. A top-5 customer share below 45% and the top supplier below 35% of purchases.
3. Receivables past due below 30% and closing receivable days below 60.
4. The upper end of the band against the ₹182.63 paid by the last institutional investor (23.5x fully diluted FY26 EPS): a band at or below it would narrow the gap to the DCF value; a band above ₹226 (more than 29x) with no improvement in cash flow would widen it.
5. Net debt to EBITDA below 2.0x after the issue and no further rise in borrowings between March and the RHP date.
6. The top-10 customer list and the supplier named, with any link to the promoters stated.
7. A reconciliation of the 18.3% current-tax rate.

## 9. Conclusion

**Screen only.** There is no price band, and the DRHP is a screen. The call rule (hard rule 6) needs a band, so no limb is applied. The evidence is balanced: a business that has grown revenue to ₹1,222 cr in two years at a 9.4% EBITDA margin and 18% ROCE, with capacity to grow into and a clean legal record, set against three years of negative operating cash flow, concentrated customers and suppliers, 2.6x net debt to EBITDA and a related-party history with the company it bought.

At the assumed reference range of ₹182.63 to ₹226.00, the Neutral DCF value is ₹102 (-44% to -55%), the Bull DCF ₹152 (-17% to -33%), and the peer-multiple value ₹630, which we do not rely on. The blended Neutral value is ₹366 (+62% against the cap). That figure comes entirely from peer multiples between 1.7 and 6 times those at which the issuer's own investors bought. **The fair value range on the cash-flow method is ₹53 to ₹152 with ₹102 as the Neutral point; on the market's pricing of listed peers it is far higher.**

Two things decide the case: whether the RHP and H1 FY27 show operating cash flow turning positive as revenue grows, and where the price band sits against the 23.5x to 29.1x the last priced transactions imply. No capital should be committed until the RHP is read.
