## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a regional jewellery retailer selling gold, diamond and CZ jewellery through its own showrooms; it buys finished jewellery from third-party manufacturers in Maharashtra and Gujarat rather than making it (pp. 156-157).
- **Footprint:** five leased showrooms, three in Mumbai and two in Ahmedabad, 6,513 sq ft in all (p. 91). The two Ahmedabad showrooms opened in FY24 and gave 62.40% of 9M FY26 revenue (77.17% in FY25) (pp. 21, 23).
- **Range:** more than 17,452 SKUs (13,322 gold designs, 4,130 diamond and stone designs) across 11 collections, from about Rs 11,000 upwards; bridal and daily wear (p. 157).
- **History and structure:** the promoter has sold jewellery since 1999 through his proprietorship, M. K. Sons Jewellers. The company (incorporated 2012) took over that business on 29-Mar-2025 under a business succession agreement, in exchange for 40,38,152 shares valued at Rs 71.07 cr (pp. 156, 189). The full-year effect of the merger therefore shows only from FY26.

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures come from the CareEdge report commissioned and paid for by the issuer (p. 156); we have not checked them independently.

- **Market:** gems and jewellery are about 7% of India's GDP and 15% of merchandise exports; domestic jewellery demand is driven by weddings, festivals and gold's role as savings (pp. 111 onwards, 156).
- **Shift to organised retail:** hallmarking (BIS), GST and consumer preference for trusted brands are moving share from unorganised jewellers to organised chains, regional and national (p. 111 onwards).
- **Gold price:** high gold prices raise ticket sizes and the value of inventory, but can cut volumes; small retailers usually do not hedge (p. 143 onwards).
- **Competition:** national chains (Tanishq, Kalyan, Malabar, Senco) and strong regional players; listed peers named are Motisons, Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri, Senco Gold and PNGS Reva (p. 100).
- **Read for an investor:** a large, formalising market where regional players can grow by adding stores, but returns depend on inventory turns and the gold cycle.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Local brand and trust** in Mumbai (Bandra) and Ahmedabad (C. G. Road), built since 1999 (p. 156).
- **Asset-light sourcing:** buying finished pieces from many vendors keeps capital in stock rather than factories (p. 157).
- **Limits:** five stores, two cities and a promoter-family business; there are no structural barriers against national chains.

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- Growth from Rs 24.91 cr to Rs 351.28 cr revenue in two years (p. 227)
- EBITDA margin 13.36% and ROCE 26.84% (9M FY26) (p. 101)
- BIS-hallmarked range of 17,452 SKUs (p. 157)
- Low debt after the issue (debt/equity 0.50x before it) (p. 101)

**Weaknesses**
- Five showrooms; Ahmedabad is 62.40% of revenue (p. 23)
- Inventory days 205; operating cash flow negative FY23-25 (pp. 101, 228)
- Short track record as a combined entity (merger in Mar-2025) (p. 189)
- Promoter family holds about 100% before the issue; small independent board (pp. 65-66)

**Opportunities**
- New Maharashtra showroom and expanded Ahmedabad showroom (FY27) (p. 91)
- Shift to organised, hallmarked jewellery (p. 111 onwards)
- Diamond and studded jewellery, which carry higher margins (p. 157)

**Threats**
- Gold price falls reducing inventory value and margins (p. 143 onwards)
- National chains expanding into Mumbai and Ahmedabad (p. 100)
- Seasonality of wedding and festival demand (p. 22)

- **Ownership:** promoter family about 100% before the issue (Ramchand Raimalani 96.25%, Neelam Raimalani 3.75%) (p. 78). After the fresh issue and OFS the family would hold about 69.8% (our computation).
- **Cost to promoters:** the weighted average cost of shares acquired in the last 18 months is Rs 35.20; shares issued for the business transfer were valued at Rs 176 before the bonus issues (pp. 75, 84).
- **Pay:** Rs 1.65 cr a year to the three promoter directors at the FY26 rate; none was paid before FY25 (pp. 64, 195).
- **Related parties:** FY25 purchases of Rs 21.52 cr from the promoter's proprietorship before it was merged in; promoter loans (Rs 1.52 cr at Dec-25, down from Rs 12.35 cr at Mar-24); rent to the promoter for showrooms (Rs 1.23 cr in 9M FY26); purchases from promoter-group company Spicy Sangria Hotels (p. 64).
- **Litigation:** no criminal or regulatory cases. Income-tax matters for AY 2012-13 (Rs 1.64 cr) and others, and contingent income-tax demands of Rs 4.995 cr (pp. 63, 319).
- **Read:** a founder-run family business that has just formalised its structure: it merged the proprietorship, converted to a public company in Jan-2026 and added independent directors. Governance is young; the merger removed the main related-party overlap. None of this is a material issue under the Fund's rules.

## 9. Conclusion

**Subscribe at Rs 162 (upper band), close to the line.** (Updated 29-Oct-2026 from Neutral, on FY26 results in the RHP.)

**The investment case.** A fast-growing regional jeweller with five showrooms in Mumbai and Ahmedabad, FY26 revenue of Rs 538.73 cr (+53%), a 15.4% EBITDA margin and PAT of Rs 51.79 cr. The issue is mostly fresh money (Rs 220 cr) to stock a new Maharashtra showroom, expand in Ahmedabad and repay some debt, which leaves the balance sheet strong. At 17.6x FY26 earnings after the issue (13.4x on the company's own EPS) the price is below listed jewellery retailers (median 21.9x), and the reported P/E is a fair measure.

**What holds the value down.** Inventory-heavy growth means little free cash flow (FY26 CFO Rs 15.40 cr against PAT Rs 51.79 cr), the business has a short history as a combined entity, two-thirds of sales come from Ahmedabad, and part of the recent margin expansion likely reflects rising gold prices.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 127 / **Rs 149** / Rs 175 (Bear / Neutral / Bull). **Call rule:** the Neutral value is 8.2% below the upper band (within 10%), so the call is **Subscribe**; at the Rs 153 floor it is 2.7% below. No material issue was found. The margin of safety is thin: the call rests on FY26 figures reproduced by trackers and on an estimate of FY26 working capital, and it would move to Neutral if working capital proves materially higher.
