## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** manufactures **geophones, geophone strings and harnesses, seismic cables, geophysical connectors** and accessories at Manesar, Gurugram. The products are used in oil and gas exploration, mineral exploration, defence research and earthquake monitoring.
- **History:** set up in 1986 as Geosource India Limited, in technical and financial collaboration with Geosource Inc., USA; renamed HGS (India) Limited in 1992.
- **Mix and markets:** geophone strings and harnesses were 66.29% of FY26 revenue (51.15% in FY25). Exports were 84.22% of FY26 revenue across 47 countries (59.15% in FY25). Imports were 73.65% of FY26 purchases.
- **Customers:** the top 10 customers were 72.58% of FY26 revenue and 90.05% in the stub period to April 2026, with no long-term contracts.
- **Promoters:** Anil Dass, Shashi Singh, Cecilia Margareta Singh Ramel and Sowar Private Limited.

### Stage of the business

**Classification: mature niche.**

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|

## 2. Industry outlook

- **Demand:** seismic sensors and cables are consumables for land and transition-zone seismic surveys. Demand follows oil and gas exploration budgets, and increasingly mineral (critical minerals), geothermal and monitoring work.
- **Structure:** a small global niche led by a few large equipment makers. Specialist suppliers like HGS compete on cost, customisation and lead times, largely in export markets.
- **Read for an investor:** a profitable niche with a cyclical, lumpy order flow. Revenue can swing with a few large survey contracts, which matches the customer concentration seen here.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Technical know-how and track record** since 1986 in a specialised product with few qualified suppliers.
- **Export relationships** across 47 countries.
- **Limits:** concentrated customers with no long-term contracts, dependence on imported components, and much larger global competitors.

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- EBITDA margin 37.4% and PAT margin 25.8% in FY26
- Nil borrowings
- Exports to 47 countries (84% of revenue)
- Forty-year track record

**Weaknesses**
- Revenue fell 8% in FY26
- Top 10 customers 73% of revenue; no long-term contracts
- Imports 74% of purchases
- Small scale (Rs 45 cr revenue)

**Opportunities**
- Mineral, geothermal and monitoring demand alongside oil and gas
- New machinery and in-house gold plating to cut cost and improve quality
- Acquisitions funded from the issue (not yet identified)

**Threats**
- Oil and gas exploration downturns
- Large global seismic-equipment makers
- Currency and import-cost swings

## 4. Management analysis and governance

## 7. Valuation: DCF and peer comparison (P2P)

### Peer comparison (P2P)

**No listed Indian peer is reported from the offer document.** HGS is a niche seismic-equipment maker, and the tracker "peer" tables list unrelated recent SME IPOs. The relative method is therefore not used, and fair value is the DCF alone. For context only: at 11.2x FY26 earnings and about 1.1x book, the price is below the roughly 15.5x average of recent SME manufacturing IPOs (IPO Platform). That is not a like-for-like comparison.

### DCF and fair value

**Inputs (FY26; Rs crore):** revenue 45.16; EBITDA 16.89; PAT 11.64; net worth 89.27; net debt **-5.83** (nil borrowings, cash and bank Rs 5.83 cr). **Estimates, not reported:**
- D&A and capex Rs 4.5 cr each.
- Working capital about Rs 16 cr (35% of revenue).

The fresh issue is credited net of 6% assumed expenses (Rs 26.24 cr). IPO-funded capex is Rs 19.36 cr. The OFS does not change the share count.

**Cost of capital (house CAPM):** risk-free 6.5%, beta 1.10, ERP 7.0%, size premium 2.0%; cost of equity 16.2%; pre-tax cost of debt 9.5%; debt weight 20%. **WACC 14.38%**; terminal growth 4.50%; terminal ROIC 18%. Terminal value is 81% of enterprise value.

| Rs per share | Bear | Neutral | Bull |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DCF (= fair value; no peers) | 45.22 | 81.07 | 116.03 |
| Against Rs 120 (upper band) | -62.3% | -32.4% | -3.3% |
| Against Rs 114 (floor) | -60.3% | -28.9% | +1.8% |

- **Scenario drivers:**
  - Growth: Bear 0-4%, Neutral 7-10%, Bull 8-15% a year.
  - EBITDA margin: 25% / 32% / 37%.
  - Working capital: 40% / 35% / 30% of revenue.
  - Terminal ROIC: 14% / 18% / 24%.
- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF):**
  - WACC 12.4-16.4% gives Rs 97-70.
  - Margin 30-34% gives Rs 75-88.
  - Working capital 29-41% moves value by only about Rs 3.
  - The discount rate and margin are the levers.
- **Objects floor:** Rs 89 a share (Rs 19.36 cr of specific objects over 23.26 lakh fresh shares, after expenses). This is above the Neutral DCF, a sign that the price leans on assets and current earnings rather than on growth.
- **Reverse DCF at Rs 120:** the price asks for 9.6 times current free cash flow in perpetuity. **In plain words: the price is about right if the FY26 margin (37%) and cash flow are sustained. That is roughly our Bull case, which reaches Rs 116.** The Neutral case assumes the margin settles nearer the FY25 level.
- **What the model does not credit:** the large net worth (Rs 89 cr against Rs 45 cr revenue) and Rs 3.75 cr of other income suggest there may be treasury assets beyond the Rs 5.83 cr of reported cash. None is reported by the trackers, so none is credited. If such assets exist, each Rs 10 cr adds about Rs 9 a share.

### What would change our view

- **Towards Neutral:** allotment at Rs 115 or below (the call rule reads Neutral there); or RHP evidence that FY26 margins are structural.
- **Towards Subscribe:** RHP showing treasury investments of about Rs 40 cr or more behind the other income; that alone would lift the Neutral value above Rs 115.
- **Further towards Avoid:** a weak FY27 order book or a qualified audit report.

### Risks

- Exploration-cycle downturns.
- Customer concentration and the absence of long-term contracts.
- Import dependence and currency swings.
- Execution of the machinery replacement.
- SME liquidity.

## 9. Conclusion

**Avoid at Rs 120 (upper band), provisional and close to the line.**

**The investment case.** A debt-free, 40-year-old niche maker of seismic sensors and cables. It exports 84% of its sales, earns a 37% EBITDA margin and is priced at 11.2x FY26 earnings and about 1.1x book. The reported P/E is a fair measure on the data available.

**What holds the value down.**
- Revenue fell 8% in FY26.
- Ten customers give 73% of sales, with no long-term contracts.
- About a quarter of pre-tax profit is other income whose source is not yet known.
- Cash flows from the operating business support about Rs 81 a share in our Neutral case.
- With no listed peer, there is no market cross-check.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 45 / **Rs 81** / Rs 116 (Bear / Neutral / Bull).

**Call rule.** The Neutral value is 32.4% below the upper band, and the Bull value is also below it, so the call is **Avoid**. At the Rs 114 floor the Neutral value is 28.9% below, which would read Neutral. No material governance or accounting issue was found.

The call is sensitive to two items the RHP will settle: the source of other income (and any treasury behind it), and whether FY26 margins are structural. Either could move the call to Neutral or better. This report is **provisional**, built without the RHP, and will be refreshed when the RHP is read.
