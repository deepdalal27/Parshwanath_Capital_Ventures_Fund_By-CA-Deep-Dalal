## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** manages alternative investment funds for institutions and wealthy investors, in two verticals (DRHP pp. 222-224):
  - **Real Assets** (49.8% of fee-paying AUM at Sep-25): infrastructure yield funds (roads, transmission, renewables, including an InvIT), commercial real estate yield, perpetual capital and, from FY26, energy transition.
  - **Private Credit** (48.0%): performing credit, special situations, real-estate credit and investment-grade credit; the oldest strategy dates from FY10.
  - Others (2.2%): co-investment PMS.
- **Scale:** AUM Rs 65,504 cr and fee-paying AUM (FPAUM) Rs 38,552 cr at Sep-25 (DRHP p. 222); FPAUM Rs 44,710 cr at Mar-26 and **Rs 48,623 cr at Jun-26** (Add. p. 7). Most large funds are in their third or fourth series.
- **Clients:** 5,398 client relationships; 51.67% of AUM from institutions (pension funds, insurers, banks), 48.33% from UHNIs, HNIs and family offices; 73% of AUM from repeat clients; offices in Mumbai, Delhi, GIFT City and Singapore (DRHP pp. 222-223).
- **How it earns** (FY26, Add. p. 7):
  - management and advisory fees Rs 579.07 cr, charged on committed or invested capital or NAV;
  - carry, which the company calls "Variable Additional Return", Rs 290.47 cr, earned once a fund clears its hurdle rate;
  - GP income Rs 79.95 cr, the return on its own investments alongside its funds.
- **Operating platform:** subsidiary Sekura runs and improves the infrastructure assets the funds own (57 people); the investment team has 80 professionals (DRHP p. 224).

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures come from the CARE report commissioned and paid for by the issuer (DRHP p. 222); we have not checked them independently.

- **What alternatives are:** private credit, private equity, real estate, infrastructure and hedge strategies, used by institutions and wealthy investors for yield and diversification (DRHP p. 17).
- **Why India:** a bank-led credit market leaves room for private credit; large infrastructure build-out and asset recycling (InvITs, operating-asset sales) need long-term yield capital; domestic family offices and global pension funds are raising allocations (DRHP pp. 158 onwards).
- **Competition:** domestic alternatives houses (360 ONE, Kotak Alternate, ICICI Pru AMC's AIF arm, Nuvama), global managers (Brookfield, KKR, Blackstone) and wealth platforms that place AIFs. Gaja Alternative Asset Management is the only listed pure-play peer named, and it has no data in the offer document (Add. p. 9).
- **Read for an investor:** a structural growth industry, but each fund's economics depend on raising money, deploying it and exiting well; carry arrives in lumps.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Track record and repeat series:** funds that have returned capital through cycles can raise larger follow-on funds; 73% of AUM comes from repeat clients (DRHP p. 222).
- **Operating capability in infrastructure:** Sekura's asset-management team gives an edge in buying, running and recycling operating roads and power assets (DRHP pp. 222, 224).
- **Global client coverage:** institutional LPs from North America, Europe, the Middle East and Asia, plus GIFT City and Singapore vehicles (DRHP p. 223).
- **Locked-in fees:** closed-ended funds pay fees for 5-10 years once raised, so revenue is visible.
- **Limits:** key people and the Edelweiss brand matter; the MD & CEO resigned in Sep-2025 (DRHP p. 272).

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- AUM Rs 65,504 cr (Sep-25); FPAUM Rs 48,623 cr (Jun-26) (DRHP p. 222; Add. p. 7)
- Funds in third and fourth series; 73% of AUM from repeat clients (DRHP p. 222)
- ROE 25.97% in FY26 with negligible capex (Add. p. 7)
- Management fees up 20-21% a year (Add. p. 7)
- In-house infrastructure operating platform (DRHP p. 224)

**Weaknesses**
- Carry is a third of revenue and lumpy; FY23 PAT was higher than FY25 (DRHP p. 19)
- Capitalised distribution costs lift reported profit while fund-raising grows (Add. p. 119)
- Cost-to-income 63.8% in FY26, 68.5% in Q1 FY27, above traditional AMC peers (Add. p. 7)
- Leadership change: MD & CEO resigned Sep-2025; now two CEOs (DRHP p. 272)
- Group linkages: brand, past purchases of rights from group companies, Rs 205.70 cr dividend to the group (DRHP pp. 22-24; Add. p. 108)

**Opportunities**
- Indian alternatives market growth (issuer-paid CARE report, DRHP p. 158)
- Energy-transition, InvIT and commercial real estate yield funds (DRHP p. 223)
- Offshore and GIFT City fund-raising (DRHP p. 223)
- Realisation of accrued carry in maturing funds (Add. p. 108)

**Threats**
- Fund underperformance cutting carry and fund-raising (DRHP p. 20)
- Subjective valuation of unlisted fund assets (DRHP p. 20)
- AIF tax or regulatory changes (DRHP p. 20)
- Supply overhang: the promoter group keeps about 84% after the OFS

### Is the reported P/E right?

**Verdict: the reported P/E flatters current earnings because distribution costs are capitalised. Adjusted P/E 73.6x against 40.8x reported (PAT -44.6%).** On the FY24-26 average the P/E is 48.4x reported and 86.4x adjusted. The policy is permitted and the cost is real, so read this as a timing flag, not a cash leak.

- **Capitalised distribution costs (in the adjusted P/E, Medium confidence):** upfront and trail fees paid to distributors for raising each fund are amortised over the fund's tenure, and the unamortised part is carried as prepaid expenses (Add. p. 119). The balance rose from Rs 255.01 cr (FY23) to Rs 384.57 / 471.52 / 638.55 cr (FY24-26) and Rs 667.94 cr at Jun-26, which is 58% of net worth (Add. pp. 133, 138; DRHP pp. 329, 333). Expensing these costs when paid would have cut FY26 pre-tax profit by Rs 167.03 cr. Ind AS allows incremental costs of winning a contract to be capitalised, and amortising them matches the multi-year fee stream, but while fund-raising grows, reported profit runs ahead of a pay-as-you-go basis.
- **Labour-code charge (in the adjusted P/E, High):** a one-off Rs 9.88 cr exceptional item in FY26, added back.
- **Prior-year tax credits (in the adjusted P/E, Medium):** Rs 13.70 cr in FY24 and small amounts since.
- **For information:**
  - Carry is booked at fair value before it is paid: Rs 290.47 cr in FY26, while realised carry was Rs 430.69 cr, so what is booked has been turning into cash. Excluding carry would give a P/E of about 226x on fees and investment income alone.
  - Excluding GP income would give 52.7x.
- **Checked, no adjustment:**
  - ESOP cost is expensed.
  - No other capitalised costs; intangibles are acquired management rights being amortised.
  - Credit-loss provisions are small.
  - Deferred tax turns into current tax as carry is realised (Q1 FY27 current tax Rs 56.41 cr).
  - (Add. pp. 106-108)

## 9. Conclusion

**Avoid at Rs 1,665 (upper band), narrowly.**

**The investment case.** EAAA is one of India's larger alternatives managers: fee-paying AUM of Rs 48,623 cr growing about 27% a year, funds in their third and fourth series, a strong infrastructure-yield and private-credit franchise, an institutional client base and ROE in the high twenties. Management fees are locked in for years, and older funds are now paying meaningful carry in cash.

**What holds the value down.** At Rs 1,665 the stock is priced like a listed mutual-fund house (40.8x earnings, peer median 38.7x), but a third of revenue is lumpy carry, costs are high, and reported profit benefits from capitalising distribution costs: on a cash-expense basis the P/E is 73.6x. The whole issue is an Edelweiss sell-down; the company gets no capital.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 1,033 / **Rs 1,130** / Rs 1,214 (Bear / Neutral / Bull). **Call rule:** at the upper band the Neutral value is 32.1% lower and the Bull value 27.1% lower, so the rule reads **Avoid**, on valuation alone and only just past the 30% line. At the Rs 1,585 floor (-28.7%) it would read Neutral. No material issue was found. On peer multiples alone the issue is fairly priced; the gap is our cash-flow view.
