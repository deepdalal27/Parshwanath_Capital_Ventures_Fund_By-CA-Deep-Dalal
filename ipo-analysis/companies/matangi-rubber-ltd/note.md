## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a B2B maker of tyre components and, since Dec-2025, two- and three-wheeler tyres. It sells mainly to tyre companies, and JK Tyre & Industries is by far the largest customer (pp. 194, 202-203). Products:
  - **Tyre flaps**, the rubber strip between tube and rim in truck and bus tyres (contract-made with the customer's logo);
  - **butyl inner tubes** (job work for JK Tyre);
  - **2/3-wheeler tyres** (job work and some own-brand sales);
  - rubber compound.
- **Three ways of earning (p. 202):**
  - **Contract manufacturing:** the company buys the raw material, makes the flaps and sells them. This is 73.4% of FY26 revenue.
  - **Job work:** the customer supplies the rubber and the company earns a conversion charge, Rs 27.54 cr in FY26 (21.2%).
  - **Own manufacture and sale:** a small share (2.5%), plus services to JK Tyre and scrap.
- **Revenue mix FY26 (Rs cr, p. 203):**

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Services, scrap, trading | 1.05 | 3.11 | 3.73 | 1.02 |
| **Revenue from operations** | **90.11** | **101.32** | **129.76** | **47.74** |

- **Plants and capacity (pp. 129, 134):**
  - Tyre flaps: 43.2 lakh units a year at Selaqui (Dehradun) and Gummidipoondi (Chennai), 78.8% used in FY26.
  - Tubes: 27.6 lakh units at Bhind (Gwalior), plus a new Dehradun plant from 16-Dec-2025; 83.5% used.
  - 2/3-wheeler tyres: 7.1 lakh units at Bhind, started 27-Dec-2025; 36.9% used in FY26 and 39.4% in Q1 FY27.
- **Prices and volumes:** flap realisation rose from Rs 292 a unit (FY24) to Rs 361 (FY25) and Rs 366 (FY26), while volumes went 27.51 -> 24.17 -> 26.00 lakh units (pp. 378, 380).
- **Acquisition:** the company bought 97.74% of MG Industries Ltd, the Bhind tube maker, in Oct-Dec 2024. It paid about Rs 20.91 cr in cash and swapped 1,51,578 shares at Rs 475 (pp. 71, 93, 375).

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures come from an Infomerics Analytics and Research report commissioned and paid for by the company (p. 19). We have not checked them independently.

- **Size and growth:**
  - The Indian tyre market is put at USD 12.17 bn in 2025, growing at about 7.3% a year to USD 24.55 bn by 2035 (p. 166).
  - The global rubber market is about USD 47 bn, growing at about 5.4% a year (p. 156).
- **Where Matangi sits:** a supplier of components (flaps, tubes) and outsourced tyre capacity to tyre makers. Flaps and tubes are used in tube-type tyres, which still dominate trucks and buses in India.
- **Structure:**
  - Tyre makers (Ceat, TVS Srichakra and others) make many components in-house and outsource the rest.
  - Outsourced suppliers compete on cost, quality and nearness to the tyre makers' plants.
- **Swings:**
  - Natural and synthetic rubber prices, which follow crude oil and the rubber crop, drive input costs (p. 378).
  - Truck and bus demand drives replacement volumes.
- **Long-term risk:** a gradual move to tubeless radial tyres in trucks and buses would shrink demand for flaps and tubes.
- **Read for an investor:** steady end-demand, but the company's growth depends on how much work tyre makers choose to outsource, and to whom.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Customer relationships and approvals:** the company is an approved, long-standing supplier to JK Tyre and the only supplier with a long-term agreement disclosed (p. 23). Approvals take time to win, which gives some switching cost.
- **Plants close to customers:** in Uttarakhand, Tamil Nadu and Madhya Pradesh (p. 129).
- **Limits:**
  - No brand or technology edge is shown.
  - Job work earns only a conversion fee.
  - One customer holds the bargaining power.
- **Overall:** the moat is narrow. It is a relationship moat with one customer.

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- Five plants; flap capacity 43.2 lakh units a year (pp. 129, 134)
- Agreement and long relationship with JK Tyre (p. 23)
- Operating history since 1985 (p. 194)
- New tube and 2/3-wheeler plants already built (p. 134)

**Weaknesses**
- JK Tyre 88.7% of Q1 FY27 revenue (p. 22)
- Working capital about 47% of revenue (pp. 68-69)
- Net debt Rs 104 cr at Mar-26 (pp. 68-69)
- No audit trail in the FY26 accounting software (p. 352)

**Opportunities**
- Filling the 2/3-wheeler tyre plant (37% used) (p. 134)
- Recycling and solid-tyre plants from the issue (p. 114)
- Own-brand tyres and tubes (p. 203)
- Indian tyre market growing about 7% a year (p. 166)

**Threats**
- A cut in JK Tyre volumes
- Rubber price swings (p. 378)
- Tyre makers taking work back in-house
- Shift to tubeless tyres

### Is the reported P/E right?

**Verdict: the reported FY26 P/E of 22.0x (on total PAT and post-offer shares) understates the multiple. On recurring earnings it is 26.5x.** The adjustments take out about 17% of PAT:
- **Depreciation method change, Rs 3.30 cr pre-tax (pp. 313, 379).**
  - In FY26 the company switched from WDV to straight-line depreciation, and so did MG Industries in FY25. Both set the excess past WDV charge against the new one, so some assets bore no depreciation.
  - FY26 depreciation was therefore Rs 0.45 cr on a gross block of about Rs 137 cr, Rs 3.30 cr lower than before the change. Tax depreciation for the same year was Rs 8.68 cr (p. 332).
- **Old creditor balances written back, Rs 1.68 cr pre-tax.** This was 68% of FY26 other income (p. 378).

On the FY24-26 average PAT the P/E is 31.3x reported and 37.1x adjusted.

- **Looking ahead:** only Rs 0.92 cr of excess past depreciation was left at Jun-26. On the Jun-26 gross block a normal straight-line charge is about **Rs 8 cr a year**, so FY27 profit carries a step-up in depreciation of roughly Rs 7.5 cr before tax. The DCF uses Rs 8 cr.
- **For information (not in the adjusted P/E):**
  - write-offs of advances and investments, Rs 1.39 cr (FY25) and Rs 0.26 cr (FY26), which lowered profit;
  - fair-value gains on investments, Rs 0.38 cr in FY26.
- **Checked, no adjustment:**
  - no revaluation and no capitalised interest found;
  - current tax 21-29% of PBT;
  - Q1 FY27 not annualised.

## 9. Conclusion

**Avoid at Rs 172 (upper band). It is also Avoid at the Rs 163 floor.** The band is as announced by the company and reported.

**The investment case.**
- A long-established supplier of tyre flaps and tubes that has scaled fast: revenue +44% over FY24-26, EBITDA margin 27% in FY26.
- It has new tube and 2/3-wheeler capacity already built, so it can grow without more capex beyond the issue.
- The issue repays Rs 45 cr of debt.

**What holds the value down.**
- One customer, JK Tyre, takes 89% of revenue in the latest quarter.
- FY26 profit is flattered by a depreciation-method change. On adjusted earnings the price is 27x, against a 20.5x peer median.
- Working capital near 47% of revenue has kept cash flow at half of profit.
- Return on capital is close to its cost.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 88 / **Rs 107** / Rs 128 (Bear / Neutral / Bull).

**Call rule.** At Rs 172 the Neutral value is 37.7% below the upper band and the Bull value is also below it (-25.4%), so the call is **Avoid**. No material governance or accounting issue was found under the Fund's test.
