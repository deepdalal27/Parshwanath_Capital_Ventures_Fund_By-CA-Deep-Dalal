## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a **steel processing centre**. It buys coated (colour-coated and galvanised) and hot-rolled steel coils and slits, cuts to length, shears and embosses them into slit coils and blanks for appliance and engineering manufacturers, with just-in-time delivery and inventory management for the customer (pp. 26, 207).
- **Who buys it:** contract manufacturers (ODMs, 60.56% of FY25 revenue) and brand manufacturers (OEMs, 39.42%) (p. 26). Appliances (air conditioners, refrigerators and similar) were 81.80% of FY25 product revenue (p. 30). Named customers include Amber Enterprises (15.53% of FY25 revenue), Western Refrigeration (13.50%), Haier Appliances (9.81%), PG Technoplast (7.37%), EPack Durable (5.13%) and Dixon (p. 37). 252 customers in FY25; top-10 67.44% (p. 37).
- **Supply:** an authorised service partner of **JSW Steel Coated Products** since 2021, which supplied 90.61% of FY25 coil needs (pp. 31, 208).
- **Plants:** four facilities on 42,209 sq m: Wada and Bhiwandi (Maharashtra), Ghiloth (Rajasthan) and Sri City (Andhra Pradesh, from April 2023), each placed next to appliance clusters (pp. 44, 208). Installed capacity 220,506 MTPA (half-year basis); H1 FY26 utilisation 46% slitting, 49% cut-to-length, 57% embossing (p. 123).
- **History:** the founder, Vijaykumar Gupta, began steel trading as Bombay Steels in 1983 and started processing at Bhiwandi in 2012; the company took over the proprietorship business in January 2021 and has since added the northern and southern plants (p. 207).

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures are from the CareEdge report commissioned and paid for by the issuer (p. 207); we have not checked them independently.

- **Market:** India's steel processing centre market was about USD 8.2 bn in CY25, having grown 8.6% a year since CY20, and is projected to grow 8.7% a year to USD 12.4 bn by CY30; the global market is about USD 215 bn (p. 26).
- **Why processors exist:** mills sell coils in bulk; appliance and auto makers want precise sizes delivered just in time. Processors earn a conversion margin plus a logistics and inventory-carrying role, typically on thin margins and with working capital tied up in steel.
- **Demand driver:** the shift of air-conditioner, refrigerator and component manufacturing to India under the white-goods production-linked incentive scheme, which concentrates demand around clusters where the company's plants sit (pp. 143, 178-187, 208).
- **Competition:** mill-owned service centres, other independent processors (listed examples: BMW Industries, Shiv Aum Steels), and coated-steel makers such as Manaksia Coated Metals (p. 121).
- **Read for an investor:** a volume business tied to appliance production and steel prices; returns come from utilisation, working-capital turns and customer stickiness rather than pricing power.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Service-partner status with JSW Steel Coated Products:** assured access to coated coils, the key input (p. 208).
- **Location next to customers:** plants beside the AC and refrigerator clusters of Amber, Western Refrigeration, PG Technoplast and EPack cut freight and lead time, and are hard to replicate quickly (p. 208).
- **Approved-vendor relationships** with large ODMs/OEMs; customer count rose from 188 to 252 in two years (p. 37).
- **Limits:** processing is not proprietary, contracts are order-by-order with no long-term commitments (p. 31), and the same mill relationship that secures supply also concentrates it.

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- Revenue up from Rs 590.88 cr to Rs 1,055.71 cr in two years (p. 279)
- Plants located inside appliance manufacturing clusters in the West, North and South (p. 208)
- Authorised service partner of JSW Steel Coated Products (p. 208)
- ROCE about 18.8% on thin margins (p. 123)
- Margin improving: adjusted EBITDA 7.84% in H1 FY26 (p. 122)

**Weaknesses**
- Thin margins: PAT margin 2.72% in FY25 (p. 122)
- Leverage: debt/equity 3.33x (FY25), interest cover 2.4x on EBITDA (pp. 122-123, 279)
- 90.61% of steel coils from one supplier (p. 31)
- Appliances 81.80% of product revenue; top-10 customers 67.44% (pp. 30, 37)
- 25 delayed statutory filings and an allotment-filing error under adjudication (pp. 44-46)

**Opportunities**
- Spare capacity: utilisation 46-66% in H1 FY26 (p. 123)
- Appliance and component manufacturing growth in India (part 2)
- Lower interest cost after Rs 125 cr of debt repayment (p. 108)
- Engineering-sector blanks (auto, cleanrooms, electricals) to diversify (p. 207)

**Threats**
- Steel price swings affecting inventory value and customer demand (p. 30)
- Slowdown in AC / refrigerator production (p. 30)
- Change in the JSW Steel Coated Products relationship (p. 31)
- Customers sourcing directly from mill service centres (part 2)

## 4. Management analysis and governance

- **Leadership:** Vijaykumar Gupta, Chairman and Whole-time Director, in steel since 1983; Nitin Gupta (his son), Managing Director, 20+ years in steel, who led the expansion into appliance OEM/ODM supply; Bhawna Gupta, Whole-time Director (pp. 248-250). CEO Shashi Rawat; CFO Ashish Sonthalia (since Sept-2025, after the previous CFO left in Aug-2025); Company Secretary Sonam Gupta (p. 32).
- **Board:** six directors: three promoter executives and three independent directors (two chartered accountants, one company secretary), all appointed in March 2025 ahead of the IPO (pp. 248-250).
- **Ownership:** the promoter family holds 97.78% before the issue; Nitin Gupta 78.22% (p. 27). The promoters' average cost is Rs 1.25 to Rs 12.50 a share (p. 35). The Oct-2025 placement at Rs 88 brought in Moneyplant SME Fund and individual investors (pp. 28, 94).
- **Pay:** three promoter directors drew Rs 0.66 cr in FY25; the CEO Rs 0.57 cr (p. 32). Modest for a Rs 1,000 cr revenue business.
- **Related parties:** the promoter family lends the company Rs 25.75 cr (Sep-2025) at interest (Rs 2.52 cr interest in FY25), and two premises are rented from promoters (pp. 32-33, 254). Related-party transactions were 3.14% of FY25 revenue (p. 33). Typical of a promoter-funded company; GCP proceeds cannot be used to repay promoter loans (p. 27).
- **Compliance record:** 25 statutory forms were filed late with the RoC over five years (with additional fees), and the 2021 allotment of 24.5 lakh shares to the founder for the Bombay Steels business transfer was filed under the wrong head without Form MGT-14; adjudication applications are pending (pp. 44-46). Procedural and disclosed; no penalty yet.
- **Litigation:** none by or against the company; one tax matter against a director, amount unascertained (p. 30).
- **Audit:** Shah Shah & Shah (peer-reviewed), no qualifications (pp. 29, 88).
- **Read:** a first-generation family business professionalising for listing: an experienced promoter team, modest pay, and a board that is new. The filing lapses point to a back office that has lagged the company's growth; none is a material issue under the Fund's rules, but board oversight after listing is a point to watch.

## 9. Conclusion

**Screen only (DRHP stage; no price band).**

**The investment case.** Bombay Coated is a four-plant steel processor embedded in India's air-conditioner and refrigerator supply chain, with JSW Steel Coated Products as its coil partner and Amber, Western Refrigeration, Haier, PG Technoplast and Dixon as customers. Revenue almost doubled in two years, margins are inching up, and plants run at only about half their capacity, so volume can grow without heavy capex. The issue is all fresh money and mostly repays debt, which should lift reported profit by about 30% through lower interest. The reported P/E is a fair measure of its earnings.

**What holds the value down.** Thin margins (2.7% PAT), high leverage before the issue (3.3x debt/equity), dependence on one supplier and one end-market, and a softer H1 FY26. The filing lapses and a newly constituted board are governance points to follow, not material issues.

**Valuation.** At the Oct-2025 placement price of Rs 88, fair value is Rs 111 / **Rs 118** / Rs 129 (Bear / Neutral / Bull). Because the fresh issue is a fixed Rs 191 cr, a higher issue price means fewer new shares and a higher value per share. Solving for that, **the call rule would read Subscribe up to about Rs 154** (28x FY25 EPS on post-issue shares), **Neutral from about Rs 154 to Rs 212** (up to 37x), **and Avoid above about Rs 212**. The relative value leans on a wide peer set (13-60x); against BMW Industries alone (13.3x) the case would be more modest. We will re-run the screen on the RHP's FY26 numbers and the actual band.
