## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a **steel processing centre**. It buys coated (colour-coated and galvanised) and hot-rolled steel coils and slits, cuts to length, shears and embosses them into slit coils and blanks for appliance and engineering manufacturers, with just-in-time delivery and inventory management for the customer (p. 239).
- **Who buys it:** OEMs and ODMs. Home and commercial appliances (air conditioners, refrigerators and similar) were 86.54% of FY26 product revenue and 89.93% in Q1 FY27 (p. 37). FY26 customers include Amber Enterprises (20.40% of revenue), Western Refrigeration (15.98%), Haier Appliances (8.70%), PG Technoplast (5.84%), Dixon (3.46%) and EPack Durable (1.76%); top-10 71.33% (p. 35).
- **Supply:** an authorised service partner of **JSW Steel Coated Products** since 2021, which supplied 86.65% of FY26 coil purchases and 85.13% in Q1 FY27 (pp. 30-31). The agreement (1-Apr-2025) carries minimum purchase commitments and inventory norms, restricts buying coated products from other mills, grants no exclusivity, and can be ended by JSW on three months' notice (pp. 31-32).
- **Plants:** four facilities on 42,209 sq m at Wada and Bhiwandi (Maharashtra), Ghiloth (Rajasthan) and Sri City (Andhra Pradesh), each placed near appliance clusters (p. 240). FY26 installed capacity 2,38,020 MT slitting and 1,84,324 MT cut-to-length, used 60.6% and 58.7%; Q1 FY27 63.8% and 59.7% (p. 132). 160 permanent employees (p. 240).
- **History:** the founder, Vijaykumar Gupta, began steel trading as Bombay Steels in 1983 and started processing at Bhiwandi in 2012; the company took over the proprietorship business in January 2021 and has since added the northern and southern plants (p. 239).

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures are from the CareEdge report commissioned and paid for by the issuer (p. 239); we have not checked them independently.

- **Market:** India's steel processing centre market was about USD 8.2 bn in CY25, up from USD 5.4 bn in CY20, and is projected at USD 12.4 bn by CY30; the global market is USD 214.9 bn, growing 6.2% a year (pp. 185, 190).
- **Why processors exist:** mills sell coils in bulk; appliance and auto makers want precise sizes delivered just in time. Processors earn a conversion margin plus a logistics and inventory-carrying role, on thin margins and with working capital tied up in steel. The RHP cites capital needs, inventory, customer qualification and multi-location networks as entry barriers (p. 185).
- **Demand driver:** the shift of air-conditioner, refrigerator and component manufacturing to India under the white-goods production-linked incentive scheme, which concentrates demand around the clusters where the company's plants sit (pp. 143 onwards, 240).
- **Competition:** mill-owned service centres, other JSW service partners (JSW may appoint more in the company's regions, p. 31), independent processors (BMW Industries, Shiv Aum Steels) and coated-steel makers such as Manaksia Coated Metals (p. 130).
- **Read for an investor:** a volume business tied to appliance production and steel prices; returns come from utilisation, working-capital turns and customer stickiness rather than pricing power.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Service-partner status with JSW Steel Coated Products:** assured access to coated coils, the key input (p. 240).
- **Location next to customers:** plants beside the AC and refrigerator clusters of Amber, Western Refrigeration, PG Technoplast and EPack cut freight and lead time, and are hard to replicate quickly (p. 240).
- **Approved-vendor relationships** with large ODMs and OEMs, including Haier, Dixon and IFB (p. 240).
- **Limits:** processing is not proprietary; the JSW arrangement is non-exclusive and terminable, so the relationship that secures supply also concentrates it (p. 31).

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- Revenue up from Rs 755.26 cr to Rs 1,266.82 cr in two years (p. 85)
- Plants located inside appliance manufacturing clusters in the West, North and South (p. 240)
- Authorised service partner of JSW Steel Coated Products since 2021 (p. 240)
- RoNW 25.12% and ROCE 16.40% in FY26 on thin margins (pp. 130, 132)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin 8.10% in Q1 FY27 (p. 131)

**Weaknesses**
- Thin margins: PAT margin 2.41% in FY26 (p. 131)
- Leverage: debt/equity 2.78x (FY26); interest cover 2.4x on EBITDA (pp. 85, 132)
- 86.65% of steel coils from one supplier (p. 30)
- Appliances 86.54% of product revenue; top-10 customers 71.33% (pp. 35, 37)
- 25 delayed statutory filings and an allotment-filing error under adjudication (pp. 32-33)

**Opportunities**
- Spare capacity: utilisation 59-64% in slitting and cut-to-length (p. 132)
- India steel processing market projected to grow from USD 8.2 bn to USD 12.4 bn by CY30 (p. 190)
- Lower interest cost after Rs 125 cr of debt repayment (p. 117)
- Engineering-sector blanks (auto, cleanrooms, electricals) to diversify (p. 239)

**Threats**
- Steel price swings affecting inventory value and customer demand (p. 37)
- Slowdown or a weak season in AC and refrigerator production (p. 37)
- JSW appointing competing service partners or ending the arrangement (p. 31)
- Customers sourcing directly from mill service centres (part 2)

## 4. Management analysis and governance

- **Leadership:** Vijaykumar Gupta, Chairman and Whole-time Director, 40+ years in steel; Nitin Gupta (his son), Managing Director, about 20 years in steel, who runs day-to-day operations and led the expansion into appliance OEM/ODM supply; Bhawna Gupta, Whole-time Director (pp. 286-288). CEO Shashi Rawat (with the company since 2022); COO Anish Arora (since Apr-2024, ex-Tata Steel); CFO Ashish Sonthalia (since Sep-2025; the previous CFO was re-designated Vice President); Company Secretary Sonam Gupta (pp. 303-305).
- **Board:** six directors: three promoter executives and three independent directors (Neelam Tater, Mihen Halani, Hemant Ostwal), all independents appointed on 21-Mar-2025 ahead of the IPO (pp. 286-288).
- **Ownership:** the promoters hold 97.78% before the issue (Nitin Gupta 78.22%) and about 63% after it; their average cost is Rs 1.25 to Rs 12.50 a share (pp. 105, 112). The Oct-2025 placement of 9,00,091 shares at Rs 88 brought in Moneyplant SME Fund and individuals (pp. 100, 103). Weighted average cost of all shares acquired in the last three years: Rs 2.59 (p. 113).
- **Pay:** three promoter directors drew Rs 0.66 cr in FY26; the CEO Rs 0.54 cr and the COO Rs 0.81 cr (pp. 89-92, 303). Modest for a Rs 1,267 cr revenue business.
- **Related parties:** the promoter family lends the company Rs 20.55 cr (Jun-26) at interest (Rs 3.08 cr in FY26) and rents it premises (Rs 0.52 cr in FY26) (pp. 89-92). GCP proceeds cannot be used to repay promoter or director loans (p. 118). Typical of a promoter-funded company.
- **Compliance record:** 25 statutory forms were filed late with the RoC over five years (with additional fees), and the 2021 allotment of 24.5 lakh shares to the founder for the Bombay Steels business transfer was filed under the wrong head without Form MGT-14. The adjudication application (6-Jan-2026) and a May-2026 application to cancel the original form are pending before the RoC (pp. 32-33). Procedural and disclosed; no penalty yet.
- **Audit:** Shah Shah & Shah (peer-reviewed), no qualifications (p. 82).
- **Read:** a first-generation family business professionalising for listing: an experienced promoter team, modest pay, a strengthened second line (CEO, COO, CFO), and a board that is new. The filing lapses point to a back office that has lagged the company's growth; none is a material issue under the Fund's rules, but board oversight after listing is a point to watch.

## 9. Conclusion

**Subscribe at Rs 82 (upper band).**

**The investment case.** Bombay Coated is a four-plant steel processor embedded in India's air-conditioner and refrigerator supply chain, with JSW Steel Coated Products as its coil partner and Amber, Western Refrigeration, Haier, PG Technoplast and Dixon as customers. Revenue grew 29.5% a year over FY24-26 to Rs 1,267 cr, Q1 FY27 was the strongest quarter yet (margin 8.1%, PAT Rs 15.1 cr), and plants run at about 60% of capacity, so volume can grow without heavy capex. The issue is all fresh money and mostly repays debt, which should lift reported profit by about a quarter through lower interest. The reported P/E is a fair measure of its earnings.

**What holds the value down.** Thin margins (2.4% PAT), leverage before the issue (2.8x debt/equity at FY26), dependence on one supplier and one end-market, and weak cash conversion in a growth year (CFO 67% of PAT over three years). The filing lapses and a newly constituted board are governance points to follow, not material issues.

**Valuation.** At Rs 82, fair value is Rs 103 / **Rs 110** / Rs 126 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), +34.7% in the Neutral case. **Call rule:** the Neutral value is above the price, so the call is **Subscribe**. The band is below the Rs 88 at which outside investors bought in Oct-2025, and values the company at 16.9x FY26 earnings after the issue (13.2x pro forma for lower interest) and 7.0x EV/EBITDA. The caution: the relative value leans on a wide peer set (15-99x) while the DCF alone (Rs 53) is below the price; on the closest peer alone the call would be Neutral.
