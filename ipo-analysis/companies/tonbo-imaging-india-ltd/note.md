## 1. Business of the company

- **What it sells:** electro-optic and infrared systems for defence and security: thermal weapon sights, hand-held thermal binoculars, imaging cores, targeting and fire-control systems, missile seekers and stabilised gimbal payloads (p. 246). Tactical systems (soldier-carried sights) were 58.37% of FY26 revenue (Rs 211.68 cr); platform systems (payloads for remote weapon stations, vehicles, loitering munitions, balloons) were 39.93% (Rs 144.81 cr), up from Rs 12.70 cr in FY24 (p. 248). Directed-energy systems are in development.
- **Who buys it:** in FY26 the Indian Government was 71.19% of revenue, private system integrators 24.10% and foreign governments 3.34%; in FY25 foreign governments were 57.07% (pp. 250-251). Customer names are withheld for confidentiality, as is usual in defence (p. 24).
- **How it earns:** competitive tenders with pricing fixed in advance (p. 23). Bid-win ratio 35.27% across FY24 to Q1 FY27 (97 of 275 bids) (p. 23).
- **What it owns vs outsources:** asset-light. It owns the IP and an R&D centre in Bengaluru (339 employees, 130 engineers), builds prototypes in-house and outsources volume manufacturing to EMS partners including Kaynes Technology and Avalon Technology (pp. 54, 251). Key inputs include imported infrared detectors (p. 25).
- **Selling abroad:** knocked-down kits, cores and white-labelling, so that partners' products qualify as locally made in the buyer's country (p. 232).

### Stage of the business

**Classification: early growth, tender-driven and lumpy.**

**What the stage means:** earnings and orders are the better guide to value than one year's cash flow, which swings with delivery timing. The valuation therefore uses three-year average margins and working capital in the Neutral case and blends the DCF equally with peer multiples.

## 2. Industry outlook

Source: "Assessment of Global and Indian Defence Electronics and Technology Industry" by Frost & Sullivan (India), **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 245).
- **Market size:** global military expenditure USD 3,362.2 bn in CY2025, projected at USD 4,410.9 bn by CY2030 (5.6% CAGR); Europe grows fastest at 7.5% (p. 161 onwards). India's EO/IR market is put at USD 522 m in CY2020 rising to USD 1.4 bn by CY2030, a 10.5% CAGR (p. 205).
- **Structure:** Indian EO/IR is led by Bharat Electronics, with Alpha Design, Paras Defence, HAL and L&T active in parts of the stack (pp. 237-240). Globally Tonbo competes with US, European and Israeli primes, whose products are often export-restricted under ITAR; Tonbo's are not (p. 246).
- **Regulation:** DAP 2020 favours "Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured" (IDDM) vendors; Tonbo qualifies (p. 247).
- **Cycle:** procurement is episodic; emergency buying lifts demand in bursts (p. 21). The RHP links FY26 to export delays from the West Asia conflict and orders moving into FY27 (p. 22).
- **Peer growth in FY26:** Paras Defence +30.69%, Data Patterns +30.55%, Astra Microwave +10.62%, Zen Technologies -29.37%, BEL +16.16% (p. 146).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Technology and know-how:** owned IP in thermal cores, image processing and stabilisation, 130 R&D engineers and US patents (pp. 232, 251). The issuer's own benchmark puts its Spartan-S weapon sight at 280 g against about 800-900 g for two Indian alternatives (p. 232).
- **Qualification and track record:** field-proven systems with the Indian Army, NATO buyers and others; new entrants take years to qualify (p. 231).
- **Export freedom:** ITAR-free products can be sold where US-origin systems cannot (p. 246).
- **Policy preference:** IDDM status favours indigenous OEMs (p. 247), shared with other Indian makers.
- **Durability:** the moat is technical and in a specialised niche. Tenders are re-competed, so it is sustained by continued R&D rather than by contracts.

### Is the reported P/E right?

**Verdict: the reported FY26 P/E of 78.8x is flattered by two items; on the three-year average, which smooths one-year items, the P/E is 62.6x reported and 75.6x adjusted.**

**Flags (in the adjusted P/E):**
- **Warranty provision written back (High confidence):** FY26 other expenses include a write-back of Rs 18.84 cr "of earlier years" (pp. 373, 433), equal to 27% of FY26 PBT. FY24 and FY25 carried warranty charges of Rs 20.82 cr and Rs 8.15 cr, so over three years the provision nets out and FY26 alone is flattered. Another Rs 5.98 cr was written back in Q1 FY27.
- **Development cost capitalised (Medium confidence):** additions to software and intangible work in progress rose to Rs 34.17 cr in FY26 from Rs 5.06 cr in FY25 and Rs 1.40 cr in FY24 (excluding the IP purchase). The policy capitalises R&D staff costs, consultancy and directly attributable spending (p. 342). This is permitted under Ind AS 38 when its tests are met, and the R&D expensed through profit also rose (Rs 25.86 cr in FY26, p. 36). We show it expensed, net of software amortisation of Rs 6.67 cr. The RHP does not split purchased licences from in-house development, hence Medium.

**Shown for information (not in the adjusted P/E):**
- *Raises earnings:* amortisation of the IP bought from CEAQ Singapore, Rs 18.32 cr a year, is a non-cash charge that replaced royalties; adding it back gives a P/E of 62.1x (p. 352).
- Executive pay at the contracted rates from Dec-2025 adds about Rs 2.45 cr a year (P/E 81.8x) (pp. 304-305).
- Other income, mainly interest on term deposits (Rs 7.29 cr in FY26); excluding it gives an operating P/E of 88.3x (p. 89).

**Also checked, no adjustment needed:** stock-option cost is charged to profit (Rs 12.28 cr in FY26, p. 90); no change in depreciation or amortisation policy (p. 342); no borrowing costs capitalised (pp. 342, 351); profit is taxed in cash (current tax 23.3% of PBT, p. 89); no exceptional gains (p. 89); bad debts are written off through profit (p. 373); no audit qualification (p. 440).

Holding today's working-capital level, the price would need a steady-state EBIT margin of about 195% against 20.2% actual, which is not a realistic path. The realistic path is working capital falling back towards FY24-25 levels as orders are delivered. In plain words: **the price assumes the business returns to its FY24-25 pattern of growth and cash conversion.**

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

### Growth drivers to track

- **Order conversion:** Rs 432 cr of orders in hand (p. 250). FY27 revenue near the FY25 level (Rs 469 cr) would bring the P/E on FY27 earnings well below the peer median.
- **Platform systems:** revenue up from Rs 12.70 cr (FY24) to Rs 144.81 cr (FY26); payloads for remote weapon stations, loitering munitions and vehicles are the largest part of the order book (Rs 198.35 cr) (pp. 248, 250).
- **Export recovery:** exports were 65.52% of revenue in FY25 and 5.57% in FY26; the Rs 51.44 cr deferred export order is the first marker (pp. 23, 144).
- **Defence procurement in India:** IDDM preference under DAP 2020 and a 10.5% CAGR projected for Indian EO/IR to CY2030 (issuer-paid report) (pp. 205, 247).
- **New products:** missile seekers and directed-energy systems (in development) (p. 248).
- **Margins:** EBITDA margin held at 26-30% in each full year; operating leverage as revenue recovers (p. 144).

### What would change our view

- **Towards Subscribe:** H1 FY27 revenue of Rs 180 cr or more; positive operating cash flow for H1 FY27; order book at or above Rs 400 cr at 30-Sep-2026; working capital back below 75% of revenue by March 2027. Any two of these would lift the Neutral fair value towards the price.
- **Towards Avoid:** a further deferral of the export order book, H1 FY27 revenue below Rs 120 cr, or a material governance development.
- **Price:** at a market price near Rs 515-560 the call on today's numbers would be Subscribe.

### Risks

- Lumpy, tender-based revenue and the timing of export approvals (pp. 21-23).
- A few large customers in any year (p. 24).
- Imported infrared detectors and suppliers on purchase orders (p. 25).
- Working capital funded partly by short-term bank debt (p. 445).
- Share supply when lock-ins end at six and 18 months (p. 121).

## 9. Conclusion

**Neutral at Rs 700.**

**The investment case.** Tonbo is an Indian defence-electronics OEM that owns its technology and leads its niche.
- It owns 100% of its IP since March 2024 across optics, infrared engines and video electronics, and has 130 R&D engineers (pp. 138, 251).
- Per the issuer-paid Frost & Sullivan report it was the largest manufacturer of thermal imaging systems for Indian government and defence agencies over Apr-2024 to Mar-2026, and shipped 94% of Indian thermal-imaging exports by units FY24-26 (p. 246). Its products are ITAR-free and deployed in 24 countries.
- Profitability is steady through a weak year: EBITDA margin 26.2% / 29.7% / 28.9% FY24-26, with profit taxed in cash (current tax 23.3% of PBT) (pp. 89, 144).
- Orders in hand of about Rs 432 cr (Rs 314.24 cr at 30-Jun-2026 plus Rs 118.15 cr won in July-September) cover about 1.2 years of FY26 revenue (p. 250).

**What holds the value back at this price.**
- FY26 was a down year: revenue fell 22.7% to Rs 362.65 cr as export orders slipped into FY27, and cash went into stock built for those orders, so operating cash flow was negative (pp. 23, 25, 90).
- At Rs 700 the P/E is 78.8x FY26 earnings, close to the 85.6x peer median, while peers grew revenue about 21% in the same year. The price already assumes the recovery.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 486 / **Rs 515** / Rs 556 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), -26.4% against Rs 700 in the Neutral case. Peers' multiples alone give Rs 924; a cash-flow DCF gives Rs 106, because the DCF is still carrying FY26's working capital. **Call rule:** the Neutral value is between -10% and -30% of the price, so the call is **Neutral**. No material issue was found.

**Earnings quality:** FY26 reported profit is flattered by a warranty write-back and capitalised development (adjusted FY26 P/E 247.5x against 78.8x reported); on the three-year average the adjusted P/E is 75.6x, close to peers, so the through-cycle valuation and the call are unchanged.

**What would move it to Subscribe:** H1 FY27 revenue of Rs 180 cr or more with positive operating cash flow, which would show the order book converting; or a market price nearer Rs 515-560.
