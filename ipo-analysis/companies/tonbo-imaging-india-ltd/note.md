## Verdict: Avoid at Rs 700. The evidence is mixed, and the price leans on peer multiples rather than cash.

**Strengths:** a real technology position.
- The company owns 100% of its IP since March 2024 and designs optics, infrared engines and video electronics in-house (pp. 138, 246).
- Per the issuer-paid Frost & Sullivan report it was the largest manufacturer of thermal imaging systems for Indian government and defence agencies by sales value over Apr-2024 to Mar-2026, and shipped 94% of Indian thermal-imaging exports by units FY24-26 (p. 246).
- Gross margin held at 48.6% / 56.4% / 51.1% FY24-26 and EBITDA margin at 26.2% / 29.7% / 28.9% (p. 144). Profit is taxed: current tax is 23.3% of cumulative FY24-26 PBT (p. 89).
- Visibility improved after June: order book Rs 314.24 cr at 30-Jun-2026 plus Rs 118.15 cr of orders received July-September (p. 250).

**Concerns:** growth reversed, cash did not follow profit, and a related party sits on both sides.
- Revenue fell 22.69% to Rs 362.65 cr in FY26 as export revenue dropped from Rs 307.34 cr to Rs 20.21 cr; Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 36.01 cr with a loss of Rs 17.74 cr (pp. 89, 144, 250).
- Operating cash flow was negative in FY25, FY26 and Q1 FY27; cumulative CFO/PAT FY24-26 is **-63.0%**. Trade working capital reached 109.7% of FY26 revenue (pp. 87, 90).
- CEAQ Singapore (formerly Tonbo Imaging Pte) sold the company its IP for Rs 183.19 cr in FY24, supplied Rs 78.98 cr of materials in FY26 (26.3% of total expenses), and with CEAQ India sells 91.5 lakh of the 121.7 lakh shares on offer (cover, pp. 94, 439).
- The company receives nothing from the offer (p. 134); bank debt rose to Rs 167.23 cr by 31-Aug-2026 (p. 445).

**What decides it:** at Rs 700 the P/E of 78.8x is within the peer range, but the peers grew revenue 11-31% in FY26 and Tonbo shrank. Our DCF, which prices cash after working capital, is Rs 67 a share; the relative value is Rs 832. The blend is Rs 373 (Neutral), **-46.7%** against the upper band. **The H1 FY27 results (revenue against the Rs 432 cr of orders, and a positive operating cash flow) will show whether the cash turn has begun.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** electro-optic and infrared systems for defence and security: thermal weapon sights, hand-held thermal binoculars, imaging cores, targeting and fire-control systems, missile seekers and stabilised gimbal payloads (p. 246). Tactical systems (soldier-carried sights) were 58.37% of FY26 revenue (Rs 211.68 cr), platform systems (payloads for remote weapon stations, vehicles, loitering munitions, balloons) 39.93% (Rs 144.81 cr), up from Rs 12.70 cr in FY24 (p. 248). Directed-energy systems are in development with no revenue yet.
- **Who buys it:** in FY26 the Indian Government was 71.19% of revenue, private system integrators 24.10% and foreign governments 3.34%; in FY25 foreign governments were 57.07% (pp. 250-251). One customer was the largest in FY24 and FY25 (44.11% and 57.07%) but not in FY26; customer names are withheld for confidentiality (p. 24).
- **How it earns:** competitive tenders, typically short-term and non-recurring, priced in advance with limited scope to reset for input-cost changes (p. 23). Bid-win ratio 40.91% (FY24), 35.94% (FY25), 34.35% (FY26), 14.29% in Q1 FY27 on 14 bids (p. 23).
- **What it owns vs outsources:** an asset-light model. It owns IP and an R&D centre in Bengaluru with 130 engineers (339 employees in all), prototypes in-house, and outsources volume manufacturing to EMS partners including Kaynes Technology and Avalon Technology under non-exclusive purchase orders (pp. 54, 251). Key inputs include imported infrared detectors (Lynred and SCD are among the top-10 suppliers named, p. 25).
- **Selling into export markets:** completely-knocked-down kits, cores and white-labelling by local OEMs so the final product qualifies as domestically made in the buyer's country (p. 232).

| Free cash flow: negative in FY25, FY26 and Q1 FY27 | Growth and the export reversal have been funded with working capital and bank debt |

| Market share: largest Indian supplier of thermal imaging to government (issuer-paid report) | Position strong in a small niche |

**What the stage means:** for an early-growth company with negative free cash flow, the DCF is sensitive to the assumed working-capital release, and peer multiples set on mature, cash-generating defence companies overstate value. We keep the house 60:40 blend because listed peers exist, but report the DCF-relative divergence as the main finding. Risk is concentrated in order timing and in collection from a few buyers.

## Industry analysis

Source: "Assessment of Global and Indian Defence Electronics and Technology Industry" by Frost & Sullivan (India), **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 245). Treat its estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** global military expenditure USD 3,362.2 bn in CY2025, projected to USD 4,410.9 bn by CY2030 (5.6% CAGR); Europe is the fastest-growing region at 7.5% (p. 161 onwards). India's EO/IR market is put at USD 522 m in CY2020 rising to USD 1.4 bn by CY2030, a 10.5% CAGR (p. 205).
- **Structure:** Indian EO/IR is led by the PSU Bharat Electronics, with Alpha Design, Paras Defence, HAL and L&T as integrators or competitors in parts of the stack (pp. 237-240). Globally the field includes US, European and Israeli primes whose products are often ITAR-restricted; Tonbo positions its ITAR-free portfolio against them (p. 246).
- **Unnamed competition:** import substitutes from Israeli and European OEMs through Indian partners, and in-house development by DRDO labs (IRDE) that can be transferred to other production agencies (p. 231). The document does not quantify Tonbo's share of the total Indian EO/IR market.
- **Regulation:** DAP 2020 favours "Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured" (IDDM) vendors, which requires Indian control and at least 50% indigenous content (p. 247). Exports need government authorisation and depend on foreign buyers' approvals.
- **Cyclicality:** procurement is episodic. The RHP links FY26's decline to delays in international orders from the West Asia conflict and to orders deferred from FY26 to FY27 (p. 22). Emergency procurement can lift demand sharply and then fall away (p. 21).
- **Peer growth for context (FY26):** Paras Defence +30.69%, Data Patterns +30.55%, Astra Microwave +10.62%, Zen Technologies -29.37%, BEL +16.16% (p. 146).

- **Scenario drivers:** year-1 growth 10% / 25% / 40% falling to 5% / 12% / 15% in year 5; EBITDA margin 22% / 27% / 30%; trade NWC 95% / 75% / 55% of revenue; terminal ROIC 10% / 14% / 18%. Rationale and page cites are in the valuation workbook.
- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF, Rs per share):** WACC 14.6% to 18.6% moves value from Rs 84.6 to Rs 54.9; terminal growth 3.5-5.5% moves it by less than Rs 1. Working capital matters more: NWC at 69% of revenue with a 29% margin gives Rs 84.6; NWC 81% with a 25% margin gives Rs 49.8. **Working-capital intensity, not the discount rate, is the dominant lever.**
- **Guards:** the DCF-relative divergence is **-91.9%** (AMBER). Its cause: the relative method prices reported earnings at peer multiples, while the DCF prices free cash flow, which has been negative because working capital absorbed the profit. In the Neutral case terminal ROIC (14%) is below WACC (16.63%) (RED): on current returns, growth does not create value. FY26 ROCE was 14.54% on the issuer's own definition, which excludes the purchased intangibles (p. 144).
- **Reverse DCF at Rs 700:** current free cash flow is negative (-Rs 50.57 cr), so **no multiple can be stated and the entire price rests on a turn that has not yet begun.** One lever at a time: implied growth and implied WACC are not meaningful on negative FCF; the steady-state EBIT margin required at the current NWC intensity is about 258% against 20.2% actual (unreachable); and no level of working capital reaches the price unless working capital turns negative (unreachable). **In plain words: the price needs both working capital to fall far below today's level and margins to hold, at the same time.**

## Moat

- **Intellectual property and know-how:** owned IP in thermal cores, image processing and stabilisation, 130 R&D engineers and US patents (pp. 232, 251). Durable as long as R&D keeps pace; evidence is the product benchmark against two unnamed Indian competitors (Spartan-S sight at 280 g against about 800-900 g, p. 232), which is the issuer's own comparison.
- **Qualification and track record:** field-proven deployments and approvals with the Indian Army, NATO buyers and others are slow for new entrants to replicate (p. 231). Moderately durable, but tenders are re-competed each time and the bid-win ratio fell to 14.29% in Q1 FY27.
- **Policy preference:** IDDM status favours indigenous OEMs (p. 247); this is shared with every Indian competitor and is not specific to Tonbo.
- **What is missing:** no recurring revenue, no long-term contracts with customers or suppliers, and a dependence on imported detectors (p. 25). **The moat is technical and narrow; it has not yet shown up as cash.**

## What would change our view

These conditions can be checked in the first two quarterly results after listing:
- **Cash:** operating cash flow positive for H1 FY27, and cumulative CFO/PAT (FY25 to H1 FY27) above zero.
- **Working capital:** inventory below Rs 220 cr (the Mar-26 level) by 31-Mar-2027 and trade working capital below 75% of FY27 revenue.
- **Execution:** H1 FY27 revenue at or above Rs 180 cr, with the deferred Rs 51.44 cr export order delivered.
- **Orders:** order book at or above Rs 400 cr at 30-Sep-2026, and a bid-win ratio back above 30%.
- **Related party:** purchases from CEAQ Singapore falling below 10% of expenses, or a disclosed arm's-length pricing basis.
- **Price:** at the Neutral blended value of Rs 373, upside would be zero; a price near Rs 400-450 would bring the call to Neutral on the current numbers.
