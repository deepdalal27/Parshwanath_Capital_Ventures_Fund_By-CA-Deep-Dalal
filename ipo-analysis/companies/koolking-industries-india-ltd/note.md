# Koolking Industries India Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 23-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026: a 100% book-built fresh issue of up to ₹3,000 m plus an offer for sale of up to 3,050,000 shares by four selling shareholders, under Reg 6(1) of the ICDR. Keynote Financial Services Limited is sole BRLM. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026, **176 days before the DRHP date and 205 days old at the date of this note**; there is no H1 FY27 data, so no capital should be committed until the RHP is read. **No price band has been announced.** Holding period assumed: 2 to 3 years. Grey market premium is not used. The merchant-banker appendix is the only part that uses outside sources, each named and marked unverified.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹115.82**, FY26 basic EPS of ₹6.07 times 19.08, the lowest P/E in the DRHP's own peer table (p. 179). It is a reference to size the model, not a band and not a view of the right price. A DRHP is a screen, not a decision.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths**
- PAT rose from ₹76.70 m (FY24) to ₹271.37 m (FY25) and ₹332.06 m (FY26); reported EBITDA margin from 6.52% to 10.89% (pp. 86, 182).
- FY26 ROCE 28.39% and ROE 42.86% as reported (p. 182); cash tax is 25.4% of cumulative PBT against the 25.17% statutory rate (p. 86).
- It supplies eight of the top ten Indian HVAC makers (issuer-commissioned CARE report); 96.91% of FY26 revenue came from repeat customers; installed capacity rose from 279.86 m to 419.26 m units in two years (pp. 174-175, 182).
- Eligibility under Reg 6(1) reproduces: average operating profit ₹366.37 m against ₹150 m required (p. 475). Related-party trade is small: purchases ₹28.57 m, 0.54% of revenue (p. 90).

**Points to monitor**
- Operating cash flow was ₹+37.40 m, ₹-66.47 m and ₹-430.08 m against PAT of ₹76.70 m, ₹271.37 m and ₹332.06 m; FY26 working capital absorbed ₹962.71 m as inventory days rose to 116 from 50 and borrowings to ₹1,532.11 m from ₹658.75 m (pp. 87, 167).
- Revenue growth slowed from +101.4% in FY25 to +9.0% in FY26 (₹5,267.45 m) in a year of higher copper and brass prices (pp. 86, 442).
- Ten customers are 89.71% of revenue and Amber Enterprises 28.79%; Supplier 1 is 32.99% of expenses, with no long-term supply contracts (pp. 24-26, 430).
- A customs show-cause notice of ₹130.38 m (14.2% of net worth) is before the Supreme Court; unhedged imports (53.07% of raw material) produced a ₹74.96 m foreign-exchange loss in FY26 (pp. 444, 461).

The cash-flow method and the peer-multiple method are far apart: the Neutral DCF is ₹47.76 per share and the peer-median relative value ₹353.74. The DCF alone is 59% below the ₹115.82 reference; the blend is 73% above it only because of peer P/Es of 19x to 141x. **H1 FY27 cash conversion, the customs outcome and the price band decide this case.**

## 1. Business of the company

Koolking (Malerkotla, Punjab; incorporated 2008) makes brass and copper components for air-conditioner indoor and outdoor units and refrigeration lines: U-bends, evaporator inlet and outlet tubes and sockets, suction, condenser and discharge tubes, C-bends, flare and dead nuts and refrigeration lines. It carries 1,697 SKUs, runs five plants (Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh) and has 773 employees (pp. 174-175).

FY26 revenue of ₹5,267.45 m was indoor-unit components ₹2,208.20 m (41.9%), outdoor-unit components ₹2,111.67 m (40.1%), scrap sales ₹670.75 m (12.7%) and other lines ₹276.83 m (5.3%, our residual) (p. 442). Scrap is counted in revenue from operations and rose 51.1% in FY26. The DRHP does not describe pricing or metal pass-through terms; its MD&A says FY26 materials cost rose with copper and brass prices (p. 442). Gross margin was 28.91% in FY26 (25.34% FY25) (p. 182). Contract labour was 1,257 workers against 883 (p. 444); imports (South Korea, China, Thailand) are 53.07% of raw material purchases (pp. 21, 444).

**Stage of the business: scaling.** Revenue doubled in FY25 and grew 9.0% in FY26 while capacity grew 50% and working capital tripled; the issuer plans three new units at ₹1,530.00 m (p. 134). For valuation this means earnings are rising but cash is being reinvested, so the DCF carries more weight on the build than on today's profit.

## 2. Industry outlook

The industry data come from the CARE Industry Report, **commissioned and paid for by the company** (p. 20), so market-size and growth statements are issuer-sourced. CARE puts the addressable HVAC-component market at ₹34.8 bn in FY26 growing 24.46% a year to FY30, and Indian air-conditioner volume at 12.8 m units in FY27 rising to 16.7 m in FY30 (9.3% CAGR) (pp. 137, 174). On that market the company's ₹5,267 m is about 15%.

The structure is a supplier tier to a few air-conditioner makers (ODMs and OEMs). The listed names the issuer compares itself with are PG Electroplast, Amber Enterprises, KRN Heat Exchanger, Elin Electronics, Centum Electronics and EPack Durables (p. 179); Amber and KRN are also customers. Government localisation policy is cited as supporting domestic sourcing (p. 137). Demand is seasonal, cyclical with consumer durables, and exposed to copper and brass prices and to import duties.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

- **Pre-qualification.** ODMs and OEMs approve suppliers by technical audit (108 audits by ten customers in three years); it protects the seat, not the price (pp. 174, 182).
- **Proximity.** Plants near customer hubs cut logistics cost and lead time (p. 175). Real but replicable; the new units extend it.
- **Scale and cost.** Capacity of 419.26 m units; a 28.91% gross margin and 10.89% EBITDA margin do not show a pricing premium.
- **Switching costs.** Repeat customers are 96.91% of revenue, but no contract tenor or volume commitment is disclosed.
- **Read.** A durable seat with a few large customers; limited evidence of pricing power or a structural cost advantage. The moat is moderate and rests on customer relationships.

**Why DCF and relative differ (-87%).** The relative method applies a median P/E of 97.1x and P/B of 5.4x, set by large listed ODMs, to a ₹332 m profit; the DCF prices the cash a 9% EBITDA-margin business with 20% working-capital intensity can release while it spends ₹1,530 m on new units. The peers trade at 4 to 7 times the 19.1x implied by the reference, and the only priced transaction in the DRHP, the 29-Jan-2024 transfer at ₹5.78 per share after the bonus and split, is not a guide to today's price (p. 190). **The relative value is not reliable here** and the Fund does not rest a view on it: it measures what listed peers fetch, not what a smaller component supplier would. The DCF does not rest on peer pricing at all, but is weighed down by the Year-2 capex with no separate revenue credit beyond the growth path, so it is a conservative reading. The price band will show where the offer sits between them.

**Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple of it can be stated: the price assumes cash generation improves from here. Holding the Neutral growth path, the ₹115.82 reference (implied EV ₹778 cr) needs a steady-state EBIT margin of 25.6% against 10.1% in FY26, and is unreachable through working capital alone (it would have to turn negative). A lower price, or evidence of a higher through-cycle margin, would change this.

**Objects floor.** None: the fresh issue is amount-based. Specific objects total ₹2,130.00 m (71.0% of ₹3,000 m); offer expenses and general corporate purposes (capped at 25% of gross proceeds, ₹750 m) take the balance of ₹870.00 m (pp. 133-134).

**Guards.** The Neutral DCF shows terminal value at 105% of enterprise value because explicit-period free cash flow is negative in total (Year-2 capex); the Bull case sits at 95%. We show the figure and do not rely on it. We did not change assumptions to pass the guard: the issuer's own plan holds working capital near 29% of revenue. WACC less terminal growth is 10.0% and terminal ROIC (17%) is above WACC. Divergence between the methods is explained above.

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

**Growth drivers to track:** air-conditioner volume (CARE: 9.3% CAGR to FY30); the ramp of the three new units and the new heat-exchanger, sheet-metal and service-valve lines; capacity utilisation on 419.26 m units.

**Points to monitor** (normal-course items, each reported once):
- *Cash conversion.* Operating cash flow ₹-459.15 m cumulative against PAT of ₹680.13 m; inventory ₹1,700.24 m (116 days) at 31-Mar-2026 (pp. 87, 167).
- *Customers.* Top 10 are 89.71%, 89.36% and 88.38% (FY26, FY25, FY24); Amber 28.79% and also named as a supplier, with no purchase amount given (pp. 24-26).
- *Suppliers and imports.* Top-10 suppliers 79.33%; Supplier 1 32.99% (22.74% FY25); purchases by purchase order, no long-term contracts; imports 53.07% (pp. 25, 430).
- *Foreign exchange.* ₹74.96 m loss in other expenses in FY26 (16.9% of PBT) plus ₹9.09 m in finance cost; no hedging policy described (pp. 32, 444).
- *Customs matter.* ₹130.38 m show-cause notice; contingent liabilities ₹133.46 m, 14.2% of net worth, not provided (pp. 55, 89, 461).
- *Debt.* ₹1,778.11 m at 31-Jul-2026 including ₹157.25 m of promoter loans; short-term debt ₹1,206.05 m (pp. 37, 449).
- *Auditor and audit trail.* Auditor change on 20-Sep-2025; audit trail not at database level; Plot UDL-7 title pending (pp. 59, 95).
- *Peer set.* The DRHP's peers carry P/Es from 19.08x to 526x (average 176.08x); none is a like-for-like component supplier (p. 179).
- *Revenue includes scrap.* ₹670.75 m of scrap sales (12.7%) is in revenue from operations; FY25 included ₹221.57 m of high-sea sales (p. 394).

**What would change our view** (measurable, from the RHP and H1 FY27 results):
1. Operating cash flow above 50% of PAT for H1 FY27 and inventory days below 100.
2. The customs matter resolved, or the exposure (duty, interest, penalty) quantified under 10% of net worth.
3. Purchases from Amber disclosed with terms, Supplier 1 identified, and the top-10 customer share below 85%.
4. Orders placed for at least half of the ₹1,384.73 m of new-unit machinery, with added capacity in units and the revenue forecast behind the working-capital plan.
5. A hedging policy disclosed and the FX loss in H1 FY27 below 5% of PBT.
6. A price band relative to the ₹115.82 reference: a band at or below it, with the same financials, narrows the gap to the DCF value; one above it widens it.

## 9. Conclusion

**Screen only.** There is no price band, and the DRHP is a screen. The call rule (hard rule 6) needs a price, so no limb is applied. The evidence is balanced: a business that quadrupled PAT in two years at a 28% ROCE, serves most of the large Indian HVAC makers and is eligible on profit, set against negative operating cash flow as working capital tripled, 90% customer concentration, 2.5x net debt to EBITDA and a ₹1,530 m expansion not yet ordered.

At the ₹115.82 reference the Neutral DCF value is ₹47.76 (-59%), the Bull DCF ₹82.33 (-29%), and the peer-multiple value ₹353.74, which we do not rely on. The blended Neutral value is ₹200.75 (+73%), and the blend comes entirely from peer P/Es of 4 to 7 times the reference. **The fair value range on the cash-flow method is ₹22 to ₹82 with ₹48 as the Neutral point; on the market's pricing of listed peers it is far higher.**

Two things decide the case: whether H1 FY27 shows inventory and receivables converting to cash as growth continues, and where the band sits against the 19.1x the reference implies. No capital should be committed until the RHP is read.
