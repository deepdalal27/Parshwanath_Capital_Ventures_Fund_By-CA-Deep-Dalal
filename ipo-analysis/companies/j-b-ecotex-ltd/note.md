# J B Ecotex Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 28-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 30-Sep-2026. Restated consolidated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **190 days old** at the date of this note (07-Oct-2026). **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹90.10**, the September 2025 pre-IPO preferential issue price (₹901 per ₹10 share) adjusted for the 4:1 bonus and 1:2 split. It is a reference point, not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The audited record is more than six months old, so no capital should be committed until the RHP, which must carry newer financials, has been read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** scale, positive cash flow and falling leverage.
- Revenue grew at a 25.3% CAGR over FY24–FY26, from ₹5,273.72 m to ₹8,276.02 m, and sales volume at 23.7%, from 66,321 MT to 101,505 MT (pp. 72, 122).
- Operating cash flow was positive in every year: ₹156.84 m, ₹315.32 m and ₹820.93 m. Cumulative CFO is 201% of cumulative PAT, and about 108% after interest paid (p. 73).
- Net debt to EBITDA fell from 5.25x to 3.40x (p. 122).
- The issuer reports itself as the second largest Indian PET recycler by installed capacity and the only one with both mechanical and chemical recycling, 180,360 MTPA at Aug-2026. This is from a report the issuer commissioned (pp. 220, 224).

**Concerns:** profit has not followed revenue, and returns are near 10%.
- PAT was ₹211.32 m in FY24 and ₹224.92 m in FY26, a 3.2% CAGR. Depreciation (+115%) and finance cost (+145%) rose faster than EBITDA (+69%) (p. 72).
- ROCE was 10.28% and ROE 9.46% in FY26 (p. 122).
- The material subsidiary JB rPET Industries, which runs the chemical recycling plant, had negative net assets of ₹70.80 m and a FY26 loss of ₹17.41 m (p. 355).
- The DRHP gives no customer or supplier concentration percentages, and the audit trail was not enabled at database level in FY26 (pp. 43–45, 244).

On a cash basis (DCF) value is far below the reference; on peer multiples (one listed peer, at a depressed-earnings P/E of 68.9x) it is also below it. **H1 FY27 PAT, the price band and the use of the ₹3,200 m debt repayment will show whether the reference is supportable.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** recycled PET products made from post-consumer PET bottles and other PET waste: recycled polyester staple fibre (RPSF), food-grade recycled PET resin (bottle-to-bottle), recycled PET flakes and chemically recycled resins (p. 221).
- **Who buys it:** 728 customers in FY26 in India and 25 other countries; exports were 20.29% of FY26 revenue against 12.79% in FY25 (pp. 224, 244). Named customers include Varun Beverages and Moon Beverages (p. 223, source: issuer-commissioned report).
- **How it earns:** a conversion margin between the price of PET waste and the price of recycled product. FY26 gross margin (revenue less cost of goods) was 32.39% and EBITDA margin 10.86% (pp. 72, 122). Revenue per tonne was ₹81,533 in FY26.
- **What it owns:** two adjacent plants at Dhamdod, Surat (1.79 million sq ft), the JB Ecotex facility (128,160 MTPA) and the JB rPET facility (52,200 MTPA) (p. 227). Captive power: 7.20 MW wind and 6.60 MW solar; 63.52% of FY26 power came from captive and contracted renewable sources (p. 223).
- **What it outsources:** PET waste collection. It buys from 522 suppliers (over 450 domestic, over 65 overseas) with no long-term supply contracts (pp. 224, 228). Outbound logistics rely on third-party carriers.
- **Structure:** the Company holds 100% of JB rPET Industries Private Limited, acquired from the promoters and others on 26–28 Mar-2025 for ₹250.00 m at ₹10 per share on a net-asset valuation (p. 260). Promoters held 22.50 of the 25.00 million shares sold, so about ₹225 m of the consideration went to nine promoters.

## Industry analysis

Source: the "Recycled resins market" report by Lattice Technologies (1Lattice), dated 25-Sep-2026, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 220). Treat its market estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** the Indian rPET market is put at US$ 2.3 bn in FY26, projected at a 20.0% CAGR to US$ 5.7 bn by FY31; by volume, 1.8 MT to 4.0 MT (17.3% CAGR) (p. 168). The Indian food-grade rPET resin market is put at 300.0 KT in FY26 and 2,210.7 KT by FY31 (p. 187). The issuer's FY26 sales volume of 101,505 MT is about 5.6% of the report's 1.8 MT rPET figure (our arithmetic).
- **Regulation:** mandatory recycled content in rigid plastic packaging of 30%, 40%, 50% and 60% for FY26 to FY29 onward under the extended producer responsibility rules (p. 222). Demand therefore depends on enforcement as well as on rules.
- **Structure:** organised and unorganised players. The DRHP names Ganesha Ecosphere, Pashupati Polytex and BLS Ecotech as key players (p. 378). The report states Ganesha has 64,500 TPA of operating food-grade rPET capacity (p. 188); the issuer's own food-grade capacity is 56,160 MTPA since Aug-2026 (p. 227). Only 17 plants in India hold the FSSAI approval needed for food-grade production, per the same report (p. 224).
- **Unnamed competitors:** global resin makers such as Indorama Ventures, which the report notes is expanding recycling capacity abroad (p. 187), virgin PET suppliers, and unorganised recyclers.
- **Cyclicality:** PET waste and recycled product prices move with virgin PET and crude oil; the DRHP states the business is not seasonal but PET waste availability and price vary by region and season (p. 378).

- **Eligibility:** Regulation 6(1). Anchor portion up to 60% of the QIB portion, one-third reserved for mutual funds. Allocation to anchors is discretionary, and anchor shares are locked in, 50% for 30 days and 50% for 90 days (p. 101).
- **Objects:** up to ₹3,200.00 m repays or prepays loans of the Company and JB rPET, scheduled for FY27; the balance is general corporate purposes, capped at 25% of gross proceeds, and offer expenses (pp. 110–115). There is no capex object. The loans listed total ₹3,493.95 m outstanding at 22-Sep-2026 at 7.01% to 10.50%, a balance-weighted average of about 7.96% (our arithmetic from pp. 112–113). Lenders are HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital; no related-party loan is listed.
- **Funding the subsidiary:** the Company will invest in JB rPET as equity, debt or both; the mode is undecided (p. 115).
- **Shares and holders:** 171,988,460 shares before the issue after the bonus and split; promoters and group 89.06%, 55 shareholders (p. 102). Pre-issue preferential rounds: ₹132 per ₹10 share in Mar-2025 and ₹901 per ₹10 share in Sep–Oct 2025 (₹90.10 on the current share count) (pp. 125–126).
- **Promoter cost:** weighted average cost of acquisition per selling promoter ranges from nil to ₹6.50; the offer-weighted figure is about ₹3.60 (our arithmetic from the cover page). Primary issuances over the last three years carry a WACA of ₹4.79 and secondary transactions ₹1.79 (pp. 127–128).
- **Lock-ins:** promoters' minimum contribution (20% of post-offer capital) for three years, the rest of promoter holding for one year, pre-issue public shareholders six months (p. 100).

## Valuation

All values are ₹ per share on 21.64 crore post-issue diluted shares at the ₹90.10 reference (fresh issue of ₹400 crore at that price, before offer expenses).

| Scenario | DCF | Relative | Blended 60:40 | vs ₹90.10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 11.69 | 69.81 | 34.94 | −61.2% |
| Neutral | 14.03 | 69.81 | 36.34 | −59.7% |
| Bull | 18.05 | 69.81 | 38.76 | −57.0% |

- **DCF.** WACC is 15.76% (house CAPM inputs; pre-tax cost of debt set at 8.00%, near the 7.0–8.0% on most listed loans, pp. 112–113). Terminal growth is 4.50%. The Neutral case has revenue growth easing from 14% to 8%, an EBITDA margin of 10.5% and trade working capital at 13% of revenue. Terminal value is 88.5% of enterprise value, above the 75% guard. Terminal return on invested capital (11% Neutral, 9–13% across cases) is below the WACC, so extra growth does not add value in the model; this follows from an issuer ROCE of about 10%.
- **Relative.** Uses the only listed peer in the DRHP, Ganesha Ecosphere (p. 121), at its P/E of 68.90x, a derived EV/EBITDA of about 21.2x and a P/B of 2.10x, weighted 40:40:20 with a 10% discount. One peer is a thin base, and that peer's FY26 PAT fell 63% from FY25 (₹1,031.20 m to ₹382.14 m, p. 124), which lifts its P/E. Peer enterprise value and net worth are derived from the DRHP's per-share figures (shares = PAT ÷ diluted EPS).
- **Why DCF and relative differ by 80%.** The relative method prices peer earnings at a trough-year multiple. The DCF prices the cash this business converts at a roughly 10% return on capital against a 15.76% cost of capital. The reference price sits near the relative value: P/E is 68.8x pre-issue and 86.7x post-issue on FY26 EPS.
- **Illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast.** If the full ₹3,200 m of repayment saved interest at the 7.96% average rate, pre-tax interest falls by about ₹254.7 m and PAT rises by about ₹190.6 m at a 25.17% tax rate. FY26 PAT would then be ₹415.5 m, or ₹1.92 per post-issue share, a P/E of about 46.9x at ₹90.10. This ignores offer expenses, the general-corporate-purposes amount, the pre-IPO placement and any change in operations.
- **Reverse DCF.** The reference price asks the business to deliver 406.6 times its current free cash flow (₹4.56 crore), in perpetuity. Holding the others at actual levels, it needs perpetual growth of 15.5% on current free cash flow (against 4.50%), or a WACC of 4.76% (against 15.76%), or a steady-state EBIT margin of 54.6% (against 7.08%); no working-capital level reaches it.
- **Sensitivity.** One point of EBITDA margin moves Neutral DCF value by about ₹2.57 per share and one point of WACC by about ₹1.1–1.3. A three-point change in working capital as a share of revenue moves it by about ₹1.5. The grids show both axes.

## What would change our view

Test each of these against the RHP:
1. **Price band.** The Neutral blended value is ₹36.34 and the peer P/E is 68.9x; a band above the reference would widen the gap, one materially below it would narrow it.
2. **Earnings.** Restated H1 FY27 PAT. Over FY24–FY26 it was ₹205–225 m a year. A first half that annualises well above ₹300 m would support the post-repayment arithmetic.
3. **Concentration.** Top-1, top-5 and top-10 customer and supplier shares. The screening tests flag a top customer above 50% or top-10 above 80% of revenue.
4. **Subsidiary.** JB rPET's standalone loss (₹17.41 m in FY26) and net assets (−₹70.80 m), and whether proceeds go in as equity or debt and at what rate.
5. **Use of proceeds.** Which loans are repaid, and whether promoter personal guarantees (pp. 260–261) are released.
6. **Control gap.** Audit trail enabled at database level in the next audited period.
7. **Bottle count.** An explanation of bottles recycled falling from 5,392.31 million to 3,528.44 million (−34.6%) while production volume was 98,435 MT and 99,190 MT (pp. 122, 225).
