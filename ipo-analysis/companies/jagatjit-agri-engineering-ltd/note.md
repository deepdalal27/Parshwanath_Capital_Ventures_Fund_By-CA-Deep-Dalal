# Jagatjit Agri Engineering Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 25-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **199 days old** at the date of this note. **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> **The three years are not on one basis.** FY26 is consolidated and includes the businesses of Saron Mechanical Works I and II (the promoters' proprietorships), which the company acquired with effect from 01-Apr-2025. FY25 and FY24 are the company's standalone accounts, before that acquisition (p. 87). The DRHP's **proforma** figures put the two acquired firms back into FY25 and FY24; this note uses the proforma series for growth and margin comparisons.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹131.69**, an illustrative reference: the lowest listed-peer P/E in the DRHP (Escorts Kubota, 23.35x, p. 156) times FY26 EPS of ₹5.64. It is not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold. A DRHP is a screen, not a decision; the RHP should carry H1 FY27.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** large, tax-paid growth on a proforma basis.
- Proforma revenue rose from ₹2,074.48 m (FY24) to ₹2,537.60 m (FY25) and ₹4,139.70 m (FY26); proforma PAT from ₹80.82 m to ₹206.95 m and ₹313.90 m (pp. 90, 158).
- Units sold rose from 12,234 (proforma FY25) to 19,111 in FY26 (p. 158).
- Tax was paid in cash at 26.1% of cumulative PBT (p. 87).
- Receivables are short: ₹608.65 m of ₹628.74 m is within six months of due date (p. 371).

**Concerns:** the history is short and concentrated, and the promoter ties are extensive.
- The operating business sat in promoter-owned proprietorships until 01-Apr-2025. In FY25 the company bought ₹446.24 m of goods from related parties (60.7% of its materials cost) and sold ₹235.22 m to them (24.5% of its revenue) (pp. 94–95).
- One customer, Seth Agro Industries, was 25.21% of FY26 revenue (₹1,043.46 m); the DRHP does not identify it as a related party (p. 261).
- Northern and Central states were 93.99% of FY26 revenue (p. 246).
- FY26 operating cash flow was ₹26.85 m against PAT of ₹313.90 m (p. 88).
- Both plants stand on land leased from the promoters, and the company lent ₹55.00 m to two promoters in FY26 (pp. 95, 284).

On earnings (peer multiples) the reference price looks full relative to the model; on cash (DCF) it looks well above. **H1 FY27 cash flow, the top customer's identity and the price band will show which reading holds.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** tractor-operated and tractor-mounted agricultural implements under the "Jagatjit" brand: 20 products and 192 SKUs, including Super Seeders, rotavators, straw reapers, laser land levellers and biomass briquette machines (pp. 245, 248).
- **To whom:** farmers, reached through 12 distributors and 233 dealers and by 2,500+ direct customers. FY26 revenue split: distributors 46.16%, dealers 39.92%, direct 13.89% (pp. 247, 260). Exports were 1.36% of revenue (p. 248).
- **How it is priced and earns its margin:** a manufacturing and assembly margin on steel-based implements; FY26 gross margin was 39.06% and EBITDA margin 13.46% (p. 158). Part of demand is helped by central and state subsidy schemes (CRM and SMAM), paid either to the farmer or to the company, which passes it on (p. 56).
- **What it owns and what it outsources:** two Punjab plants (Cheema, Sangrur and Dehlon, Ludhiana) with in-house cutting, fabrication, machining, painting and assembly (p. 246). **Both plants are on land leased from the promoters** until 31-Mar-2055 (Cheema 19.47 acres; Dehlon 16.20 acres) (p. 284). Raw materials come from third-party suppliers without long-term agreements (p. 23).
- **Capacity (units):** Cheema 14,632 produced of 19,875 (73.62%); Dehlon 3,587 of 4,000 (89.68%) in FY26 (p. 267). Capacity is the independent chartered engineer's estimate.
- **Seasonality:** the fourth quarter carried 37.88% of FY26 revenue (p. 35).
- **Subsidiary:** Jagatjit Energies Private Limited; its separate financials are not shown, so it cannot be backed out of the consolidation.

## Industry analysis

Source: the CARE Industry Report, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 25). Treat its market estimates accordingly.
- **Size and growth:** the Indian implements market is put at ₹440 bn in FY26, growing at a 10.2% CAGR to ₹649 bn in FY30 (p. 239). Domestic implements are said to have grown at 10.2% over FY23–26 (p. 248). Farm equipment overall is projected at 7.7% FY26–30 (p. 129). The issuer's proforma revenue CAGR is stated as about 28% over FY23–26 (p. 248).
- **Structure:** moderately fragmented; organised makers hold about 60% of the market and unorganised or regional makers 40% (p. 221).
- **Competitors:** the DRHP names VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment and Escorts Kubota (p. 63), and says there is no direct listed peer with a comparable portfolio (p. 156). Regional fabricators and other implement makers are not named.
- **Regulation and cyclicality:** demand depends on monsoon and crop cycles, state mechanisation schemes (60:40 Centre–State funding) and empanelment of manufacturers by state governments (pp. 35, 56).

## Revenue and profit growth drivers

- **Revenue: volume and channel.** Units sold rose from 8,895 (proforma FY24) to 12,234 (FY25) and 19,111 (FY26). Revenue per unit moved from ₹0.233 m to ₹0.207 m to ₹0.217 m (pp. 158, 90). Growth is therefore mostly volume. SKU count rose from 128 to 192 and states from 18 to 20 (p. 158). Sales through distributors rose from ₹750.08 m (proforma FY25) to ₹1,911.07 m (p. 247).
- **Profit: gross margin and operating leverage.** Proforma gross margin went from 23.58% (FY24) to 37.19% (FY25) and 39.06% (FY26); EBITDA margin from 9.74% to 14.51% and 13.46% (p. 158). The DRHP does not split the FY24-to-FY25 gross-margin change between price, mix and input cost.
- **Comparability effect.** Restated FY25 gross margin of 20.98% reflects a company buying finished goods from promoter firms; FY26 consolidates the manufacturing. The jump from 20.98% to 39.06% is mostly this change in structure.
- **Forward drivers named by the company:** the Dehlon expansion, the biomass machinery line, more states and exports (p. 246).

## The offer and objects

- **Size:** fresh issue of ₹3,000 m plus an offer for sale of up to 3,100,000 shares by promoter Jagatjit Singh (WACA ₹5.40 per share). A pre-IPO placement of up to ₹600 m may reduce the fresh issue (pp. 1, 123). At the ₹131.69 reference the offer is about ₹3,408 m.
- **Eligibility:** Regulation 6(2). The company does not meet Regulation 6(1)(a) and (c), so at least 75% of the offer goes to QIBs, no more than 15% to non-institutional and no more than 10% to retail; if 75% cannot be allotted to QIBs the issue is refunded (p. 57). Retail investors therefore have a small allocation.
- **Anchor portion:** up to 60% of the QIB portion, with one third of that reserved for mutual funds and 6.67% for insurers and pension funds. Anchor allocation is discretionary, and anchor shares are locked in, half for 30 days and half for 90 days.
- **Objects (p. 124):**

|---|---:|---:|
| Working capital | 1,100.00 | 36.7% |
| Dehlon capex | 716.64 | 23.9% |
| Debt repayment/prepayment | 185.00 | 6.2% |
| General corporate purposes and offer expenses | balance, 998.36 before expenses | 33.3% |

- **Working capital:** deployed ₹700 m in FY28 and ₹400 m in FY29, nil in FY27. The plan assumes inventory of 130 days, receivables of 45 days and payables falling from 105 to 95 days because of earlier payment using proceeds (p. 128). Proceeds therefore partly shorten supplier credit.
- **Capex:** no orders have been placed for any of the ₹716.64 m (p. 129). Implementation runs April 2027 to February 2028 (p. 137). Claimable GST and ancillary charges of ₹91.74 m are funded from accruals. The project report is by L&C Techfin Advisory. The expansion is on land leased from a promoter. The capacity table reads "₹ million 4,000 → 33,000" while the Dehlon table is in units (4,000); the unit needs clarification (p. 133).
- **General corporate purposes** are stated at up to 25% of gross proceeds (p. 124). A monitoring agency is to be appointed before the RHP (p. 101).
- **Shares and holders:** 55,650,984 shares of ₹5 before the issue, after a 22:1 bonus and a 2-for-1 split. Promoters hold 99.97%: Jagatjit Singh 88.03%, Simran Saron 8.25%, Dharam Singh 3.69% (p. 110). There is no institutional shareholder before the issue.
- **Price of the 2025 business purchase:** shares were issued to the promoters at ₹1,547 per ₹10 share, about ₹33.63 per ₹5 share after the bonus and split, to pay ₹324.57 m for the two firms (p. 388).

## Valuation

All values are ₹ per share on 7.84 crore post-issue shares at the ₹131.69 reference, blended 80:20 (DCF:relative) because the DRHP lists no direct peers.

| Scenario | DCF | Relative | Blended 80:20 | vs ₹131.69 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 32.55 | 104.34 | 46.91 | −64.4% |
| Neutral | 49.11 | 104.34 | 60.16 | −54.3% |
| Bull | 64.65 | 104.34 | 72.59 | −44.9% |

- **DCF.** WACC 16.49% (risk-free 6.5%, beta 1.1, equity risk premium 7.0%, size 2.0%, company-specific 3.0%; pre-tax cost of debt 7.56%, from finance cost ₹101.63 m over closing borrowings). Terminal growth 4.50%. Neutral: revenue growth easing from 14% to 8%, EBITDA margin 12.5%, trade working capital 21% of revenue. Terminal value is 86.7% of enterprise value, above the 75% warning level.
- **Relative.** Peer P/E from the DRHP: VST 33.5x, Indo Farm 27.6x, Escorts Kubota 23.4x, median 27.6x (p. 156). The peers make tractors and tillers, not implements, so the match is weak.
- **Why DCF and relative differ by 53%.** Relative value prices reported FY26 earnings. The DCF prices cash after working capital, and the model's near-term cash flows are negative because the working capital the issuer projects (₹1,029.46 m rising to ₹2,653.59 m by FY29, p. 127) absorbs the growth.
- **Reverse DCF.** Free cash flow is negative in the first years, so the reference price cannot be expressed as a multiple of current cash flow. In steady state it needs an EBIT margin of about 42% against 12.3% in FY26, and working capital below zero, which is unreachable.
- **Sensitivity.** Moving trade working capital from 21% to 15% of revenue lifts Neutral value from ₹49.11 to ₹53.18 at a 12.5% margin; each point of margin is worth about ₹3.7.
- **Post-money returns.** FY26 PAT over post-issue net worth (₹709.29 m + ₹3,000 m gross) is about 8.5%, against 44.26% pre-issue RoNW (p. 155).
- **The reference is generous to the issuer.** It uses the lowest peer P/E on pre-issue EPS; on post-issue EPS of ₹3.99 the same price is 32.9x.

## What would change our view

Test each of these against the RHP:
1. Audited or reviewed H1 FY27 operating cash flow is positive and at least half of H1 PAT (supportive), or negative again (adverse).
2. The top customer's identity: if Seth Agro Industries is related to the promoters or its share of revenue stays above 20%, concentration is higher than the DRHP shows.
3. The price band against the Neutral blended value (₹60) and the 27.6x peer-median P/E.
4. Share of FY26 revenue sold under CRM or SMAM subsidy schemes; a share above 50% would make demand policy-dependent.
5. Purchase orders placed for the ₹716.64 m capex, and a clarified capacity unit.
6. Whether the ₹185 m debt repayment is term debt or revolving working capital, and whether promoter guarantees are released.
7. Like-for-like revenue growth in H1 FY27 against the proforma H1 FY26.

## Risks

- Demand depends on government subsidy schemes (p. 56).
- Revenue is concentrated in one customer and one region.
- Working capital is heavy: inventory 132 days and ₹1,150 m of bank lines with ₹945.02 m drawn at 31-Aug-26 (pp. 125–126).
- Promoters hold 99.97% and sell 3.10 million shares; both plants and the expansion depend on promoter-leased land.
- The company has stated past non-compliance with RoC filings and past director disqualification (p. 24).
- Promoter remuneration began at ₹60.00 m in FY26 (₹30.00 m, ₹18.00 m and ₹12.00 m), 19.1% of PAT, with ₹5.00 m payable at year end (pp. 94–95).

## Verification
