# Jagatjit Agri Engineering Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 25-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **197 days old** at the date of this note. **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers; PDF page minus 6) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> **Comparability warning.** FY26 is *consolidated* and includes the businesses of Saron Mechanical Works I and II (both promoter proprietorships) acquired with effect from 01-Apr-2025. FY25 and FY24 restated figures are *standalone* of the much smaller pre-acquisition company, from special-purpose Ind AS statements. Growth in this note is therefore measured on the issuer's unaudited **proforma** FY24 and FY25 (pp. 91-92) against restated FY26, and says so each time.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹158.47**, which is 28.10x (the average P/E of the issuer's three listed peers, p. 155) times FY26 basic EPS of ₹5.64 (p. 154). It is a mechanical reference, not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2-3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The RHP must carry financials no more than six months old, so it should include H1 FY27. No capital should be committed until the RHP is read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** fast growth with profit taxed in cash.
- Proforma revenue rose from ₹2,074.48 m (FY24) to ₹2,537.60 m (FY25) and ₹4,139.70 m (FY26), +22.3% and +63.1%. Proforma PAT rose from ₹80.82 m to ₹206.95 m and ₹313.90 m (p. 87, 92).
- Current tax was ₹130.30 m on PBT of ₹444.12 m (29.3%), and ₹104.52 m of direct tax was paid in FY26 (pp. 87-88).
- ROCE 31.10% and interest cover 5.03x in FY26 on the issuer's definitions (p. 158).
- The top 10 suppliers were 40.53% of FY26 raw-material purchases (p. 33).

**Concerns:** cash conversion, concentration and an untested audited record.
- FY26 operating cash flow was ₹26.85 m against PAT of ₹313.90 m (8.6%). Inventory rose ₹445.46 m and other assets ₹281.35 m (p. 88).
- Top customer Seth Agro Industries was 25.21% and the top 10 customers 50.04% of FY26 revenue, all dealers, with no long-term agreements (p. 36).
- Borrowings are ₹1,344.07 m (debt/equity 1.89x), of which ₹1,340.89 m is covered by promoter personal guarantees (pp. 40, 158).
- Only FY26 is audited on the enlarged business. The earlier two years are the pre-acquisition company.

On earnings (peer P/E) the reference price is by construction in line with listed peers. On cash (DCF) the model gives values below the reference in all three scenarios, because working capital absorbs about ₹0.20 of every incremental rupee of revenue and the plan adds a large plant. **H1 FY27 operating cash flow, the price band and the top-customer share in the RHP will show which reading holds.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** tractor-operated and tractor-mounted agricultural implements for "end-to-end crop solutions", 20 products and 192 SKUs in FY26. The top five by revenue are Super Seeder, Laser Land Leveller, Rotavator, Straw Reaper and a fifth machine (pp. 261, 267).
- **Who buys it:** farmers through a dealer and distributor network. Revenue is 98.61% domestic, in 20 states (10 in FY24). Punjab is 25.79% and Haryana 18.48% of FY26 revenue (pp. 56, 158).
- **How it earns:** manufacturing margin on fabricated steel products. Raw-material cost is 61.22% of FY26 revenue (p. 42). Gross margin was 39.06% and EBITDA margin 13.46%. Part of the sales price is subject to Central and State mechanisation subsidies (60:40), which are either paid to the farmer or credited to the company and passed on (p. 56).
- **What it owns vs outsources:** two plants, Cheema (Sangrur, 19,875 units installed) and Dehlon (Ludhiana, 4,000 units installed), covering cutting, bending, fabrication, machining, painting and assembly. Steel plates, sheets, pipes, gearboxes, hydraulics, tyres and electricals are bought in with no long-term supply contracts (pp. 33, 267). The Dehlon land (16.20 acres) is leased from promoter Jagatjit Singh at ₹0.08 m a month, rising 15% every three years (p. 129).
- **Subsidiary:** Jagatjit Energies Private Limited, bought from promoters in FY26 for ₹0.075 m and ₹0.025 m (p. 94).

## Industry analysis

Source: CARE Analytics and Advisory, "Industry Research Report on Agri Equipment Sector" dated 23-Sep-2026, **exclusively commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 3). Treat its market claims accordingly.
- **Growth:** the domestic farm-equipment market is projected to grow at a 7.7% CAGR over FY26-FY30 (p. 129).
- **Structure:** moderately fragmented. Organised manufacturers are put at about 60% of the implements market in FY26, with 40% held by unorganised and regional fabricators; the organised share is expected to rise (p. 221).
- **Peers named in the DRHP:** VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment and Escorts Kubota. The DRHP states that none has a comparable product portfolio or business model (p. 156).
- **Unnamed competitors:** regional implement makers and local fabricators; tractor makers' own implement ranges.
- **Cyclicality and regulation:** demand follows monsoon, crop prices, farm incomes and state subsidy budgets (CRM and SMAM schemes), which are set state by state (p. 56).

## Revenue and profit growth drivers

- **Revenue:** units sold rose from 8,895 (FY24 proforma) to 12,234 (FY25 proforma) and 19,111 (FY26), +37.5% and +56.2%. Revenue per unit was ₹233.2 thousand, ₹207.4 thousand and ₹216.6 thousand, so growth was volume-led rather than price-led (p. 158). Part of FY26 is the first year of consolidating the acquired businesses, and the subsidiary is not in the proforma.
- **Gross margin:** 23.58%, 37.19%, 39.06%. The 13.6-point jump in FY25 proforma is the largest single driver of profit and is not explained in the sections read.
- **Margin bridge, FY25 proforma to FY26 (pts of revenue):** gross margin +1.87; employee cost 10.22% to 6.66% (+3.56); other expenses 12.46% to 18.94% (-6.48); EBITDA margin 14.51% to 13.46% (-1.05). Employee cost rose only 6.3% (₹259.35 m to ₹275.61 m) while revenue rose 63.1%, and other expenses rose 148% (₹316.17 m to ₹784.09 m). The split inside other expenses is not given in the pages read.
- **One-offs:** other income ₹35.03 m is 7.9% of FY26 PBT; expected credit loss provision of ₹34.90 m was charged (p. 88).
- **Operating leverage:** employee cost as a share of revenue fell, but the other-expense rise offset most of it.

## Valuation and reverse DCF

Reference ₹158.47 (see Basis). Model basis: FY26 consolidated, 55,650,984 pre-offer shares, ₹3,000 m fresh issue, 80:20 DCF:relative (the DRHP states there is no directly comparable listed peer). Peers used: VST Tillers (P/E 33.34x) and Indo Farm (27.62x); Escorts Kubota (23.35x) excluded on scale (₹115,402 m revenue, p. 156). Use of proceeds funds only the capex (₹716.64 m) in Neutral and Bull.

| Scenario | Revenue growth FY27→FY31 | EBITDA margin | NWC % revenue | DCF ₹/share | Relative ₹/share | Blended ₹/share | vs reference |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 8% → 5% | 10.5% | 22% | 37.61 | 109.95 | 52.08 | -67.1% |
| Neutral | 18% → 8% | 12.5% | 20% | 52.62 | 109.95 | 64.08 | -59.6% |
| Bull | 28% → 12% | 13.5% | 18% | 66.54 | 109.95 | 75.22 | -52.5% |

Rationale, page-cited: FY27 growth is limited by installed capacity of 23,875 units against FY26 production of 18,219 (about +31% at constant mix, p. 267) until Dehlon expands in FY28; the margin range spans proforma FY24 (9.74%) to FY26 (13.46%) (p. 158); NWC 18.5% to 20.5% of revenue (pp. 85, 91).

- **Divergence:** DCF and relative differ by 52.1%. The relative method prices FY26 earnings (PAT ₹313.90 m); the DCF prices cash, and FY26 free cash flow (CFO ₹26.85 m less capex ₹321.8 m) was negative. That separation is the finding. The terminal value is above 75% of enterprise value in the DCF, so it is largely a terminal assumption.
- **Reverse DCF:** current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple can be stated; the whole reference price rests on a turn in cash conversion that has not yet begun. The four-lever solve was not run beyond the toolkit's output.
- **Dilution (our computation, before offer expenses and general corporate purposes):** adding ₹3,000 m to FY26 equity of ₹709.29 m gives post-issue equity of about ₹3,709 m and an FY26 ROE of about 8.5% on unchanged PAT, against the 44.26% on pre-issue net worth. Post-issue NAV at the reference is about ₹49.7 a share (about 3.2x book). Promoter average cost of ₹35.15 is 22% of the reference.
- **Grey market premium** is not used and does not move fair value.
- **Caution:** the reference is built from the same peer multiples as the relative leg, so the gap to the reference comes from the DCF leg. A price band set well below ₹158.47 would change the comparison.

## What would change our view

1. **Price band:** a cap price at or below the Neutral blended value (about ₹64) would remove the gap to the cash-based value; a cap above ₹158.47 (28.1x FY26 EPS) would sit above the issuer's own peer-average P/E.
2. **Cash conversion:** H1 FY27 operating cash flow above 50% of H1 PAT, against 8.6% in FY26.
3. **Customer concentration:** RHP top-customer share below 20% of revenue, or disclosure of any link between Seth Agro Industries and the promoters.
4. **Margin:** H1 FY27 EBITDA margin at or above 13.0%, with the other-expense breakdown that explains the 148% rise.
5. **Working capital:** RHP closing inventory days at or below 130 and receivable days at or below 45 on closing balances.
6. **Audited record:** reconciliation of the proforma and actual purchase accounting (goodwill ₹65.66 m vs ₹4.55 m) and a proforma cash flow statement.
7. **Capex:** firm orders placed for the Dehlon plant, and the lease term for the promoter-owned land disclosed.

## Risks

- Dealer and customer concentration without long-term agreements (p. 36).
- Subsidy dependence: 60% Central and 40% State schemes, administered state by state (p. 56).
- Leverage and working-capital dependence: ₹945.02 m of ₹1,150 m limits drawn; promoter personal guarantees on ₹1,340.89 m (pp. 40, 42).
- Execution of an 8.25x capacity expansion on a promoter-leased site, not independently appraised (pp. 129-131).
- Governance: promoters' limited experience of listed-company governance; past director disqualification (pp. 36, 48).
- Litigation: against the company, 8 tax matters and 1 statutory or regulatory matter (₹27.71 m); by the company, 2 criminal matters and 1 material civil matter (₹14.56 m); against promoters, 2 regulatory matters (₹1.00 m) (p. 54).
- Seasonal and weather exposure of farm demand.

## Verification

- **Totals reproduce:** FY26 expenses sum to ₹3,730.62 m (printed 3,730.61, rounding); PBT ₹444.12 m less tax ₹130.21 m gives PAT ₹313.91 m (printed 313.90). FY25 and FY24 expense lines and the proforma P&L (PBT ₹268.98 m less tax ₹62.03 m = ₹206.95 m) also reproduce.
- **Balance sheets balance:** FY26 assets ₹3,188.36 m = equity ₹709.29 m + liabilities ₹2,479.07 m; proforma FY25 ₹1,957.55 m.
- **EBITDA reproduces the KPI table:** ₹444.12 m + finance cost ₹101.63 m + depreciation ₹46.66 m - other income ₹35.03 m = ₹557.38 m.
- **Cash flow ties:** ₹26.85 m - ₹196.81 m + ₹236.13 m = ₹66.17 m; closing cash ₹68.67 m including ₹2.15 m acquired.
- **EPS and NAV:** ₹313.90 m ÷ 55,650,984 = ₹5.64; ₹709.29 m ÷ 55,650,984 = ₹12.75. Peer P/E equals price ÷ EPS (VST 4,039.90 ÷ 121.16 = 33.34).
- **Differences inside the document:** RoNW for FY26 is 44.26% on net worth (p. 155) and 44.54% in the peer table (p. 156); the latter is PAT ÷ tangible net worth (₹313.90 m ÷ ₹704.74 m). Not an error, a different base.
- **Not re-performed:** the issuer's Regulation 6(2) eligibility computation. The summary restated statements (pp. 85-93) were read as text; the notes to the full restated annexure were not each reviewed.
- **Merchant-banker figures:** listing gains are recomputed from DRHP issue and opening prices.
