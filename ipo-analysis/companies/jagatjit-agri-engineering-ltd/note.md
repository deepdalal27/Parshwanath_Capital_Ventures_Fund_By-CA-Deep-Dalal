# Jagatjit Agri Engineering Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 25-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **192 days old** at the date of this note (09-Oct-2026). **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹131.69**, which is the lowest listed-peer P/E in the DRHP (Escorts Kubota, 23.35x, p. 156) applied to FY26 basic EPS of ₹5.64 (p. 154). It is our construct, not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold.
>
> **The three years are not like-for-like.** FY26 is consolidated and includes two businesses (Saron Mechanical Works I and II, owned by the promoters) that the Company took over on 01-Apr-2025. FY24 and FY25 are the small standalone Company before that. The DRHP supplies unaudited proforma figures for FY25 and FY24 that combine the three businesses (pp. 91–92); this note uses them for growth.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The audited record is more than six months old, so no capital should be committed until the RHP, which must carry newer financials, has been read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** profitable growth on a like-for-like base, high stated returns, cash tax above the statutory rate.
- On the proforma base, revenue rose from ₹2,074.48 m (FY24) to ₹2,537.60 m (FY25) and ₹4,139.70 m (FY26), growth of 22.3% and then 63.1%; PAT rose from ₹80.82 m to ₹206.95 m to ₹313.90 m (+51.7% in FY26) (pp. 87, 92).
- EBITDA margin was 13.46% in FY26 against 14.51% proforma FY25; ROCE was 31.10% and interest cover 5.03x (p. 157).
- Current tax was ₹130.30 m, 29.3% of PBT, above the 25.17% statutory rate (p. 87).

**Concerns:** cash conversion, concentration and a structure that fails if institutions do not take 75%.
- FY26 operating cash flow was ₹26.85 m against PAT of ₹313.90 m (8.6%), after a ₹445.46 m inventory build and a ₹281.35 m rise in other assets. After interest and lease payments it was negative, −₹87.06 m (p. 88).
- The top customer was 25.21% of FY26 revenue and the top ten 50.04%; distributors were 46.16% and dealers 39.92% of revenue. There are no long-term customer or supplier agreements (pp. 29, 36).
- The offer is made under Regulation 6(2) because net worth was ₹5.32 m in FY24. If less than 75% of the offer is allotted to QIBs, the offer fails (p. 57).
- The proceeds fund a capacity rise at Dehlon from 4,000 to 33,000 units against 18,219 units produced across both plants in FY26 (pp. 136, 267).

On a cash basis (DCF) the value is below the reference; on peer multiples, after a 25% discount for business-model gap and size, it is closer to the reference but still below. **H1 FY27 operating cash flow, the price band and the QIB response will show whether the reference is supportable.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** tractor-operated and tractor-mounted agricultural implements for land preparation, sowing, crop-residue management, levelling and harvesting, plus briquette machines for residue. 20 products, 192 SKUs and 19,111 units sold in FY26 (p. 157). Top five by FY26 revenue: Super Seeder, Laser Land Leveller, Rotavator, Straw Reaper and Briquette Machine (p. 267).
- **Who buys it:** farmers, through 11 distributors and 224 dealers in 20 states (FY26); exports were 1.36% of revenue (pp. 29, 157). Some products are bought at subsidised prices under State schemes; empanelment is a condition, and State payments have been delayed (p. 57).
- **How it earns:** manufacturing margin on steel-based equipment. FY26 gross margin was 39.06% and EBITDA margin 13.46% (p. 157). Cost of materials was 61.22% of revenue (p. 473).
- **What it owns:** two plants, Cheema (Sangrur) and Dehlon (Ludhiana), with 19,875 and 4,000 units of installed capacity in FY26 (p. 267). Dehlon sits on 16.20 acres leased from the promoters, rent escalating 15% every three years (p. 129).
- **What it outsources:** raw materials and bought-out parts (gear boxes, tynes, pipes, HR sheets); no long-term supply agreements. 224 dealers and 11 distributors sell the product; distributors earn a commission (pp. 23, 29).
- **Structure:** the Company took over the businesses of Saron Mechanical Works I (Dharam Singh) and II (Jagatjit Singh) by slump sale on 01-Apr-2025 at carrying values. Net assets were ₹66.09 m and ₹253.94 m; consideration of ₹69.13 m and ₹255.44 m was paid by issuing 209,804 shares of ₹10 at ₹1,547 each (22-Oct-2025) (pp. 387–388, 105). It also bought Jagatjit Energies Private Limited from Jagatjit Singh and Lakshjit Singh for ₹0.10 m (p. 94).

## Industry analysis

Source: "Industry Research Report on Agri Equipment Sector" by CARE Analytics and Advisory, dated 23-Sep-2026, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 66). Treat its market estimates accordingly.
- **Size and growth:** the report projects the domestic farm equipment market at a 7.7% CAGR over FY26–FY30 (p. 129). The organised segment is put at about 60% of the implements market, at ₹264 bn in FY26 and ₹415 bn in FY30 (p. 221). The issuer's FY26 revenue of ₹4.14 bn is about 1.6% of the FY26 organised figure (our arithmetic).
- **Structure:** moderately fragmented; the remaining 40% is unorganised and regional (p. 221). The report calls the issuer the third-largest homegrown agricultural implement maker in India and puts its straw reaper share at about 13% by value in FY26 (p. 236).
- **Competitors named in the DRHP:** VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment and Escorts Kubota, which the DRHP itself says are not direct peers (p. 156). The group company Goodfield Technology Private Limited has common pursuits and may compete (p. 23).
- **Regulation and cyclicality:** demand is tied to State subsidy schemes, crop-residue management rules in Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, and the kharif–rabi cycle; the fourth quarter is seasonally weaker (pp. 24, 236).

## The offer and objects

- **Size:** fresh issue of up to ₹3,000.00 m plus an offer for sale of up to 3,100,000 shares by Jagatjit Singh (cover page). At the ₹131.69 reference the OFS is ₹408.2 m and the offer ₹3,408.2 m, of which the OFS is 12.0% (our arithmetic). A pre-IPO placement of up to ₹600.00 m may reduce the fresh issue (p. 123).
- **Eligibility:** Regulation 6(2). At least 75% of the offer to QIBs, not more than 15% to non-institutional bidders and 10% to retail. Anchor allocation is discretionary, up to 60% of the QIB portion, and anchor shares are locked in, 50% for 30 days and 50% for 90 days. An anchor bid therefore cannot be sold on listing day (pp. 57, 82).
- **Objects (₹ m):** working capital 1,100.00 (36.7% of gross proceeds), Dehlon capex 716.64 (23.9%), debt repayment 185.00 (6.2%); the balance of 998.36 (33.3%) is offer expenses plus general corporate purposes, capped at 25% of gross proceeds (₹750 m) (pp. 123–124). Deployment: nothing in FY27; working capital 700.00 in FY28 and 400.00 in FY29; capex and debt repayment in FY28 (p. 124).
- **Working capital object tested:** the issuer's table shows a requirement of ₹1,029.46 m at FY26, funded by ₹852.66 m of short-term borrowings; sanctioned limits were ₹1,150 m with ₹945.02 m used at 31-Aug-2026 (pp. 125–126). The ₹1,100.00 m object is therefore about the size of the FY26 requirement, deployed over two later years.
- **Capex object:** ₹716.64 m excludes ₹91.74 m of GST and ₹2.60 m of freight, which the Company pays itself. The project report is by L & C Techfin Advisory, stated not to be a related party; the plan is not appraised by a bank or institution (pp. 124–125).
- **Shares and holders:** 55,650,984 shares before the issue; promoters hold 99.97% (Jagatjit Singh 88.03%, Simran Saron 8.25%, Dharam Singh 3.69%) (p. 110). 22:1 bonus on 27-May-2026 and 1:2 split on 04-May-2026 (p. 105).
- **Promoter cost:** weighted average cost of acquisition ₹5.40 for the selling promoter and ₹5.94 for all shares in the last three years, including bonus shares at nil (pp. 1, 120). The Oct-2025 allotment was ₹33.63 per ₹5 share after bonus and split (our arithmetic from pp. 105, 388). That implies a pre-money value of about ₹1,872 m (6.0x FY26 PAT) against ₹7,329 m at the reference (23.3x).
- **Lock-ins:** promoters' minimum contribution (20% of post-offer capital) 18 months; remaining promoter holding and all pre-issue non-promoter holdings six months (p. 116). At the reference that releases about 368.4 lakh promoter shares and 0.18 lakh non-promoter shares after six months (our arithmetic).

## Valuation

All values are ₹ per share on 7.84 crore post-issue diluted shares at the ₹131.69 reference (fresh issue of ₹300 crore at that price, before offer expenses).

| Scenario | DCF | Relative | Blended 80:20 | vs ₹131.69 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 37.64 | 103.48 | 50.81 | −61.4% |
| Neutral | 54.38 | 103.48 | 64.20 | −51.3% |
| Bull | 73.08 | 103.48 | 79.16 | −39.9% |

- **DCF.** WACC is 16.78% (house CAPM inputs plus a 3% size premium; pre-tax cost of debt 9.5%, set near finance cost over average borrowings). Terminal growth is 4.50%. The Neutral case has revenue growth easing from 22% to 10%, EBITDA margin 13.0% and trade working capital 20% of revenue (FY26: 20.3%). Free cash flow to the firm is negative in years 1–3 (−₹21.1 crore, −₹30.9 crore, −₹9.5 crore) because growth absorbs working capital, and positive from year 4. Terminal value is 89.6% of enterprise value, above the 75% guard. Terminal return on capital (18% Neutral) is above the WACC.
- **Relative.** Uses the DRHP's three listed peers (VST Tillers 33.34x P/E, Indo Farm 27.62x, Escorts Kubota 23.35x). The DRHP says none is a direct peer; all three make tractors or tillers, are far larger and are close to debt-free. We apply a 25% discount for business model, size and liquidity and weight the blend 80:20 toward the DCF because no comparable peer exists. Peer net debt is shown as gross borrowings because the DRHP gives no cash figure.
- **Why DCF and relative differ by 47%.** The relative method prices peer earnings. The DCF prices cash, and this business has not yet converted profit into cash: working capital is about 20% of revenue and each rupee of added revenue absorbed about ₹0.20 of trade working capital between proforma FY25 and FY26 (our arithmetic from pp. 85–86, 91). The gap is the investment question: does the profit turn into cash once growth slows?
- **Dilution.** On the same FY26 profit, post-issue ROE is about 8.5% against 53.9% pre-issue, before offer expenses (our arithmetic: ₹313.90 m ÷ (₹704.74 m + ₹3,000 m)). P/E rises from 23.3x pre-issue to 32.9x on post-issue shares.
- **Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple can be stated and the reference price rests on a turn that has not yet begun. Holding the other drivers at actual levels, the price needs a steady-state EBIT margin of 43.1% (against 12.34%); the growth and WACC levers cannot be solved on negative current cash flow; and no working-capital level reaches it.
- **Sensitivity.** The grids show WACC against terminal growth and the margin and working-capital axes; at Neutral, one point of WACC moves value by about ₹1.5 per share.

## What would change our view

Test each of these against the RHP:
1. **Price band.** The Neutral blended value is ₹64.20 and the peer P/E range is 23.35x–33.34x; a band well below ₹131.69 would narrow the gap.
2. **Cash conversion.** H1 FY27 operating cash flow. A first half where CFO exceeds 50% of PAT would support the DCF; another build of inventory (raw materials rose from ₹41.78 m to ₹478.99 m in FY26, p. 473) would not.
3. **Earnings quality.** FY26 other income without the ₹26.05 m of written-back balances, and the reconciliation of proforma to restated goodwill (₹65.66 m vs ₹4.55 m).
4. **Concentration.** The identity of the top customer (25.21% of FY26 revenue) and its share in H1 FY27; the share of revenue from related parties above 2%.
5. **Director loans.** Repayment of the ₹55.00 m advanced to Jagatjit Singh and Dharam Singh before listing, and confirmation that no proceeds repay promoter loans.
6. **Capex.** Orders placed and quotations for the ₹716.64 m Dehlon project, and utilisation of the 4,000-unit Dehlon line (89.68% in FY26) after commissioning.
7. **QIB response.** Anchor book quality and QIB subscription of at least 75%, without which the offer fails.
8. **Control gap.** Audit trail enabled at database level through the full next audited period.

## Verification

- **Restated P&L reproduces:** FY26 expenses sum to ₹3,730.61 m; PBT ₹444.12 m less tax ₹130.21 m equals PAT ₹313.90 m. FY25: ₹80.20 m less ₹14.74 m = ₹65.47 m (PAT as printed). FY24: ₹38.32 m less ₹13.30 m = ₹25.02 m.
- **Balance sheet balances** in all three years (FY26 assets ₹3,188.36 m equal equity plus liabilities).
- **EBITDA reproduces the issuer's KPI:** FY26 PBT ₹444.12 m + finance cost ₹101.63 m + depreciation ₹46.66 m − other income ₹35.03 m = ₹557.38 m.
- **EPS reproduces:** ₹313.90 m ÷ 55,650,984 shares = ₹5.64; peer P/E ₹2,900.15 ÷ ₹124.20 = 23.35x.
- **Cash flow ties:** ₹26.85 m − ₹196.81 m + ₹236.13 m = ₹66.17 m increase; with ₹0.34 m opening and ₹2.15 m acquired, closing cash is ₹68.67 m.
- **Acquisition consideration ties:** 209,804 shares × ₹1,547 = ₹324.57 m = ₹69.13 m + ₹255.44 m.
- **Extraction:** the restated statements are text-readable in the PDF, so no OCR was used. Page numbers are printed folios; PDF pages differ by six in the front block.
- **Not read:** the full risk-factor text, MD&A and annexure notes were read selectively. Statements about the pages read are limited to those pages.
- **Workbooks:** the toolkit verification passed 44 of 44 checks.
