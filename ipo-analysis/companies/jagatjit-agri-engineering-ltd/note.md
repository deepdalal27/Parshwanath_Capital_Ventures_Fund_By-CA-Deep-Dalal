# Jagatjit Agri Engineering Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 25-Sep-2026 (SEBI filing page dated 01-Oct-2026), 100% book-built fresh issue of Rs 3,000 m plus an offer for sale of up to 3,100,000 shares by promoter Jagatjit Singh, under Reg 6(2) of the ICDR (at least 75% to QIBs). Keynote Financial Services Limited is sole BRLM. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026, **178 days before the DRHP date and 198 days old at the date of this note**. No price band exists. All issuer figures are from the DRHP; printed page numbers are cited. Only the merchant-banker section uses outside sources, each named and unverified.
>
> Upside is measured against **Rs 131.69**: FY26 basic EPS of Rs 5.64 times 23.35, the lowest peer P/E in the DRHP's own table (p. 156). It is a reference to size the model, not a price band and not a view of the right price. Exit route assumed: 2-3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The RHP must carry financials no more than six months old and should include H1 FY27. Until it is read, this note supports no commitment of capital.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** growth and returns are high on a like-for-like basis, and profit is taxed.
- Proforma revenue (the DRHP's own like-for-like series) went from Rs 2,074.48 m in FY24 to Rs 2,537.60 m in FY25 and Rs 4,139.70 m in FY26; proforma PAT from Rs 80.82 m to Rs 206.95 m to Rs 313.90 m (p. 158).
- ROCE was 31.10%, ROE 53.89% and interest cover 5.03x in FY26 (p. 158).
- Current tax was 26.1% of cumulative PBT (Rs 147.09 m of Rs 562.64 m), above the 25.17% statutory rate (p. 87).
- No outstanding criminal proceedings against the company (p. 489); contingent liabilities are Rs 71.70 m, 10.1% of equity (p. 93).

**Concerns:** the history is not comparable, cash has not followed profit, and the revenue base is concentrated.
- FY26 restated revenue (Rs 4,139.70 m) is 4.3x FY25 (Rs 959.26 m) because the promoters' two proprietorships (SMW I and II) were absorbed from 1-Apr-2025. In FY25 the company sold 21.9% of its revenue to SMW II and bought 52.6% of its expenses from it (pp. 262-263).
- FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 26.85 m against PAT of Rs 313.90 m (8.6%); cumulative CFO/PAT is 69.7% (p. 88). Inventory stands at Rs 1,137.11 m, 132 days (pp. 85, 127).
- Seth Agro Industries is 25.21% of FY26 revenue (17.20% in FY25) and Northern and Central states are 93.99% (pp. 247, 261).
- At the Rs 131.69 reference the issue is 23.35x FY26 EPS and 35.4x proforma FY25 EPS; blended fair value in our model is Rs 47.77 / 61.52 / 77.63 (Bear / Neutral / Bull).

The two readings separate on whether FY26 is the new run-rate. If the Oct-2025 to Mar-2026 sales season repeats at the FY26 level and inventory converts to cash, the returns shown are real. If FY26 was inflated by one distributor and by a one-time stocking of the channel, growth will slow toward the industry's 10.2% and the working-capital need will outrun profit. **H1 FY27 cash flow, the identity and terms of Seth Agro Industries, and the RHP price band are what resolve it.**

## Business model

The company designs and builds tractor-mounted implements: Super Seeder, Straw Reaper, Rotavator, Laser Land Leveller, Reaper Binder, briquette machines and 15 others (p. 266). Super Seeder (Rs 1,365.60 m, 32.99% of FY26 revenue) and Straw Reaper (Rs 1,079.40 m, 26.07%) together are 59.06% (p. 26). Both are crop-residue machines, and the DRHP says demand is linked to central and state schemes (CRM and SMAM) funded about 60% by the centre and 40% by states, with past delays in subsidy payments (p. 56).

Customers are farmers, reached through 233 dealers and 12 distributors in 20 states (p. 158). Dealers and distributors were 86.08% of FY26 revenue (dealers 39.92%, distributors 46.16%, direct 13.89%), against 64.46% in FY25 (p. 247). Dealers and distributors get 30 to 45 days of credit; distributors earn commission and sales incentives; there is a one-year warranty (p. 264). The company makes in-house from cutting and bending to machining, painting and assembly at two plants, Cheema and Dehlon, and buys steel, gearboxes, bearings and other bought-out parts (p. 266). Gross margin is 39.06% because, after the transfer, it earns both the manufacturing and the selling margin that earlier sat with the promoters' proprietorship; the proforma FY25 gross margin was 37.19% (p. 158).

## Industry analysis

The industry data come from the CARE Industry Report, **commissioned and paid for by the company** for the offer (p. 21), so market-size and growth statements are issuer-sourced. CARE puts the domestic farm-equipment market at Rs 1,397 bn in FY26 growing 7.7% a year to FY30, the tractor market at 7.5% and soil-preparation and cultivation implements at 10.2% (pp. 206, 209); the implements industry grew at a 10.2% CAGR over FY23-FY26 (p. 247). The market is fragmented between large listed tractor makers that also sell implements and many regional fabricators. The DRHP states there are no listed peers with a comparable portfolio (p. 156) and names VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment and Escorts Kubota as the nearest. Demand depends on monsoon and crop cycles, farm incomes, and state subsidy budgets, so revenue is seasonal; management extended vendor credit from October to January during peak season (p. 474). The company does not name its unlisted competitors.

## Moat

- **Brand and channel.** The DRHP describes the Jagatjit brand as active since the 1980s and a 245-partner dealer and distributor network (pp. 246, 158). Brand strength is asserted, not measured: no market-share figure is given beyond the issuer's claim of "leading market share across top three products" (p. 154).
- **Scale and integration.** Single-roof manufacturing and 23,875 units of installed capacity; the Dehlon expansion would take it to 52,875 (pp. 137, 266). Fabrication is not a durable barrier; the proforma 9.74% to 14.51% to 13.46% EBITDA margin sits at the level of the three listed peers (13.17% to 13.27%, p. 161).
- **Subsidy empanelment.** Manufacturer empanelment at state level is a licence-type barrier, but it is state-by-state and revocable (p. 56).
- **Evidence of a moat** would be margins persistently above peers, which the data do not show. The honest reading is a strong regional brand and distribution position with limited structural protection, durable to the extent the dealer relationships are.

## Revenue and profit growth drivers

- **Revenue.** Restated FY26 revenue is up Rs 3,180.44 m on FY25; the DRHP attributes the increase primarily to the acquired businesses (p. 473). Like-for-like (proforma), growth was Rs 1,602.10 m or 63.1%. Units sold were 19,111 against 12,234 proforma in FY25 (+56.2%), so most of the growth is volume rather than price: revenue per unit was Rs 216.6 thousand against Rs 207.4 thousand (+4.4%) (p. 158; our arithmetic). Domestic revenue was 98.61%; exports fell to Rs 56.33 m (p. 247).
- **Profit.** Proforma PAT rose 51.7% against revenue 63.1%. Gross margin rose from 37.19% to 39.06% but EBITDA margin fell from 14.51% to 13.46%: commission rose to Rs 151.23 m, contract labour to Rs 172.45 m and travel to Rs 80.77 m (pp. 474-475). Other income of Rs 35.03 m includes Rs 26.05 m of old creditor balances written back, 5.9% of PBT. Finance costs were Rs 101.63 m.
- **One-offs.** Penal charges of Rs 4.39 m and vendor interest of Rs 7.36 m in finance cost relate to the transition and peak-season credit (p. 474).

- **Working capital.** The plan holds bank borrowing flat at Rs 1,150 m for FY27 to FY29 and uses IPO funds of Rs 700 m in FY28 and Rs 400 m in FY29, with working capital need rising from Rs 1,029.46 m (FY26) to Rs 2,653.59 m (FY29) (pp. 126-127). On our arithmetic that implies revenue of roughly 2.5x to 2.8x FY26 by FY29, a CAGR of about 36% to 41%. Our Neutral case is 18% / 16% / 14% / 12% / 10%, 1.6x at FY29; the Bull is about 2.0x at FY29. The RHP should state the revenue projection behind the plan.
- **Capex.** Rs 716.64 m for 29,000 more units of capacity (about Rs 24,700 per unit) compares with FY26 PPE of Rs 516.63 m for 23,875 units (about Rs 21,600 per unit). **No orders have been placed for any of it** (100% of cost); quotations are valid nine months to one year; the Dehlon land is **leased from promoter Jagatjit Singh** (pp. 129, 139). Total installed capacity after expansion, 52,875 units, is 2.8x FY26 units sold (19,111).
- **Debt repayment.** Rs 185 m against Rs 1,641.44 m outstanding at 31-Aug-2026 (up from Rs 1,344.07 m at 31-Mar-2026); Rs 1,340.89 m carries promoter guarantees (pp. 40, 140). Repayment may release some guarantees, an indirect benefit to the promoters.
- **Offer for sale.** 3,100,000 shares, 6.3% of the selling shareholder's holding, 12.0% of the offer at the reference price, within the limits tested (R5).

The relative method prices FY26 earnings of Rs 31.39 cr at peer multiples, while the DCF prices cash: net working capital absorbs about 25% of each rupee of revenue and FY26 free cash flow is negative. The gap is the investment question: whether earnings convert to cash as working capital stabilises.
- **Terminal value** is above 75% of enterprise value in the Neutral case (guard flag): the DCF leans on the terminal year.
- **Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple can be stated, and the entire price at the reference rests on a turn in cash conversion that has not yet begun.
- **Post-money dilution.** If the Rs 3,000 m fresh issue were counted in full, FY26 PAT of Rs 313.90 m on equity of Rs 3,709.29 m would be a return of at most 8.5%, against the 53.89% shown pre-issue (p. 158). Promoter cost: Rs 5.40 (selling shareholder) and Rs 33.63 (Oct-2025 allotment) against the Rs 131.69 reference.
- The reference sits above all three blended values. That is a statement about the reference, which is ours; the actual band may differ materially.

**Supply overhang (at the reference price).** Promoter minimum contribution about 156.9 lakh shares locked for 18 months; promoter holding above it about 368.5 lakh shares for six months; non-promoter pre-issue shares 0.184 lakh for six months (pp. 111, 116-117). Anchor allotments are discretionary and locked in (half for 30 days, half for 90 days), so cannot be sold on listing day.

## What would change our view

Measurable against the RHP:
1. Audited or limited-review H1 FY27 financials show CFO above 50% of PAT and inventory days below 120.
2. The identity of Seth Agro Industries is disclosed with no promoter link, and its FY27 share of revenue is below 20%.
3. FY26 supplier concentration is disclosed and the top supplier is below 30% of purchases.
4. Orders are placed for at least half of the Rs 716.64 m capex, with the Dehlon lease terms and rent stated.
5. The RHP states the revenue projection behind the working-capital plan, and it is within the range of our Neutral and Bull cases (1.6x to 2.0x FY26 by FY29), or the price band is at or below our blended values (Rs 47.77 / 61.52 / 77.63).
6. The goodwill of Rs 4.55 m is reconciled to the Rs 324.57 m consideration and the proforma Rs 65.66 m.
7. State subsidy receivables outstanding are disclosed and are below 10% of receivables.
In the other direction: a price band above Rs 131.69 with the same financials, or a larger share of revenue from one counterparty.

## Verification

- **Restated statements reproduce.** FY26 expenses sum to Rs 3,730.62 m against Rs 3,730.61 m printed (rounding); PBT Rs 444.12 m = income Rs 4,174.73 m less expenses; PAT = PBT less tax Rs 130.21 m (Rs 313.91 m vs Rs 313.90 m printed, rounding). FY25 and FY24 expense lines sum exactly. Total assets Rs 3,188.36 m agree with total equity and liabilities. The FY26 balance sheet balances.
- **KPI EBITDA** Rs 557.38 m reproduces (444.12 + 101.63 + 46.66 - 35.03); FY25 and FY24 also reproduce (80.20 + 19.16 + 14.63 - 2.34 = 111.65; 38.32 + 21.18 + 9.47 - 2.31 = 66.66).
- **Cash flow.** FY26 net movement: 26.85 - 196.81 + 236.13 = 66.17, plus 0.34 opening and 2.15 acquired = 68.66 vs 68.67 printed (rounding).
- **EPS.** Rs 313.90 m / 55,650,984 shares = Rs 5.64 (reproduces); FY25 and FY24 EPS use smaller pre-allotment counts (see check 1).
- **Document inconsistencies, not extraction errors:** promoter-issue WACA (Rs 35.15 vs Rs 33.63 computed, p. 163); the objects-table payables vs the proforma balance sheet; goodwill under the restated and proforma bases. These are in the issuer's document as printed.
- **Not tested:** the image-only annexures were not needed because the text layer was readable; the investor presentation was not reviewed; the industry report was not read in full.
- **Outside sources** are used only in the merchant-banker section and are marked unverified.
- **Workbooks:** the toolkit verification result is recorded in the pull request.
