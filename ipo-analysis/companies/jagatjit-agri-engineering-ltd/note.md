# Jagatjit Agri Engineering Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 25-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **195 days old** at the date of this note (12-Oct-2026). **No price band has been announced.** Figures are in ₹ million unless stated; ₹ crore = ₹ million ÷ 10. Page numbers are the DRHP's printed folios. All figures are from the DRHP; nothing is taken from outside the document.
>
> **Comparability.** FY26 is consolidated. FY24 and FY25 are standalone and exclude the businesses of two proprietorships owned by the promoters (Saron Mechanical Works I and II), which the company acquired with effect from 1-Apr-2025 (pp. 301–302). The DRHP therefore also gives **proforma** FY24 and FY25 as if the acquisition had happened earlier (pp. 91–92). Growth in this note is stated on the proforma basis unless it says "restated".
>
> Upside is measured against **₹158.48**, a reference point built only from the DRHP: its industry-average P/E of 28.10x (p. 156) times restated FY26 basic EPS of ₹5.64 (p. 154). It is not a price band. The three peers are tractor and tiller makers. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The audited record is more than six months old, so no capital should be committed until the RHP, which must carry newer financials, has been read.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** fast, volume-led growth, an adequate cash tax rate and falling leverage.
- Proforma revenue grew from ₹2,074.48 m (FY24) to ₹2,537.60 m (FY25) and ₹4,139.70 m (FY26): +22.3% and +63.1% (pp. 92, 87). Units sold rose from 8,895 to 12,234 to 19,111; FY26 growth was 56.2% in units and 4.4% in revenue per unit (p. 158).
- EBITDA margin was 13.46% in FY26, against 14.51% proforma FY25 and 9.74% proforma FY24 (p. 158).
- Current tax was ₹130.30 m on PBT of ₹444.12 m in FY26, 29.3%, above the 25.17% statutory rate (p. 87).
- Interest coverage was 5.03x and debt to equity 1.89x in FY26, against 2.70x and 50.36x in FY24 (p. 158).

**Concerns:** cash generation, concentration and what the reference price implies.
- Operating cash flow was ₹26.85 m in FY26 against PAT of ₹313.90 m (8.6%). Cumulative CFO/PAT over FY24–FY26 is 69.7%, on mixed standalone and consolidated bases (p. 88).
- Super Seeder, Straw Reaper and Laser Land Leveller were 64.40% of FY26 revenue, and demand for them depends on the CRM and SMAM subsidy schemes (pp. 450, 56).
- The top customer was 25.21% and the top ten 50.04% of FY26 revenue (p. 36).
- At the ₹158.48 reference, post-issue P/E is 37.7x and P/B 3.3x; return on equity of 44.26% before the issue becomes about 8.8% on post-issue equity (pp. 155, 85; our calculation).
- Two promoter-directors were appointed while disqualified under the Companies Act; an adjudication application is pending (p. 489).

On a cash basis (DCF) value is below the reference in all three scenarios; on peer multiples it is also below it. **The price band, H1 FY27 financials and the RHP's reconciliation of the proforma and restated figures will show whether the reference is too high.**

## Business model

**What is sold.** Tractor-operated and tractor-mounted farm implements under the "Jagatjit" brand: 20+ products and 190+ SKUs, including Super Seeders, straw reapers, laser land levellers, rotavators, mulchers and briquette machines (pp. 446, 450). Revenue by product in FY26 was Super Seeder ₹1,365.60 m (32.99%), Straw Reaper ₹1,079.40 m (26.07%) and Laser Land Leveller ₹221.05 m (5.34%) (p. 450).

**To whom.** Farmers, mostly through a dealer and distributor network, plus more than 2,500 direct customers in FY26 (p. 260). Domestic customers were 98.61% of FY26 revenue; 20 states were served, against 15 in FY25 and 10 in FY24 (p. 158).

**How it is priced and why subsidies matter.** Farmers buy a large share of these implements under central and state schemes: the Crop Residue Management scheme and the Sub-Mission on Agricultural Mechanisation, funded roughly 60% by the Centre and 40% by states, with manufacturer empanelment managed state by state (p. 56). The issuer says it obtains approvals so farmers can use subsidies where available (p. 251). The DRHP does not state what share of sales is subsidy-linked.

**How it earns its margin.** Gross margin rose to 39.06% in FY26 from 37.19% proforma FY25 and 23.58% proforma FY24 (p. 158). Other expenses rose to 18.94% of revenue from 12.46% proforma FY25 (pp. 87, 92). The DRHP does not give the contents of "other expenses".

**Owned versus outsourced.** Two Punjab plants, Cheema (Sangrur) and Dehlon (Ludhiana), each able to make the whole range (p. 446). The Dehlon plant stands on 16.20 acres leased from Jagatjit Singh at ₹0.08 m per month, rising 15% every three years (p. 129). The trademarks (35) and three patents were assigned to the company by the promoters between Oct-2025 and Sep-2026 (p. 303).

## Industry analysis

- **Who paid.** The industry report is by CARE Analytics and Advisory, a subsidiary of CARE Ratings. It was commissioned and paid for by the company for this offer (p. 21). Treat its figures as the issuer's framing.
- **Size and growth.** Indian farm equipment: ₹1,397 bn in FY26, up from ₹769 bn in FY20 (10.5% CAGR), forecast at ₹1,876 bn by FY30, a 7.7% CAGR (p. 250).
- **Structure.** The report says small and marginal farms are about 86% of operational holdings, limiting affordability, and that demand follows monsoon, crop prices and rural income (CARE report, PDF p. 241).
- **Competitors.** The DRHP's listed peers are VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment and Escorts Kubota, and it states that no listed Indian peer has a comparable portfolio and model (p. 156). Competing implement makers were not identified in our reading; this is open.
- **Regulation and cyclicality.** Subsidy design (CRM, SMAM) and monsoon are the two external drivers named in the DRHP (pp. 56, 250).

## Moat

- **Brand and distribution.** The brand dates from 1994 and the group's machinery activity from the 1980s (p. 446). Estimated shares, from the issuer-commissioned report: about 13% by value in straw reapers nationally in FY26, and 30% / 15% / 12% of Super Seeder units deployed under subsidy programmes in Madhya Pradesh, Haryana and Punjab (p. 446). These are narrow measures; they do not give a share of total implements sales.
- **Subsidy empanelment.** Approval and empanelment can limit new entrants in a state, but the DRHP gives no count of empanelled rivals.
- **Switching costs.** None are described. Implements are bought per season and the issuer has no long-term supply commitments with customers (p. 36).
- **Intellectual property.** Three patents, 35 registered trademarks (p. 446). Patents were assigned to the company on 21-Sep-2026 (p. 303).
- **Read.** The evidence supports a regional brand and dealer position; it does not yet show pricing power. Gross margin expansion (23.6% to 39.1% proforma) is consistent with either mix or pricing, and the DRHP does not separate them.

FY24 to FY25: units +37.5%, revenue per unit −11.1% (₹233.2k to ₹207.4k) (pp. 92, 158; our calculation). Product contribution: Super Seeder +₹408.5 m and Straw Reaper +₹379.6 m between proforma FY25 and FY26, together 49% of the increase (p. 450).

**Profit (proforma).** EBITDA margin moved 14.51% to 13.46% (−1.05 pp) in FY26: gross margin +1.87 pp, employee cost −3.56 pp of revenue (10.22% to 6.66%) and other expenses +6.48 pp (12.46% to 18.94%) (pp. 87, 92, 158; our calculation). Proforma employee cost rose from ₹88.52 m to ₹259.35 m between FY24 and FY25; the DRHP does not explain the step. Promoters drew ₹60.00 m of remuneration in FY26 (₹30.00 m, ₹12.00 m and ₹18.00 m), nil in FY25 (p. 94).

**One-offs.** Other income was ₹35.03 m in FY26 (7.9% of PBT) (p. 87). The provision for expected credit loss was ₹34.90 m (p. 88).

## Valuation and reverse DCF

Reference: ₹158.48 (see basis). Financial inputs: FY26 consolidated, ₹413.97 cr revenue, ₹55.74 cr EBITDA, ₹31.39 cr PAT, net debt ₹119.42 cr (borrowings including leases less cash and bank balances), trade working capital 20.3% of revenue. Prior-year revenue for growth is the proforma ₹253.76 cr. Cost of capital 16.06% (CAPM build-up, house defaults), terminal growth 4.50%. House policy blend is 60:40; because the issuer identifies no comparable listed peer (p. 156) we used 80:20.

| ₹ per share | Bear | Neutral | Bull |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DCF | 36.63 | 52.10 | 68.07 |
| Relative (P/E and P/B, peer median less 20%) | 97.65 | 97.65 | 97.65 |
| Blend (80:20) | 48.84 | 61.21 | 73.98 |
| Against ₹158.48 | −69.2% | −61.4% | −53.3% |

- **DCF against relative differ by 46.6%** (Neutral). The relative method prices reported earnings and book value of three tractor makers; the DCF prices cash. Free cash flow is negative for three years (−₹18.7 cr, −₹29.3 cr, −₹5.1 cr in Y1–Y3) because working capital absorbs about 21% of each incremental rupee of revenue and capex runs ahead of depreciation. That gap is the finding.
- **Terminal value is 87.7% of enterprise value**, above the 75% guard. The DCF is mostly a terminal assumption.
- **Peers.** Per-share P/E (23.4x–33.3x) and P/B from the DRHP (p. 156); EV/EBITDA is not used because the DRHP gives the ratio but not net debt or EBITDA for each peer. The 20% discount is ours, for size, regional concentration and a single-year consolidated base. Peer RoNW is 4.4%–11.3%, against the issuer's 44.26% before the issue; on post-issue equity the issuer's return (about 8.8%) sits inside the peers' range.
- **Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple can be stated: the price rests on a turn that has not yet begun. Holding other inputs at actual levels, the steady-state EBIT margin required to reach ₹158.48 is 46.8% against 12.3% in FY26. A working-capital intensity that supports the price is unreachable. The growth and cost-of-capital levers cannot be solved while free cash flow is negative.
- **The reference is not a market price.** It applies the DRHP's peer-average P/E to pre-issue EPS. The only transaction price in the DRHP is ₹1,547 per ₹10 share (about ₹33.63 per ₹5 share after the bonus and split) for the shares issued to the promoters for their proprietorships; that is a non-cash, related-party valuation, not a market price (pp. 301–302).

## What would change our view

1. A price band at or below about ₹61 (the Neutral blend) would remove the valuation gap. A band above ₹158.48 widens it.
2. H1 FY27 operating cash flow above 60% of H1 PAT, with inventory days at or below 110 (against 132 in FY26).
3. A disclosed FY26 revenue and PAT on the proforma basis that reconciles to the consolidated figures, with the ₹88.52 m to ₹259.35 m rise in proforma employee cost explained.
4. Share of sales that is subsidy-linked disclosed, and top three products below 50% of revenue.
5. The goodwill difference (₹65.66 m proforma against ₹4.55 m restated) reconciled by the auditors, and an independent valuation of the promoter proprietorships or a statement of net assets acquired.
6. Repayment or terms of the ₹55.00 m promoter advances before listing.
7. Firm orders placed for at least half of the ₹716.64 m capex, with quotations that cover the FY28 schedule.
8. Disposal of the pending adjudication on the directors' disqualification without penalty above the materiality threshold of ₹6.74 m.

## Risks

- Subsidy dependence: the CRM and SMAM schemes can be reduced or discontinued (p. 56).
- Product concentration: three products are 64.40% of revenue (p. 450).
- Customer concentration: top customer 25.21% (p. 36); no long-term contracts.
- Single-year combined history: the two acquired businesses were run by the promoters as proprietorships until 31-Mar-2025 (pp. 301–302).
- Working capital intensity: inventory 132 days; the largest object is working capital (pp. 124, 128).
- Promoter control: promoters hold 99.97% before the offer and will remain the controlling shareholders (p. 112).
- Related parties: promoter-owned land, trademarks and patents, advances and continuing trade (pp. 95, 129, 303).
- Governance: directors served while disqualified (p. 489); no database-level audit trail up to Dec-2025 (p. 343).
- Supply: no long-term supplier agreements (p. 23); top supplier 7.94%, top ten 40.53%, against 96.69% in FY24 restated (p. 33).
- Monsoon and rural income cycles.
