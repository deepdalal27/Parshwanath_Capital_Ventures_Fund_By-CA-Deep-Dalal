Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 (FY26 consolidated; FY25 and FY24 standalone) and are **202 days old** at the date of this note (19-Oct-2026). **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in Rs million unless stated; Rs crore where marked. Outside the DRHP: the BRLM's ownership and one share price in Appendix B, from secondary sources and marked unverified.
>
> Upside is measured against an **illustrative Rs 158.48**: the DRHP's own listed-peer average P/E (28.10x, p. 155) applied to the DRHP's FY26 EPS (Rs 5.64, p. 154). It is not a price band and nobody has proposed it. Exit route assumed: 2-3 year hold. Grey market premium is not used.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The audited record is more than six months old, so no capital should be committed until the RHP, with a price band and newer financials, has been read.

## 1. Business of the company

- **What it sells:** tractor-operated agricultural implements: Super Seeders, Straw Reapers, Rotavators, Laser Land Levellers, briquette machines and about 15 other products, 192 SKUs in FY26 (pp. 246, 158). Top three products were Rs 2,666.06 m, 64.40% of FY26 revenue (p. 23).
- **Who buys it:** farmers, through 12 distributors and 233 dealers that account for 86.08% of FY26 revenue; direct customers 13.89% (p. 247). Exports were 1.36% of FY26 revenue (p. 248). Northern states 68.04% and Central states 25.95% of revenue (p. 23).
- **How it is priced and earns:** manufacturer's sale price to the trade; subsidy schemes (Crop Residue Management and the Sub-Mission on Agricultural Mechanisation, funded about 60% by the Centre and 40% by states) lower the farmer's net price (p. 56). Gross margin 39.06% and EBITDA margin 13.46% in FY26 (p. 158).
- **What it owns vs outsources:** two plants in Punjab, Cheema (Sangrur) and Dehlon (Ludhiana), with cutting, bending, fabrication, machining, painting and assembly in-house (p. 246). Raw materials and components are bought on short purchase orders with no long-term agreements (p. 23). The Dehlon land (16.20 acres) is leased from the promoter (p. 129).
- **Structure:** the business ran for three decades as two promoter proprietorships, Saron Mechanical Works I and II, and moved into the company from 1-Apr-2025 against 2,09,804 shares issued at Rs 1,547 per Rs 10 share (about Rs 324.6 m) (pp. 105, 247). A subsidiary, Jagatjit Energies (briquette and pellet machinery), was bought on 22-Jan-2026 and was 0.39% of FY26 revenue (pp. 456, 477).

## 2. Industry outlook

Source: "Industry Research Report on Agri Equipment Sector" by CARE Analytics and Advisory, dated 23-Sep-2026, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 245). Treat its estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** domestic farm equipment Rs 769 bn in FY20, Rs 1,397 bn in FY26 (10.5% CAGR), Rs 1,876 bn projected for FY30 (7.7% CAGR) (p. 205). The issuer's FY26 revenue is about 0.3% of that figure (our arithmetic). The implement segment grew 10.2% a year over FY23-26 (p. 248).
- **Structure:** moderately fragmented; organised makers about 60% of the market in FY26, the rest unorganised and regional (p. 221). The report profiles Tirth Agro (Shaktiman, revenue Rs 18,917 m in FY25), Maschio Gaspardo, KS Agriculture and Ecostan India (p. 241); the DRHP's listed proxies are VST Tillers, Indo Farm and Escorts Kubota (p. 156). The issuer says no listed company has a comparable portfolio (p. 156).
- **Regulation and policy:** demand depends on state-managed subsidy and empanelment under the CRM and SMAM schemes; the DRHP says reduction or discontinuance of subsidies could reduce demand (p. 56).
- **Cyclicality:** seasonal, with lower revenue in the fourth quarter of each fiscal (p. 34); crop-residue machines also follow state policy on stubble management.
- **Share:** about 13% of the Straw Reaper market by value in FY26; Super Seeder share 30% in Madhya Pradesh, 15% in Haryana and 12% in Punjab by subsidised units (issuer-paid report, p. 246).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Brand and distribution:** the 'Jagatjit' brand since the 1980s, 233 dealers and 12 distributors across 20 states (pp. 246-247).
- **Product breadth and in-house manufacturing:** 20 products on shared lines lets the plants switch capacity between products by season and region (p. 246).
- **Regulatory empanelment:** state-level manufacturer empanelment under subsidy schemes is a hurdle for new entrants, shared with other empanelled makers (p. 56).
- **Durability:** moderate. The advantages are brand, channel and execution, not patents (three patents, p. 246). Dealers are partly on letters of intent rather than agreements (p. 23).

## 4. Management analysis and governance

- **Promoters:** Dharam Singh (78, Chairman and whole-time director), his son Jagatjit Singh (44, Managing Director) and Simran Saron (38, whole-time director) (pp. 312-313). Dharam Singh built the business from the 1980s; Jagatjit Singh leads it (p. 246).
- **Board:** six directors: three executive (all promoters) and three independent, Jitendra Pratap Singh, Sudha Laxminarayan Singh and Nimesh Pandya, one of whom, Jitendra Pratap Singh, joined on 10-Feb-2026 (pp. 312-314). The CFO is Rajesh Malik (p. 94).
- **Pay:** FY26 remuneration Rs 30.00 m (Jagatjit Singh), Rs 18.00 m (Dharam Singh) and Rs 12.00 m (Simran Saron), Rs 60.00 m together: 1.4% of revenue and 19.1% of PAT (p. 94).
- **Shareholding and the offer:** promoters and group hold 99.99% before the offer: Jagatjit Singh 88.03%, Simran Saron 8.25%, Dharam Singh 3.69% (pp. 109-110). Jagatjit Singh sells up to 31.00 lakh shares (6.3% of his holding) at a weighted average cost of Rs 5.40 a share (cover, p. 82). A bonus of 22:1 was issued on 27-May-2026 (p. 105).
- **Related parties:** the proprietorships were the company's main counterparties until FY25: in the standalone FY25 accounts they supplied Rs 419.72 m of Rs 735.68 m cost of materials and bought Rs 218.22 m of Rs 959.26 m revenue (pp. 95, 87). In FY26 related-party purchases were Rs 164.91 m (6.5% of cost of materials) (p. 94). Advances to promoters stood at Rs 45.00 m (Jagatjit Singh) and Rs 10.00 m (Dharam Singh) at 31-Mar-2026, and the Dehlon land is leased from Jagatjit Singh at Rs 0.08 m a month (pp. 95, 129).
- **Other group interests:** Goodfield Technology Pvt Ltd, a group company, has common pursuits with the issuer and is also a customer and supplier (Rs 5.46 m sales, Rs 6.24 m purchases in FY26) (pp. 24, 94).
- **Litigation:** no criminal proceedings against the company. Two promoters, Jagatjit Singh and Dharam Singh, were appointed directors on 29-Sep-2020 while a disqualification arising from Jaggatjit Agro Pvt Ltd ran to 31-Oct-2021; the company attributes this to conflicting MCA records, filed an adjudication application on 11-Sep-2026 and the matter is pending (p. 489). The company is the complainant in cheque-dishonour cases against a crane supplier, Rs 2.56 m in the first (p. 489). Materiality threshold Rs 6.74 m (p. 488).
- **Auditor:** Anup Kumar Jain & Co (FRN 005731N). Database-level audit trail was not enabled in the accounting software until January 2026 (pp. 52, 482).

**Governance read.** The company is promoter-run, as is usual for a business that began as a family firm, and half the board is independent. Points to monitor are the disqualification matter (a past, adjudication-pending item), the scale of dealings with promoter-linked parties before FY26, advances to promoters, and the promoter-owned land under the expansion.

- **Why the two methods differ:** peer multiples price FY26 earnings of Rs 31.39 cr as the market prices listed tractor makers (Rs 122 a share, on post-issue shares). The DCF prices cash: free cash flow is negative until the expansion and the working-capital build are paid for, and the model grows revenue at 15% falling to 10% against an FY26 base that already includes a 63% step-up (Rs 64).
- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF, Rs per share):** WACC 12.4% to 16.4% moves it from Rs 78 to Rs 55; working capital at 17.4% of revenue with a 14.6% margin gives Rs 80, and 29.4% with 10.6% gives Rs 49. Margin is the larger lever.
- **Reverse DCF at Rs 158.48:** FY26 free cash flow was negative (-Rs 26.39 cr), so no cash-flow multiple can be stated; the price assumes cash generation improves from here. Holding today's working-capital level, a steady-state EBIT margin of about 40.8% would be needed against 12.3% actual, and no working-capital level reaches the price on its own. This says the illustrative price needs revenue to keep compounding above our Neutral path, not that it cannot be reached.

## 9. Conclusion

**Screen only: no price band yet.** For reference, the call rule in the Fund's standing instructions would give **Avoid** at the illustrative Rs 158.48: the Neutral blended value (Rs 93.09) is more than 30% below it and the Bull value (Rs 110.22) is also below it. No material issue was found. The illustrative price is generous, being the DRHP's peer average applied to a pre-issue EPS, so a lower band would change the answer.

**The investment case.**
- The business scaled quickly: pro forma revenue +63.1% and units +56.2% in FY26, EBITDA margin 13.46%, RoCE 31.10% (pp. 92, 158).
- It sells a branded range through 245 dealers and distributors in 20 states, with in-house manufacturing in two Punjab plants (pp. 246-247).
- Tax is paid in cash (29.3% of PBT) and interest cover is 5.03x (pp. 87, 158).

**What holds value back at the illustrative price.**
- FY26 profit did not turn into cash: CFO was 8.6% of PAT as inventory and other assets grew (p. 88).
- The earnings base is one year long. FY24-25 standalone numbers were mostly dealings with the promoters' proprietorships (p. 95), so the trend rests on pro forma figures.
- At Rs 158.48 the post-issue P/E is 37.7x against a 27.6x peer median, while the DCF is Rs 64 on three-year average margins.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 80.78 / **Rs 93.09** / Rs 110.22 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), -41.3% against Rs 158.48 in the Neutral case. **Call rule:** limb two of the Avoid test (Neutral more than 30% below the price and Bull also below it) would apply at this price; there is no price band, so the published call stays Screen only.

**What decides it:** the price band relative to roughly Rs 103-133, and whether H1 FY27 results show cash conversion improving.
