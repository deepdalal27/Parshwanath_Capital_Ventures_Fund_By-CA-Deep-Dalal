Page numbers are the DRHP's printed pages; amounts are in ₹ million unless stated (₹ crore in the model).
>
> Upside is measured against **₹155.78**, an assumption: FY26 basic EPS ₹5.64 x 27.62x, the median P/E of the three listed peers the issuer names (pp. 154-156). It is a reference point, not a price band. The only priced issue, the Oct-2025 allotment against the promoters' businesses, was at ₹33.63 after bonus and split and was paid in kind, so it is not used (pp. 163, 388). ## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths**
- Proforma revenue rose from ₹2,074.48 m (FY24) to ₹2,537.60 m (FY25) and ₹4,139.70 m (FY26), a 41.3% CAGR; the industry grew 10.2% a year over FY23-FY26 per the issuer-commissioned report (pp. 92, 248).
- EBITDA margin 13.46% in FY26 against 14.51% (proforma FY25) and 9.74% (proforma FY24); ROCE 31.1%; interest cover 5.03x (p. 158).
- Cash tax of 26.1% of cumulative PBT, above the statutory rate (p. 87).
- Supplier concentration fell: top 10 suppliers 40.53% of FY26 purchases against 76.68% in FY25 (p. 39).

**Points to monitor**
- Seth Agro Industries was 25.21% of FY26 revenue (₹1,043.46 m); top 10 customers 50.04% (p. 261).
- FY26 operating cash flow was ₹26.85 m against PAT of ₹313.90 m; finished goods rose from ₹19.26 m to ₹432.33 m (pp. 88, 474).
- Net debt ₹1,194 m (2.14x EBITDA); borrowings reached ₹1,641 m by 31-Aug-2026 (pp. 85, 140).
- The offer is made under Reg 6(2) because net worth and net tangible assets did not meet Reg 6(1) in FY24 (p. 57).

At the ₹155.78 reference the blended Neutral value is ₹95.28 (-38.8%) and the Bull value ₹108.06 (-30.6%). The gap is mostly the difference between what the market pays peers for earnings and what the cash flows of a business still absorbing working capital support. **The RHP price band and H1 FY27 cash flow decide it.**

## 1. Business of the company

Jagatjit makes tractor-mounted and tractor-operated farm implements: Super Seeders, straw reapers, rotavators, laser land levellers, briquette and pellet machines, plus threshers, harvesters and a tubewell boring machine. It lists 20 products and 192 SKUs (pp. 248-249). CARE ranks it the third-largest homegrown implement maker by FY26 revenue and puts its Straw Reaper value share near 13% (pp. 245-246). Two plants in Punjab, Cheema (Sangrur) and Dehlon (Ludhiana), do cutting, bending, fabrication, machining, painting and assembly in-house; raw material is steel and bought-out components such as gearboxes (pp. 246, 473).

Sales run through 233 dealers and 12 distributors (86.08% of FY26 revenue), plus direct customers (13.89%) (p. 247). The distributor share rose from 12.61% to 46.16% of revenue between FY24 and FY26 (p. 247). End demand is farmer-led and partly subsidised under the Crop Residue Management and SMAM schemes, funded about 60% by the Centre and 40% by states; empanelment is a barrier to entry and state payments have been late in the past (pp. 56-57). Northern states were 68.04% of FY26 revenue, Central 25.95% (p. 29).

**How the company came to be in this shape.** The Jagatjit brand traded for three decades through two promoter proprietorships, Saron Mechanical Works I (Dharam Singh) and II (Jagatjit Singh). The company, incorporated in 2020, was a small supplier to them. On 27-Aug-2025 it bought both businesses on a slump-sale basis, effective 1-Apr-2025, at carrying values: net assets of ₹320.03 m for ₹324.57 m paid in shares at ₹1,547 per ₹10 share (pp. 387-388). That is why restated revenue jumped from ₹959.26 m (FY25) to ₹4,139.70 m (FY26), and why the proforma statements, not the restated ones, are the right base for growth (p. 92).

**Stage of the business: scaling.** A 40-year brand and a very recent corporate structure. The record is real but short as a listed-company entity: the audit trail in the accounting software only began in January 2026 (p. 416).

## 2. Industry outlook

The CARE report, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** (p. 245), sizes the Indian farm equipment market at ₹1,397 bn in FY26, up from ₹769 bn in FY20 (10.5% CAGR), and expects ₹1,876 bn by FY30 (7.7% CAGR) (p. 250). It sees mechanisation moving from a high-growth adoption phase to a steadier, replacement-and-precision phase. Biomass briquette and pellet equipment is a separate, smaller opportunity.

Structure: the listed names the issuer benchmarks against are tractor makers (Escorts Kubota, VST Tillers Tractors, Indo Farm Equipment) which the document itself says have no directly comparable product portfolio (p. 156). Implement makers such as Tirth Agro (Shaktiman) are private (p. 241). Cyclicality: kharif and rabi seasons, monsoon, MSP and subsidy timing; steel is the main cost driver and moves quarterly margin (p. 220). Regulation and subsidy policy sit with state governments (p. 56).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

The moat is **brand and distribution, with modest technology**. Evidence: a 233-dealer, 12-distributor network across 20 states (up from 10 in FY24) (pp. 247-248), subsidy empanelment that non-empanelled makers lack (p. 57), 46 R&D staff and three patents, acquired from the promoters by deed of assignment (pp. 246, 54). Against that, R&D spend is 0.34% of revenue (p. 246) and implements are a low-barrier category; switching costs for a farmer are low. Durability is therefore moderate and depends on dealer loyalty and subsidy empanelment.

## 4. Management analysis and governance

**Promoters:** Dharam Singh (Chairman and Whole-time Director, the founder's generation of the brand), Jagatjit Singh (Managing Director, second generation) and Simran Saron (Whole-time Director) (pp. 96-97, 246). The three executive directors share one residential address, and the company is promoter-run. Three independent directors sit on the board (p. 97).

**Shareholding.** The promoter group holds 99.99% before the offer: 55,646,384 of 55,650,984 shares; one public holder has 4,600 shares (p. 108). Jagatjit Singh holds 48.99 m shares at an average cost of ₹5.40, Dharam Singh 2.05 m at ₹33.70, Simran Saron 4.59 m at ₹0.22; the three-year weighted average cost is ₹5.94 (p. 119). He sells up to 3.10 m shares (6.3% of his holding) in the offer for sale. At the reference price that is about ₹48.3 cr, 13.9% of a combined fresh-plus-OFS offer of about ₹348.3 cr.

**Pay.** Promoter remuneration is ₹60.00 m in FY26 (Jagatjit Singh 30.00, Dharam Singh 18.00, Simran Saron 12.00) and nil in earlier years, 19.1% of PAT and 21.8% of employee cost (pp. 94, 87). This is within the range for a promoter-managed company of this size; it is a new charge in FY26 and a base for FY27.

**Related parties (p. 94-95).** FY26 purchases from related firms were ₹164.91 m (Expert International 83.42, G9 International 42.65, A.P.U Traders 32.60, Goodfield 6.24), 6.5% of materials consumed; sales to them ₹69.99 m (1.7% of revenue), mostly Guru Nanak Agriculture Implements (₹63.43 m, receivable ₹49.09 m = 8.0% of trade receivables). Loans and advances to promoters of ₹55.00 m were outstanding at 31-Mar-2026, and borrowings from promoters and their family were ₹8.17 m. The Dehlon land (16.20 acres of a 30-acre promoter parcel) is leased from Jagatjit Singh at ₹0.08 m a month to 2055 (pp. 129, 138).

**Compliance record.** Dharam Singh and Jagatjit Singh were disqualified as directors from 29-Sep-2020 to 31-Oct-2021 through a struck-off promoter company that had not filed returns; compounding applications are filed and no penalty has been imposed (p. 37). Other late RoC filings have adjudication applications pending (p. 38). **Litigation:** eight tax proceedings and one regulatory matter against the company, ₹27.71 m in total; two criminal and one civil matter by the company, ₹14.56 m; none against promoters or directors (p. 54). Contingent liabilities ₹71.70 m, 10.1% of net worth (p. 93).

**Auditors.** Anup Kumar Jain & Co since 30-Sep-2024, after a resignation (Singla Gupta, 23-Apr-2024) and a short appointment of Kansal Sandeep & Associates (2-May to 30-Sep-2024) (p. 98). FY24 and FY25 are special-purpose Ind AS statements; the earlier auditor did not have a peer-review certificate and so did not perform the restatement (p. 343). No qualification or emphasis of matter.

**Governance read:** a family-run company with a clear business, a clean audit opinion and a short corporate history, whose governance infrastructure (audit trail, independent board, committees) is new. The record on disqualification and filings is historical and being regularised. Nothing here meets the material-issue test; the points are for the RHP to settle.

### Is the reported P/E right?

Largely yes. The one adjustment found is ₹26.05 m of sundry creditor balances written back into other income in FY26 (5.9% of PBT; p. 473). Taking it out lowers FY26 PAT by 6.2%, within the 10% threshold. On the post-issue share count (7.49 cr shares) the P/E at the reference is 37.2x reported and 39.6x adjusted. Items tested and found clean: the credit-loss charge (₹34.90 m), vendor-credit interest and bank penalties (₹11.75 m), promoter pay (charged in full), the promoter lease rent (charged; a market rent is not disclosed) and deferred tax (negligible). The FY24-26 average P/E (86.6x reported) is not meaningful because the business changed scale in FY26.

## 6. Future outlook and projections

**What management says.** The expansion adds a fabrication unit, utilities and an R&D unit at Dehlon, taking capacity from 4,000 to 33,000 units; civil work from April 2027, trial production in February 2028 and commercial production on 1-Mar-2028 (pp. 137, 143). Group capacity (Cheema 19,875 plus Dehlon 4,000 = 23,875 units) ran at about 76% in FY26 (18,219 units produced; p. 267); after expansion it would be 52,875 units, and FY26's output would fill about 34% of it. Capex orders for ₹716.64 m, 100% of the project, are **not yet placed** and quotations are valid one year (pp. 129, 139). Order book data is not given.

**What the working-capital plan implies.** The Board-approved plan holds receivables at 45 days of revenue and inventory at 130 days (p. 127). Receivables of ₹946.42 m, ₹1,150.88 m and ₹1,681.64 m at March 2027, 2028 and 2029 therefore imply revenue of roughly ₹7.7 bn, ₹9.3 bn and ₹13.6 bn (our arithmetic), against ₹4.14 bn in FY26: about 85% growth in FY27 and a 48% CAGR to FY29. That is above even our Bull case.

**Why DCF and relative differ (-43%).** The relative method prices FY26 earnings at peer multiples; the DCF prices the cash the business is expected to release, and here those have separated: FY26 earnings of ₹31.4 cr against negative operating free cash flow. The terminal value is 94.9% of enterprise value, so the DCF is mostly a view on the long-run margin and returns, not on the next three years. Both statements are true; the RHP's cash flow decides which reading earns more weight.

**Reverse DCF.** Current free cash flow is negative, so no multiple of it can be stated: the price assumes cash generation improves from here. Holding growth at the Neutral path, justifying ₹155.78 needs a steady-state EBIT margin of about 38.7% against 12.3% achieved, and it is unreachable through working capital alone (the 20.3% NWC intensity would have to turn negative). A lower price changes this: at roughly ₹106 or below the Neutral value would be within 10% of the price.

**Objects floor.** None: the fresh issue is amount-based, so no share count fixes a floor price.

**Growth drivers to track:** Super Seeder and straw reaper volumes in subsidy states; dealer and distributor additions (233 and 12 now); the Dehlon build against the March 2028 date; biomass machine sales; export recovery (1.36% of revenue).

**Points to monitor** (normal-course items, each with its number):
- Customer concentration: Seth Agro 25.21% of FY26 revenue (3.85% in FY24); the DRHP does not describe the relationship (p. 261).
- Subsidy exposure and payment delays (pp. 56-57); seasonality with a weak Q4 (p. 34).

- Micro and small enterprise payables ₹424.14 m, 46.5% of trade payables; vendor credit interest ₹7.36 m (pp. 85, 474).

- Offer under Reg 6(2): at least 75% of the offer to QIBs or it fails (p. 57); general corporate purposes up to 25% of gross proceeds, to be checked against the ICDR cap in force at the RHP (p. 124).
- Promoter sells 3.10 m shares; the fresh issue carries ₹998 m not yet allocated to a named object (offer expenses and general corporate purposes).

**What would change our view (measurable):**
1. A price band at or below about ₹106 (Neutral value within 10% of price) would move the rule to *Subscribe*; ₹106-136 to *Neutral*; above about ₹136 the Neutral value is more than 30% below the price and the Bull value (₹108) is also below it, which is the *Avoid* limb.
2. H1 FY27 CFO/PAT above 70% with finished goods stock falling from ₹432 m.
3. Seth Agro's share of revenue and its relationship to the promoters disclosed; share below 20%.
4. Placed capex orders and a monitoring agency named in the RHP.
5. The RHP carrying audited statements to a date within six months of filing, with proforma cash flow.

## 9. Conclusion

**Call: Screen only.** There is no price band, so the call rule cannot be applied to a price. The valuation was struck at the ₹155.78 reference. At that price the rule's *Avoid* limb would apply (Neutral ₹95.28 is 38.8% below, and Bull ₹108.06 is also below), driven by the DCF; no issue in the DRHP is material under the Fund's definition, so the call would rest on valuation alone, not on the accounts.

**Fair value range:** ₹82 (Bear) to ₹108 (Bull), Neutral ₹95, on the blended method.

**What decides it:** (1) the price band relative to about ₹106-136; (2) whether the FY26 stock build converts to cash in H1 FY27. The business growth, margins and returns are real on the proforma figures; the open question is how much of the peer multiple the market will extend to a 40-year brand in its first year as a single company.
