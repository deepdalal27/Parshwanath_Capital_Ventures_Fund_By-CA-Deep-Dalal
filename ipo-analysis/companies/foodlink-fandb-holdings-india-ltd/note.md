## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** a Mumbai-based luxury food-services group founded and run by Sanjay Manohar Vazirani (pp. 175, 230). It has three businesses (FY26 share of revenue, p. 26):
  - **Events catering, 52.89%:** ultra-luxury and luxury weddings, MICE, sports and personal celebrations in India and abroad. It catered 1,092 events in FY26 (p. 177).
  - **Casual dining restaurants and cloud kitchens, 24.17%:** brands China Bistro, India Bistro, Glocal and Art of Dum. It ran 21 restaurants and 13 cloud kitchens at Mar-26 (17 and 11 at Sep-26) (pp. 25, 177).
  - **Banquets and integrated F&B services, 22.51%:** three own banquets (Mumbai, Ahmedabad, New Delhi) and exclusive F&B rights at other venues. It had 6 venues and 1,089 events in FY26 (p. 177).
- **Kitchens:** seven centralised kitchens, six in India (Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Mohali, Hyderabad, Jaipur, New Delhi) and one in Dubai. Together they cover 91,500 sq ft with capacity for over 100,000 meals a day (pp. 151-152).
- **Geography:** 26.3% of FY26 revenue came from outside India, mainly the UAE. The company also operates in the UK and Malaysia and has catered events in 15 countries (pp. 175, 176).
- **Seasonality:** the October-March wedding season brings 63-70% of annual revenue. The first half (April-September) is usually loss-making: H1 FY27 PAT was Rs -16.15 cr (pp. 50, 90).
- **Size:** about 1,423 employees on the provident fund rolls at Mar-26 (p. 58).

## 2. Industry outlook

- **Market size:** India's organised wedding catering market is estimated at **Rs 1,24,026-2,03,732 cr in FY26**. It is projected to grow about **12% a year to FY30** (F&S report, p. 205).
- **The company's niche:** luxury and ultra-luxury wedding catering is put at Rs 38,700-82,350 cr in FY26, 31-40% of the wedding catering market (p. 152).
- **Restaurants:** cloud kitchens and cafés are the fastest-growing foodservice formats (pp. 203-204). The casual-dining segment is crowded, which shows in the company's own closures.
- **Drivers:** rising wealth (high-net-worth households and billionaires growing at 5-9% a year, p. 196), destination weddings and the government's "Wed in India" push (p. 205).
- **Risks:**
  - Event spending is discretionary and seasonal.
  - The UAE business was disrupted by the regional conflict in Q4 FY26, through cancelled and postponed events and higher logistics costs (p. 47).
- **Source caveat:** the industry report was commissioned and paid for by the company (p. 1).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

**Narrow: brand and execution, not structure.**
- **Brand and reputation:** the company calls itself India's largest organised ultra-luxury and luxury caterer by FY25 revenue among its peers (p. 175). In events, repeat referrals and the ability to run large destination weddings abroad matter.
- **Centralised kitchens** give consistency and some cost advantage across cities.
- **Limits:**
  - There are no long-term contracts with event hosts.
  - Every wedding is won one at a time.
  - Restaurants face low switching costs.
  - The promoter's personal standing is central to the brand.

### Is the reported P/E right?

**Verdict: roughly yes on the items found, but the tax charge flatters it.** On the model's fully diluted 6.40 crore shares, the reported FY26 P/E is 42.0x. After the small one-offs it is 41.4x:
- the FY26 exceptional charge of Rs 1.07 cr (p. 90), added back;
- old payables written back to profit, Rs 0.51 cr in FY26 (p. 91), taken out.

Those two items move PAT by about 1%.

- **For information, the tax charge:** FY26 tax was a credit of Rs 0.11 cr on PBT of Rs 29.00 cr, because earlier losses absorbed taxable profit (p. 90). **At a normal 25.17% rate the P/E would be 56.4x.** We keep the reported tax in the adjusted figure because the saving is real cash while the losses last. A buyer for 2-3 years should expect the normal-tax figure to apply soon.
- **On the FY24-26 average:** PAT averages Rs 9.3 cr because FY24 was a loss, giving a P/E above 130x. The track record of profit is short.
- **Checked, no adjustment:**
  - Share-based payment (Rs 1.56 cr) is a real cost and stays in.
  - No capitalised interest was found.
  - Interest income is small (Rs 1.23 cr).
  - The H1 FY27 loss is seasonal and is not annualised.

### Positives and points to monitor

- **Positives:**
  - Revenue grew 23% a year over FY24-26.
  - Margin after leases rose from 2.7% to 9.3%.
  - Operating cash flow was positive in every year and Rs 44.16 cr in H1 FY27 despite the seasonal loss.
  - The balance sheet holds net cash once leases are excluded.
- **To monitor:**
  - Thin margins: PAT is 5% of revenue.
  - About Rs 26 cr a year of lease rentals is a fixed cost against seasonal revenue.
  - Receivables doubled to Rs 60.05 cr in FY26.
  - The Income Tax block assessment is pending.
  - The tax shield will end.

## 6. Future outlook and projections

- **Base case (Neutral):**
  - **Revenue:** growth of 17% in FY27, fading to 10% by FY31. That is a little below the FY24-26 pace and close to the 18% rise in H1 FY27 over H1 FY26.
  - **Margin:** EBITDA after lease rentals of 10% (FY26: 9.3%).
  - **New capacity:** the IPO-funded venues arrive in FY28 (all deployment is scheduled for Fiscal 2028, p. 151).
- **What could do better (Bull):**
  - Faster growth, 24% fading to 12%, as new banquets fill and the UAE recovers.
  - Margin of 12% as kitchens and banquets gain scale.
- **What could do worse (Bear):**
  - Growth of 6-8% if luxury wedding spending slows, UAE disruption lasts or restaurants keep closing.
  - Margin of 8% (the FY25 level).
  - In the Bear case the IPO projects are not credited.
- **Execution risk on the objects:**
  - No locations have been identified for the new kitchen (Rajasthan), the banquets (Rajasthan and Bengaluru) or the four UAE restaurants.
  - No orders have been placed (pp. 22-24).

## 7. Valuation: DCF and peer comparison (P2P)

### Peer comparison (P2P)

**No listed peer is reported from the offer document.** The RHP states that there are no listed companies in India or globally with a similar business model. It gives no industry P/E (p. 176). The relative method is therefore not used, and fair value is the DCF alone (as for other no-peer issuers on this desk).

For context only, two reference points:
- the company's own **recent private share issue at Rs 363** (p. 329);
- **FY26 P/E of 40.9x** (about 55x at a normal tax rate) on a business with a short profit record.

### DCF and fair value

**Inputs (FY26; Rs crore):**
- Revenue 567.50 and **EBITDA after lease rentals 52.57**.
- D&A 19.51 (PPE and intangibles only; right-of-use depreciation of Rs 21.51 cr excluded).
- PAT 29.11, net worth 229.35.
- Net debt **-4.14**: borrowings 24.29 less cash and bank balances 28.43. Lease liabilities of Rs 103.68 cr are excluded, because rentals are charged in EBITDA (pp. 88-92, 339).

**Treatment of the issue and capex:**
- **Fresh issue:** credited net of 8% assumed expenses (Rs 176.64 cr), because the RHP prints the expenses as [●].
- **IPO capex:** Rs 113.71 cr, placed in year 2 (FY28) as the RHP schedules it.
- **Organic capex:** D&A plus 9.5% of each rupee of revenue growth, from FY25. FY25 had no new venue; FY26 capex of Rs 68.77 cr included three new venues, the same kind of spend the IPO now funds.
- **OFS:** it does not change the share count. The 16.05 lakh ESOPs at Rs 149.34 are included in the diluted count (6.40 crore).

**Cost of capital (house CAPM):**
- Risk-free 6.5%, beta 1.10, ERP 7.0%, size premium 2.0%: cost of equity 16.2%.
- Pre-tax cost of debt 9.5%; debt weight 20%.
- **WACC 14.38%**; terminal growth 4.50%; terminal ROIC 15%.
- Terminal value is 97.5% of enterprise value, because the explicit years carry the IPO capex. **The DCF is mostly a terminal estimate.**

| DCF (= fair value; no peers) | 32 | 60 | 90 |

- **Scenario drivers:**
  - Growth: Bear 6-8%, Neutral 10-17%, Bull 12-24% a year.
  - EBITDA margin after lease rentals: 8% / 10% / 12%.
  - Working capital: 5% / 3.5% / 2% of revenue.
  - Terminal ROIC: 12% / 15% / 18%.
- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF):**
  - WACC 12.4-16.4% gives Rs 71-52.
  - Margin 8-12% gives Rs 44-76.
  - Working capital from -2.5% to 9.5% of revenue gives Rs 67-52.
  - **No plausible single input bridges the gap to Rs 191.** Each point of margin is worth about Rs 8 a share, so the price implies a margin after rentals in the mid-20s, more than double the best year so far.
- **Objects floor:** not applicable; the fresh issue is amount-based.
- **Reverse DCF:** current free cash flow is negative (FY26 capex exceeded cash generation), so no multiple of it can be stated. **In plain words: the price assumes margins and cash generation far above anything the company has shown.**
- **Why the gap is so wide:**
  - The market cap of Rs 1,191 cr is set against EBITDA after rentals of Rs 53 cr and a near-untaxed PAT of Rs 29 cr.
  - A cash-flow valuation at a 14% cost of capital cannot reach the price unless profitability steps up sharply.
  - The private round at Rs 363 sets a reference that this valuation does not support.

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

### Growth drivers to track

- Occupancy and event count at the two new banquets once locations are fixed (none identified yet, p. 22).
- Recovery of UAE events and the four new Dubai and Abu Dhabi restaurants (p. 47).
- Margin after lease rentals: FY26 9.3%. Above 12% would support the Bull case.
- Restaurant closures: 4 restaurants and 2 cloud kitchens closed in H1 FY27 (p. 25).

### Points to monitor

- The Income Tax block assessment under section 158BC (p. 45).
- The GST appeal and the remaining show-cause amounts (p. 45).
- The tax rate as earlier losses are used up (p. 90).
- Receivables, Rs 60.05 cr at Mar-26 (p. 88).
- Six-month lock-in expiry for the pre-IPO investors and the promoters' surplus shares (about 3.10 crore shares, around 50% of post-offer capital) in June-2027.

### What would change our view

- **Upward:**
  - A clean outcome of the tax assessment.
  - Two full years with margins after rentals above 12% and a normal tax charge.
  - Evidence that the new banquets earn their cost within two years.
- **Downward:**
  - An adverse tax order.
  - Further restaurant closures.
  - A weak wedding season (H2 FY27).

### Risks

- **Discretionary demand:** luxury weddings and events are cyclical and seasonal (p. 50).
- **Fixed costs:** leases and staff do not flex with seasonal revenue (p. 38).
- **Execution:** the IPO projects have no sites or orders yet (pp. 22-24).
- **Regulatory and tax:** search proceedings, GST demands, food-safety licences (pp. 32, 45).
- **Key person:** the brand rests on the promoter (p. 175).
- **Allocation structure:** under Reg 6(2), retail gets at most 10% of the offer (p. 442).

## 9. Conclusion

**Avoid at Rs 191 (upper band).** The band was confirmed by the company on 23-Nov-2026. The call is the same at the Rs 181 floor.

**The investment case.**
- A recognised name in Indian luxury catering with three complementary businesses.
- Revenue grew 23% a year.
- The company was profitable in FY25-26 and generates operating cash.
- The issue funds visible venue expansion and leaves the company with net cash.

**What holds the value down.**
- Profit is thin and recent: a loss in FY24, a seasonal loss every first half, and PAT of 5% of revenue.
- FY26 profit carried almost no tax.
- At Rs 191 the price is about 41x reported and about 55x normal-tax earnings.
- An Income Tax search with an open block assessment is a material unresolved matter.
- The IPO projects have no locations yet.
- The band sits 47% below the Rs 363 at which shares were sold to investors in FY25-26, which the RHP does not explain.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 32 / **Rs 60** / Rs 90 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), DCF only, as there are no listed peers.

**Call rule.** At Rs 191 the Neutral value is 68.8% below the upper band and the Bull value is also below it, so the call is **Avoid**. The gap is wide enough that reasonable changes to growth, margin or the discount rate do not alter it.
