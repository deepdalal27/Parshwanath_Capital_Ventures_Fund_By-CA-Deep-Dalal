## 1. Business of the company

- **What it makes:** custom-formulated polymer compounds (127 grades): polyethylene compounds (HDPE, MDPE, LLDPE), cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE), halogen-free flame-retardant (HFFR), PVC, cable-filling and adhesive coating compounds (pp. 123, 247-248). They are used to insulate and sheath power, telecom and railway cables, and as layers of the three-layer polyethylene coating that protects oil, gas and water pipelines.
- **Who buys it:** cable makers and pipe coaters. FY26 revenue by division: wires and cables 80.93%, industrial pipelines 19.07%; by end-industry: telecom 35.10%, water 16.93%, power 14.00%, oil and gas 2.14%, others 31.83% (p. 248). Top-10 customers were 66.81% of FY26 revenue and the largest 31.74% (p. 27). Exports were 5.46% of FY26 revenue (p. 46).
- **How it earns:** manufacturing margin on compounds, plus trading of polyethylene resin. Trading ("procurement and supply" of HDPE and LLDPE resin) was Rs 41.00 cr / Rs 85.72 cr / Rs 111.32 cr FY24-26, or 13.83% / 26.10% / 31.86% of revenue (p. 33). Manufactured polymer revenue was Rs 254.42 cr / Rs 241.62 cr / Rs 237.00 cr (pp. 406, 408).
- **What it owns:** one plant at Goa on 24,220 sq m of land with eleven product-dedicated lines and 35,251 MTPA of installed capacity; capacity utilisation 75.26% for PE and 73.19% for XLPE in FY26 (pp. 123-124). Raw material (polyethylene) comes mainly from a few domestic petrochemical producers; one supplier was 71.68% of FY26 procurement (p. 39). 175 permanent employees (p. 275).
- **Approvals:** products are enlisted with a Maharatna power-transmission CPSE, a Navratna engineering CPSE and a Miniratna telecom CPSE, among others; enlistment involves product and process testing and takes years (pp. 139, 249).

### Stage of the business

**Classification: scaling, with a capacity expansion under way.**

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Revenue Rs 296.36 cr -> Rs 328.44 cr -> Rs 349.43 cr (8.6% CAGR FY24-26) | Moderate growth, led by trading |
| Manufactured revenue Rs 254.42 cr -> Rs 237.00 cr | The core business has been flat; the expansion is the step-change |
| Operating EBITDA margin 5.54% -> 9.67% -> 11.99%; Q1 FY27 14.46% | Mix and pricing improving |
| Capacity 35,251 -> 87,301 MTPA (+148%) by FY28; utilisation ~75% | Growth depends on filling new lines |
| Operating cash flow positive each year; debt/equity 2.05x -> 0.56x | Self-funding until now; the issue funds the next step |

**What the stage means:** the value rests on filling a large new capacity with manufactured (higher-margin) volume rather than trading. The DCF captures that ramp-up; peer multiples price today's earnings.

## 2. Industry outlook

Source: CRISIL Intelligence report, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 160).
- **Market size:** the global polymer compounding market was USD 75.5 bn in 2025 and is projected at USD 109.8 bn by 2031 (6.7% CAGR). India's market grew from USD 5.97 bn (2019) to USD 9.93 bn (2025) and is projected at USD 16.5 bn by 2031, about 9% a year (p. 179).
- **Demand drivers:** power transmission and renewable build-out (about 170 GW of solar and 47 GW of wind to be added FY26-31), power-cable market growth of about 15-16% a year in value, telecom fibre roll-out (BharatNet), the National Gas Grid, and water pipelines (pp. 160-246).
- **Structure:** a mix of multinational subsidiaries (Kingfa), large domestic compounders (DDev Plastiks, Plastiblends) and many small regional players. CPSE enlistment limits new entrants in power, telecom and pipeline applications (p. 249).
- **Cyclicality:** compounders pass through polyethylene prices with a lag; margins move with resin prices and capacity use. Export markets have raised tariffs in places (Nigeria, Algeria) (p. 51).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Approvals and qualification:** enlistment with CPSEs and utilities for cable and pipe-coating compounds is slow and costly to obtain, which protects incumbents in those segments (p. 249).
- **Product range:** 127 grades across PE, XLPE, HFFR, PVC and coating compounds let it serve a cable maker's full bill of compounds (p. 248).
- **Cost position:** a single plant with dedicated lines; scale will more than double with the expansion. Raw material is a commodity bought from a few large producers, so there is no input advantage.
- **Durability:** moderate. Approvals are a real barrier in regulated segments, but customers are concentrated and the product is specification-driven, so pricing power is limited.

- **Shareholding:** promoters and promoter group 98.53% before the issue, about 71.5% after (pp. 107, 117). Promoters' average cost Rs 2.13-14.93 a share after the 2025 bonus (p. 112). No OFS: no existing holder sells.
- **Related parties:** promoter-group trader Vinsul Makardi Limited (purchases from it Rs 32.35 cr in FY26, 9.26% of revenue; sales to it Rs 9.46 cr in FY24, nil in FY26; Rs 9.00 cr letter of comfort given in FY25, since released) and Basant India Limited (a Rs 8.97 cr loan repaid in FY25, interest of Rs 0.30 cr waived) (p. 89). **Governance read.** A family-run company with long-serving management, clean litigation record and an established auditor. Points to monitor: purchases from a promoter-group trader in the same business, independent directors with group directorships, and post-listing pay as profits grow.

### What would change our view

- **Towards a firmer Avoid:** delays or cost overruns on the expansion, or the trading share rising further.
- **Price:** on today's numbers the call would be Neutral at a price between about Rs 91 and Rs 116 (where Neutral fair value is 10-30% below) and Subscribe at about Rs 90 or below.

- Expansion delay or overrun (p. 29).

- Working capital needs as revenue grows (p. 84).
- Export-market tariffs and geopolitics (p. 51).

## 9. Conclusion

**Avoid at Rs 150 (reported upper band).**

**The investment case.** BLS is a 35-year-old polymer compounder with CPSE approvals in cables and pipe coating, an operating margin that rose from 5.5% to 12.0% in two years, profit that turns into cash, and a balance sheet that has deleveraged from 2.05x to 0.56x debt/equity. The issue is all fresh money and funds a 148% capacity increase at its Goa plant plus working capital. The reported P/E is a fair measure of its earnings.

**Why the price is too high.** The core manufactured business was flat over FY24-26 (growth came from trading resin), and the expansion will take until FY28 to commission. At Rs 150 the post-issue P/E is 34.5x, against 13.0x for DDev Plastiks and a 14.5x peer median; the price already pays for a successful expansion.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 68 / **Rs 81** / Rs 96 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), -45.7% against Rs 150 in the Neutral case. **Call rule:** the Neutral value is more than 30% below the price and the Bull value is also below it, so the call is **Avoid**, on valuation alone. No material issue was found.
