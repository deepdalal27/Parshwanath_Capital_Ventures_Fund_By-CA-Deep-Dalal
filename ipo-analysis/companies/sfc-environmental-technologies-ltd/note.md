## 1. Business of the company

- **What it does:** environmental technology for **wastewater treatment (WWT)**, **wastewater recycling and reuse (WRR)** and **solid waste treatment (SWT)** (pp. 29, 260). In WWT it designs and supplies the core technology package for sewage treatment plants: its proprietary C-Tech sequencing batch reactor (SBR) process, plus decanters, diffusers, turbo blowers, fibre-disc filters, solar sludge dryers and PLC / SCADA automation, followed by supervision, O&M and spares (pp. 261-262).
- **Scale:** 639 WWT installations treating 15,209 MLD at 31-Mar-2025 (577 / 13,907 MLD a year earlier). F&S, in an issuer-paid report, puts its share of India's SBR market above 80% (p. 261).
- **Who buys it:** mainly EPC contractors who win municipal STP tenders (top customers include GVPR Engineers, Vishvaraj Environment, Enviro Control, SMC Infrastructures). Top-10 customers were 61.37% of FY25 revenue and the largest 16.76% (pp. 48, 54). In SWT the customer is Goa Waste Management Corporation (GWMC), for whom it built and runs two plants with up to 425 TPD of capacity; the Saligao plant has run for over 8 years (pp. 261-262).
- **New lines:** exclusive Indian distribution of DuPont ultrafiltration, CCRO and MBR membranes for municipal WRR (first order July 2025); compressed-biogas (CBG) plants: Rs 501.70 cr of EPC + 10-year O&M orders from a public-sector oil marketing company for Tinsukia (125 TPD) and Bhubaneswar (300 TPD), won by subsidiary HWTPL in Apr-May 2025; an agri-residue biogas project in Maharashtra and a Punjab acquisition term sheet (pp. 49, 261-262).
- **Segments, FY25 (p. 450):** WWT Rs 469.21 cr (67%), SWT Rs 119.36 cr (17%), automation (Chavare Engineering) Rs 109.29 cr (16%).
- **Group:** subsidiaries include HWTPL and Vasudha Waste Treatment (SWT), Chavare Engineering (automation), Fine Aeration Systems, Pentagen Biofuels and SFC Umwelttechnik GmbH, an overseas company that was one of the issuer's original 2005 shareholders (pp. 35-36, 114, 508).

## 2. Industry outlook

Industry figures are from the Frost & Sullivan report commissioned and paid for by the issuer (p. 260); we have not checked them independently.

- **Sewage treatment:** India's wastewater-treatment market grew at 5.4% a year over FY20-25 and is projected to grow at 9.6% a year over FY25-30, driven by industrial demand, urban growth and groundwater depletion (p. 29). Municipal STP spending runs through state and central programmes such as AMRUT and the National Mission for Clean Ganga, often on the hybrid-annuity model where the government funds 40% of capital cost (pp. 203-209).
- **Recycling and reuse (tertiary treatment):** projected to reach Rs 4,924 cr by FY30, a 30.3% CAGR from FY25, as states mandate reuse of treated water (p. 29).
- **Municipal solid waste:** revenue potential from biogas, electricity, recyclables and compost projected at Rs 30,300 cr by FY30, an 8.5% CAGR (p. 29). The CBG blending obligation on oil marketing companies is creating a pipeline of MSW- and agri-based CBG plants (p. 262).
- **Competition:** listed players with water / waste businesses include Thermax, Praj Industries and Ion Exchange (p. 168), plus VA Tech Wabag and a long tail of EPC contractors and technology licensors. SFC's position is upstream: it is the technology supplier the EPC contractor specifies at tender stage.
- **Read for an investor:** demand is policy-led and long-term; the cash cycle follows government payment cycles through the EPC contractor.

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

### Moat

- **Installed base and specification:** 639 plants and over 80% of India's SBR installations (issuer-paid F&S estimate) mean consultants and EPC contractors know and specify C-Tech; each plant adds spares and O&M (p. 261).
- **Proprietary process plus key equipment:** C-Tech and OREX are proprietary; the company makes the critical equipment (decanters, diffusers, blowers) and the automation, so it earns technology and equipment margins together (pp. 261-262). WWT segment margin was 34.7% in FY25 (p. 450).
- **Exclusive DuPont membranes** for municipal WRR in India (p. 261).
- **Operating track record in SWT:** an 8-year-old integrated MSW-to-biogas plant, which matters when bidding for CBG plants (p. 262).
- **Limits:** contracts are mostly purchase orders, not long-term; payment depends on EPC contractors and, behind them, municipal budgets (p. 49).

### SWOT

**Strengths**
- Over 80% share of India's SBR STP installations; 639 plants, 15,209 MLD (p. 261)
- Operating EBITDA margin 29.7% and ROCE 29.3% in FY25 (p. 170)
- Net cash of Rs 52.35 cr at FY25 (p. 170)
- Profit taxed in cash: current tax 26.1% of cumulative PBT (p. 364)
- Proprietary C-Tech and OREX processes with in-house key equipment (pp. 261-262)

**Weaknesses**
- Working capital rose to 63.5% of revenue; receivable days 158 -> 197 -> 262 (p. 34)
- Cumulative operating cash flow only about 22% of PAT over FY23-25 (p. 368)
- WWT revenue fell 10.5% in FY25 and the WWT order book fell 30% (pp. 49, 450)
- Top-10 customers 61.37% of revenue; mostly purchase orders (pp. 48-49)
- Two overseas subsidiaries consolidated on unaudited accounts (p. 357)

**Opportunities**
- Tertiary treatment market projected to grow 30.3% a year to FY30 (issuer-paid F&S, p. 29)
- Rs 501.70 cr of CBG plant orders with 10-year O&M (p. 262)
- DuPont-backed WRR offering and first order in July 2025 (pp. 49, 261)
- Agri-residue biogas (Maharashtra land acquired; Punjab term sheet) (p. 262)

**Threats**
- Effluent show-cause notice and FIR against subsidiary HWTPL at the North Goa plant (p. 507; corrigendum)
- EUR 5.60 m (Rs 56.75 cr) damages claim against SFC Umwelttechnik GmbH in Portugal (p. 508)
- Delays in municipal payments passing through EPC contractors (p. 34)
- Technology displacement by newer treatment processes (p. 33)

The points to follow up are the Goa environmental proceedings (operational and customer-relationship risk at one plant), the Portugal claim, and audited accounts for the overseas subsidiaries. - **Scenario drivers:** growth 5-8% / 10-15% / 12-22% a year; operating EBITDA margin 24% / 28% / 30%; working capital 70% / 60% / 50% of revenue; terminal ROIC 15% / 20% / 25%. Rationale and page cites are in the valuation workbook.
- **Why DCF and peers diverge (-67%):** the DCF discounts at 14.3% and charges the business for the working capital its growth needs, which caps it at about 11x FY25 earnings in the Neutral case. Listed water and bio-energy peers trade at 25-58x. The divergence is the finding: the market prices this theme well above what today's cash generation supports.
- **Sensitivity (Neutral DCF):** WACC 12.3-16.3% gives Rs 206-137; working capital at 40% / 50% / 70.5% of revenue gives Rs 185 / 175 / 154; terminal ROIC 25-30% gives Rs 171-175. The discount rate matters most.

- **Reverse DCF at Rs 533:** the price asks the business to deliver 168.6 times its current free cash flow in perpetuity. One lever at a time, it needs perpetual growth of 13.3% (vs 4.5%) or a WACC of 7.5% (vs 14.3%); margin alone cannot get there. **In plain words: a peer-median price assumes SFC is valued like a listed water-and-energy franchise, not on its own cash flows.**

## 9. Conclusion

**Screen only (DRHP stage; no price band).**

**The investment case.** SFC is the dominant technology supplier for SBR sewage treatment plants in India, with 639 installations, a 29.7% operating EBITDA margin, about 29% ROCE, net cash, and profit taxed in cash. The order book more than doubled to Rs 1,267.50 cr by August 2025 as it moved into CBG plants and membrane-based water reuse, both backed by policy. The reported P/E is a fair measure of its earnings.

**What holds the value down.** Working capital has risen from 28% to 64% of revenue, so only about 22% of three years' profit arrived as operating cash; the core WWT business dipped in FY25; and the offer is mostly an OFS by early shareholders whose average cost is Rs 36.72. The Goa environmental proceedings and the Portugal claim need answers in the RHP.

**Valuation.** Fair value Rs 304 / **Rs 330** / Rs 358 (Bear / Neutral / Bull), about 21x FY25 EPS in the Neutral case. **Price guide under the Fund's call rule** (fair value fixed, ignoring the small effect of price on the fresh-share count): **Subscribe up to about Rs 367** (Neutral within 10% of price; 23.8x FY25 EPS), **Neutral from about Rs 367 to Rs 472** (Neutral within 30%; up to 30.6x), **Avoid above about Rs 472**. At the illustrative peer-median Rs 533 the Neutral value is 38.0% lower and the Bull value 32.8% lower, which would read Avoid. We will re-run the screen on the RHP's FY26 numbers and the actual band.
