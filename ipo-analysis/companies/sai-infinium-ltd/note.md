## 1. Business of the company

Sai Infinium makes MS billets, TMT bars (brand "Bandhan") and, since February 2026, structural steel sections (angles, beams, channels, flats) at one site in Chamardi, Bhavnagar, Gujarat, on 3.0 lakh sq m of owned land (pp. 287-288, 299). It also holds a ship recycling yard at Alang-Sosiya (no ship bought since Nov-2023, so no recycling revenue in FY26; sales of opening stock gave Rs 96.62 m in FY25), a real estate inventory of 43 properties (Rs 56.99 m) and trades ferrous and non-ferrous scrap (pp. 293, 298).

For valuation this means FY26 earnings come from the old asset base, while the cost of the new one is already in borrowings (Rs 3,575.72 m at 31-Mar-2026; Rs 5,024.07 m at 30-Jun-2026, p. 50).

## 2. Industry outlook

The industry report (CARE Analytics, "Industry Research Report on Steel Industry", 11-Aug-2026) was **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** (p. 287). It reports finished steel consumption of 164 mt in FY26, up 11.6% in the year and growing 8.5% a year since FY20 (p. 218). TMT bar consumption was 49.2 mt in FY25 (10% a year from FY21) and is projected at 72.6 mt by FY30, 8% a year; billets 86.4 mt in FY25 to 124.5 mt by FY30, also 8% (pp. 235-236). Demand drivers cited are public capex, housing and railways. The same report's section on the issuer lists, in its own words, exposure to scrap and sponge-iron price swings with delayed pass-through, "intense competition limits pricing power" among regional re-rolling mills in Gujarat and Alang, debt-funded project execution risk, and credit ratings "in the BB/BBB range" (pp. 282-283). Infomerics rates the bank facilities of Rs 4,194.40 m at **IVR BBB/Stable** (6-Apr-2026, p. 89). Named listed peers in the DRHP are Vraj Iron and Steel, Rudra Global Infra Products, VMS TMT, Gallantt Ispat, MSP Steel Power and VMS Industries (p. 178). Steel is cyclical; demand is tied to construction. Ship recycling at Alang-Sosiya (about 98% of India's activity) is a small and currently idle part of this issuer (p. 293).

## 3. Moat and SWOT analysis

**Moat: narrow and regional.** What the evidence shows: a semi-integrated site that turns scrap and sponge iron into billets and rolled products (lower conversion cost than buying billets, 98.5% of TMT billet input captive in FY26, p. 311); a dealer network of 226 built since 2022 (p. 296); BIS licences and CII-GreenPro certification (p. 289); and an owned 3.0 lakh sq m site that has room for the expansion (p. 294). What it does not show: scale (108,000 t a year against Gallantt's Rs 44,189 m revenue), pricing power (CARE says it is limited) or a cost advantage in the numbers (gross margin 31.8% includes scrap trading whose margin is not disclosed). Durability is moderate and rests on the captive power plant and billet self-sufficiency, neither yet in place.

**Key personnel and auditors.** Three CFOs between Oct-2024 and Jun-2026 (Singhal, Vidyarthi, Joisher) and two company secretaries since May-2023 (p. 386). Statutory auditors: Parshva Shah & Co and Deepak Goyal & Co (joint, from Sep-2024) both resigned on 20-Nov-2025; Lahoti & Lahoti were appointed on 24-Nov-2025 and resigned on 14-Mar-2026 before starting the audit; ANR & Associates (appointed 16-Mar-2026) audited FY26 and examined the restated numbers. **Related parties and group.** Group entities in the same trades (Honey Ship Breaking, Pratyaksh Ship Breaking, Sai Endeavours) signed non-compete agreements on 25-Jul-2026 and 11-Aug-2026 that bar them from trading metals with the issuer's **top 10 customers** and (for the ship breakers) from ship breaking in Gujarat; the agreements can be varied or terminated (p. 62). Related-party dealings are set out in part 8. Metarex Global, Falvi Infinium and Sai Endeavours were related parties only between March 2025 and May-June 2025 (pp. 470, 391); the DRHP gives no reason for the change and the three together bought Rs 1,824.80 m (26.5% of FY26 revenue) after they left the list, of which Metarex is the largest customer at Rs 1,280.88 m (p. 38).

**Litigation and approvals.** No criminal proceedings or regulatory actions against the company. Disputed statutory dues Rs 53.52 m (ESIC, GST, income tax; p. 499). A civil suit (RCS 154/2025, filed 3-Apr-2025) seeks cancellation of the sale deed for Survey 1027 paiki 3, where Rolling Mill 2 stands (p. 542). The Income Tax Department told the NCLT that Rs 844.10 m of FY21 transactions of a merged company with EP Tradelink were unexplained and that reassessment for AY 2021-22 was contemplated; no notice has been received (p. 543). **Governance read.** Board composition and disclosure are in line with listing requirements. The points to carry into the RHP are the auditor and CFO turnover, the size and two-way nature of group trading, and the loose boundary of the related-party list. Nothing here is a finding of misconduct; each item has a stated reason or a stated approval route.

**Why the methods differ (divergence -90%).** The multiple method prices FY26 earnings at steel-peer multiples and credits the Rs 266 cr of proceeds; the DCF prices cash, and the explicit period burns cash (cumulative FCFF -Rs 331 cr over Y1-Y3, so the terminal value is 2.4 times enterprise value) because the model funds the melting shop, a rebuild of working capital from 7.7% to 19% of revenue and the remaining mill and power spending before the new capacity earns. **The terminal-value guard fires (237% of EV), so the DCF leg is a statement about timing and cost of capital, not a point estimate to lean on.** For that reason the call limb is shown both ways below. Sensitivity (Neutral DCF, Rs per share): WACC 12.5% to 16.5% moves it from 7.4 to 0.4; EBITDA margin from 10.4% to 14.4% moves it from -0.7 to 6.9 at 19% working capital; at FY26's 18.6% margin it would be Rs 14.9, still below one-fifth of the reference.

**Reverse DCF in plain words.** Current free cash flow is negative (FY26 FCFF -Rs 228.9 cr), so no multiple of cash flow can be stated. Holding growth at the Neutral path, the steady-state EBIT margin needed to support Rs 90 is about 80% against 17.2% today, and the working-capital lever is unreachable. In plain terms: none of the three scenarios reaches Rs 90. A price of 32x earnings for a steel maker assumes something the DRHP does not show, for example a durable 18% margin on a much larger structural-steel business.

## 8. Areas to watch and growth drivers

**Growth drivers to track.** (1) Rolling Mill 2 and 3 utilisation and structural-steel realisation per tonne, quarter by quarter. (2) Whether TMT volume and price recover from FY26 (85,093 t at Rs 44,982). (3) Billet self-sufficiency after the melting shop (the object). (4) Power cost per tonne after the wind-solar plant commissions. (5) Dealer additions outside Gujarat.

**Points to monitor** (normal-course items, each reported once, none cutting fair value on its own):

- *Group trading.* Related-party sales Rs 1,593.47 m (23.1% of revenue; 59.1% in FY25), purchases Rs 1,253.88 m (35.7% of purchases; 44.9%), fixed-asset purchases Rs 537.68 m (13.7% of cash capex; 52.4% in FY25), loans taken Rs 173.97 m. Devansh Infinium (a promoter) is customer (Rs 1,086.91 m), supplier (Rs 456.48 m) and capex vendor (Rs 353.55 m). Five of the ten largest customers (Rs 3,375.74 m, 49.0% of revenue) and five of the ten largest suppliers (about 46% of purchases) are or were related. The DRHP says all dealings are at arm's length with Audit Committee approval (pp. 31, 34) and gives no price benchmark. This is a disclosed feature; its size is why it is listed first.
- *Guarantees.* Rs 782.30 m for Devansh Infinium (Rs 450 m), Axat Impex (Rs 332.30 m) and Sai Endeavours (Rs 200 m) (p. 48); all three are customers.
- *Leverage.* Net debt / EBITDA 2.58x at 31-Mar-2026; borrowings Rs 5,024.07 m at 30-Jun-2026 of which Rs 1,708.12 m unsecured and repayable on demand (p. 50). The lender consent from Bank of Baroda for the offer was assumed after no reply (p. 51).
- *Cash tax and bank returns.* Rs 249.29 m of tax unpaid at year end; quarterly stock and book-debt returns to Bank of India differed from books by Rs 66-212 m for stock, explained as excluded divisions (p. 499).

**What would change our view** (measurable, checkable in the RHP):

1. A price band at or below about Rs 25 would put the rule in the Neutral limb or better; each Rs 10 above that reduces Neutral upside by about 10 percentage points (thresholds in part 9).
2. A split of FY26 gross profit and EBITDA between TMT, structural steel, billets and scrap, with the purchase cost and customers of the Rs 1,519.78 m of non-ferrous scrap; if scrap earned less than the EBITDA margin the Neutral 12.4% margin is supported, if more, the three-year margin is too high.
3. Customer-level evidence that Metarex, Falvi and Sai Endeavours (Rs 1,824.80 m after leaving the related-party list) are unrelated, or inclusion of them in the list.
4. H1 FY27 results: structural steel above 30% of Rolling Mill 2's capacity, positive operating cash flow after tax, and borrowings no higher than Rs 5.0 bn.
5. The auditors' resignation letters and ADT-3 filings, and an explanation of why two joint auditors left on the same day.
6. A resolution of the Rs 844.10 m tax observation and the RCS 154/2025 suit.

## 9. Conclusion

**Call: Screen only** (no price band). At the Rs 90 reference the rule in the standing instructions would read **Avoid**, on the second limb: the Neutral blended value of Rs 17.26 is 80.8% below the price (more than 30%), and the Bull value of Rs 23.83 is also below it. The same limb applies if the DCF leg is set aside and the relative value of Rs 31.41 is used (-65%). No check is a FAIL: the items above are disclosed, in proportion to a regional steel maker with a large group, and none is a misstatement, qualification or undisclosed dealing.

**Price thresholds under the rule (Neutral blended Rs 17.26; Bull Rs 23.83):** Subscribe at or below about Rs 19; Neutral up to about Rs 25 (or while the Bull value is at or above the price); Avoid above that. On relative value alone (Rs 31.41) the thresholds would be Rs 35 and Rs 45. The fair value range from the three scenarios is Rs 13 to Rs 24 per share (Rs 31 on the multiple method alone).

**What decides it.** First, whether FY26's 18.6% EBITDA margin is the new level or a one-year result helped by scrap sales and low commission; the issuer's own report describes pricing power as limited. Second, whether the Rs 10 bn-plus build (mills, power plant, melting shop) earns its cost of capital once the group trading flows around it are seen at arm's length. Neither can be settled from the DRHP.
