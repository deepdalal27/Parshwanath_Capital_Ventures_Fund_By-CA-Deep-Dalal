# AITMC Ventures Limited: UDRHP-I screen

> **Basis.** Updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus-I dated 30-Sep-2026 (SEBI filing page dated 30-Sep-2026), book-built fresh issue of up to 3,50,00,000 equity shares of Rs 2, Khandwala Securities Limited as sole BRLM. Restated financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **191 days old** at the date of this note. **No price band has been announced** and the issue price, offer expenses and general corporate purposes amount are blank in the document. FY26 is **standalone**; FY25 and FY24 are **consolidated** (p. 81), so year-on-year comparisons mix two bases. All figures are from the UDRHP-I; printed page numbers are cited, and "Annex." refers to the restated-financials annexure.
>
> Upside is measured against **Rs 44.29**, the objects floor (identified objects of Rs 155.03 cr divided by 3.50 crore fresh shares, before offer expenses and general corporate purposes). It is a reference point, not a price band. The last secondary transfers (Apr-Jun 2026) were at **Rs 17.50** (p. 207). Exit route assumed: 2-3 year hold. Grey market premium is not used.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The RHP must carry financials no more than six months old and should therefore include H1 FY27. The company filed an earlier draft in October 2023 for NSE Emerge (SME); the document does not state what became of it (Definitions, "Previous DRHP").

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** contracted, fast growth in a policy-supported niche.
- Revenue grew from Rs 41.87 cr in FY24 to Rs 106.76 cr in FY26 (2.5x); PAT from Rs 8.63 cr to Rs 13.38 cr (p. 84).
- The company holds a 20-year concession for 20 Uttar Pradesh institutes and an MoU for 50 AICTE labs; Rs 27.74 cr is already deployed (pp. 134-135, 145).
- Profit is taxed: current tax is 32.0% of cumulative PBT, and there are no contingent liabilities (p. 84; Annex. 43).
- Reg 6(1) eligibility reproduces: average operating profit Rs 18.38 cr against the Rs 15 cr test (p. 459).

**Concerns:** growth has not yet turned into cash, and the numbers lean on a few counterparties.
- Operating cash flow was -Rs 5.90 cr, -Rs 6.59 cr and +Rs 2.67 cr; cumulative CFO is -25.7% of cumulative PAT (pp. 86-87).
- Ten customers are 83.58% of FY26 revenue; related parties supply 24.3% of purchases and hold Rs 8.06 cr of supplier advances (pp. 49-50, 89-92).
- FY26 PBT fell 24.8% and PAT 17.1% despite revenue up 22.0%; gross margin fell from 55.66% to 46.45% (pp. 84, 205).
- The objects need at least Rs 44.29 a share; Neutral fair value is Rs 17.71.

The two readings separate on price. If the company can clear Rs 44.29 and convert receivables and advances to cash, growth carries it. At the Rs 17.50 secondary price the P/E is about 11.7x on FY26 earnings, but the issue cannot raise its stated need at that price. **The H1 FY27 cash flow, the named customer list and the vendor-wise advance schedule in the RHP will show which reading holds.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** two verticals. Vocational education and skill development was 96.82% of FY26 revenue (Rs 103.36 cr); drone manufacturing and assembly was 3.18% (Rs 3.40 cr), down from Rs 10.40 cr (11.89%) in FY25 (p. 49).
- **Who buys it:** central and state government bodies and private entities. In FY26 Government customers were 26.84% of revenue (58.36% in FY24) and private customers 69.97%; the document says the FY26 rise in private revenue came from supplying IT equipment for "Future Technology Laboratories" under government-funded skilling projects, so that demand still depends on public funding (p. 50).
- **How it earns:** training fees under government schemes (NSDC, PMKVY, state missions), delivered partly through subcontracting or back-to-back arrangements (p. 50). Cost of services was Rs 27.72 cr in FY26, of which "infra partner expenses" were Rs 23.23 cr (Annex. 34). At the AV labs, course fees are shared 50:50 with the host institution, which carries faculty and facility costs (p. 137). FY26 also carried Rs 27.21 cr of stock-in-trade purchases of IT equipment (Annex. 36), at a lower margin than training: gross margin fell to 46.45%.
- **What it owns vs outsources:** a Gurugram drone facility (installed capacity 600 units; production 4 to 100 a quarter in the table, p. 301), three DGCA-recognised remote pilot training sites in Jhajjar, Bhuna and Hisar, and a Hisar site being built as the Future Tech Park, which also houses 23 planned vendor-operated component units (p. 272). Training centres sit in host institutions' premises.
- **Drone products:** the VIRAJ agricultural drone has DGCA type certification; 162 units were deployed and 28 other drones sold through FY26 (p. 271). Other variants await certification.
- **Perimeter changes:** FY26 is shown standalone after a former subsidiary (Farmers City International Private Limited, sold in FY25 for Rs 0.64 cr net cash, p. 87) left the group. A company with that name now sells services to the issuer and holds Rs 1.85 cr of advances from it (p. 92).

## Industry analysis

Source: "Industry Report - Skill Development and Commercial Applications of Drones" by Dun and Bradstreet, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 267). Treat its estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** the report projects the Indian drone market at Rs 2,95,000 cr by 2030, a 35% CAGR from 2025, of which Rs 2,48,000 cr domestic; defence is the largest segment at Rs 1,01,000 cr, commercial Rs 75,000 cr. It puts agriculture drones at Rs 26,800 cr by FY2030 (39% CAGR) (p. 239).
- **Structure:** the issuer names two small listed peers, Drone Destination (FY26 revenue Rs 35.07 cr) and Droneacharya Aerial Innovations (Rs 14.67 cr), and states there is no like-for-like listed company in skill and vocational training (p. 204). Training competitors named in the document are government institutions, private providers and sector skill councils (p. 303).
- **Regulation and demand drivers:** DGCA Remote Pilot Certificates are legally required to operate drones (p. 268); demand for training depends on government schemes, drone-subsidy programmes and tender cycles. A production-linked incentive scheme for drones and components exists (p. 233).
- **Cyclicality:** budget-linked. Government receivables are paid after verification (see below).

## Moat

- **Licences and empanelments:** DGCA RPTO recognition, NSDC empanelment and a type-certified drone are real barriers to a new entrant, but they are available to any qualifying competitor; the document does not claim exclusivity.
- **Concession and MoU:** the UP concession (20 years, 20 institutes) and the AICTE MoU give access to sites. Their economics depend on the terms, which the document summarises but does not publish in full.
- **Scale and cost:** none evident. Gross margin fell 9.2 points in two years and the issuer says no like-for-like listed comparable exists.
- **Switching costs, brand, network:** none evidenced in the document.
- **Durability:** access-based rather than structural; the evidence for a durable moat is limited at this stage.

## Revenue and profit growth drivers

- **Revenue:** FY24 to FY25 +108.9% (consolidated), FY25 to FY26 +22.0% (standalone against consolidated). Vocational revenue rose Rs 26.3 cr in FY26; drone revenue fell Rs 7.0 cr. Within FY26 growth, Rs 27.21 cr was IT equipment bought for resale (nil in FY25), so volume of core training grew by less than the headline. A split of training revenue between price and number of candidates trained is not given (gap).
- **Gross margin:** 55.66% / 54.63% / 46.45%. The FY26 decline coincides with traded IT equipment and higher infra-partner costs.
- **Operating leverage:** other expenses were 11.9% of revenue in FY26 (Rs 12.73 cr) against 11.5% in FY25, including an expected credit loss provision of Rs 3.80 cr and Rs 1.31 cr of balances written off (Annex. 41). Finance cost rose from Rs 1.89 cr to Rs 4.28 cr and depreciation from Rs 4.09 cr to Rs 6.49 cr as the labs were built.
- **One-offs:** FY24 other income of Rs 4.87 cr included Rs 4.42 cr of liabilities written back, 33.1% of FY24 PBT (p. 86). The issuer's EBITDA of Rs 16.71 cr in FY24 includes that income; excluding all other income it is Rs 11.84 cr.

## Financials and key findings

**Positives**
- Revenue and PAT scale: Rs 106.76 cr and Rs 13.38 cr in FY26.
- Cash tax: current tax Rs 17.56 cr over three years is 32.0% of PBT.
- Capital is being deployed against named assets: Rs 27.74 cr spent on AV labs, UP institutes, R&D labs and the park site, funded by two bank term loans.
- Equity was raised from promoters' loans converted at Rs 5 per share in FY24 and FY25 (Rs 10.19 cr and Rs 10.50 cr), strengthening net worth.

**Concerns**
- **Cash conversion.** Cumulative CFO is -Rs 9.82 cr against PAT Rs 38.16 cr. Capital advances appear to be inside operating cash flow rather than investing (the cash-flow line "other non-financial assets" is -Rs 26.41 cr in FY26 and matches the balance-sheet rise in capital and supplier advances). Adding capital advances back gives FY26 CFO of Rs 12.26 cr and FY25 of Rs 6.93 cr, still below PAT. FY24 cannot be adjusted because the opening balance is not shown.
- **Tax paid vs charged.** Income tax paid was Rs 11.07 cr over three years against Rs 17.56 cr charged; the current-tax liability is Rs 6.86 cr at FY26 (Annex. 31).
- **Receivables.** Gross receivables Rs 66.11 cr; Rs 14.10 cr has been outstanding 1-3 years with authorities "pending verification and assessment" and is carried as good with no specific provision (Annex. 14). Overall ageing from due date: 51.6% not due, 12.6% 0-6 months, 6.3% 6-12 months, 10.8% 1-2 years, 11.9% 2-3 years, 6.9% over 3 years. The FY26 expected-credit-loss provision of Rs 4.23 cr (Rs 3.80 cr charged in the year) covers only the "significant increase in credit risk" bucket.
- **Advances.** Supplier advances rose from Rs 7.07 cr to Rs 22.17 cr and capital advances from Rs 16.22 cr to Rs 25.81 cr in FY26, together 68.2% of net worth. Rs 8.06 cr of the supplier advances sits with related parties. The vendors and delivery dates are not disclosed.
- **Concentration.** Top-1 customer 18.73%, top-5 71.13%, top-10 83.58% (88.55% in FY24); names withheld for want of consent (p. 50). Delhi 58.47% of revenue (p. 301).
- **Basis mixing.** FY26 standalone vs FY25 consolidated: the revenue and profit growth rates above compare unlike perimeters. The issuer's KPI table does the same (p. 205).
- **Margins.** The KPI table defines EBITDA as profit "less other income" but the figures equal PBT + finance cost + depreciation, with other income left in (FY24 Rs 4.87 cr, FY25 Rs 1.32 cr, FY26 Rs 0.51 cr).

**Document errors found in re-performance**
- The restated P&L shows FY26 cost of services as Rs 27.22 cr; Annexure 34 totals Rs 27.72 cr, and total expenses of Rs 89.49 cr only reproduce with Rs 27.72 cr. The error is in the document (a digit transposition on the P&L line), not in our extraction.
- Basis for Issue Price (p. 206): FY24 basic EPS of Rs 20.41 is on the pre-split count (42.05 lakh shares) and is weighted with Rs 1.91 and Rs 1.50 on 8.98 crore shares to give Rs 4.79. On the post-split Rs 1.76 the weighted figure is about Rs 1.68. The EPS numerators also use total comprehensive income (Rs 13.51 cr in FY26) rather than PAT (Rs 13.38 cr).
- Weighted average cost of acquisition (p. 206): the table prints Rs 1,090.00 lakh for the March 2024 allotment of 2,03,80,000 shares at Rs 5, which is Rs 1,019.00 lakh (the cash-flow statement agrees); the printed total of Rs 2,060.00 lakh should be Rs 2,069.00 lakh. The summary table then shows "NA" for the same primary issuances.
- Top-10 shareholder percentages (p. 129) do not match the share counts (for example 41,50,500 shares shown as 1.73% is 4.62% of 8,97,55,000).
- Upgrade status of UP institutes: the objects chapter says 9 ITIs and 3 polytechnics are partly done (p. 145); the business chapter says 14 ITIs and all 3 polytechnics (p. 269).
- The issuer's working-capital table shows net current liabilities of Rs 55.36 cr at FY26; total current liabilities less short-term borrowings are Rs 48.46 cr (p. 39). The table does not reproduce.

## What would change our view

The call moves from Screen only to a decision at the RHP. These conditions can be tested against it:
- **Price:** a band floor at or above Rs 44.29 would need FY26 P/E (post-issue) near 41x to be defended; a band whose floor is below the objects need would require the objects to be cut or the share count raised.
- **Cash conversion:** H1 FY27 CFO positive and cumulative CFO/PAT above zero after reclassifying capital advances to investing.
- **Receivables:** the Rs 14.10 cr outstanding 1-3 years collected or specifically provided; closing receivable days below 150.
- **Counterparties:** names and related-party status of the top-10 customers disclosed, and the top-10 share below 70%.
- **Advances:** a vendor-wise schedule for the Rs 22.17 cr supplier advances and Rs 25.81 cr capital advances, with delivery dates.
- **Comparability:** FY25 and FY24 restated on the same (standalone or consolidated) perimeter as FY26, with the terms of the Farmers City International disposal.
- **Related parties:** related-party share of purchases falling from 24.3% and the basis for pricing explained.
- **Documents:** the errors listed above corrected, including the weighted EPS, the WACA table and the cost-of-services line.
- **Margin:** gross margin at or above 50% in H1 FY27 with traded equipment shown separately.
