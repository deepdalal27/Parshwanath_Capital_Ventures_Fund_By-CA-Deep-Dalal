# Royal Chain Limited: DRHP screen

> **Basis.** Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 28-Sep-2026, filed with SEBI on 01-Oct-2026. Restated standalone financials run to 31-Mar-2026 and are **187 days old** at the date of this note. **No price band has been announced.** Figures are from the DRHP (printed page numbers) in ₹ million unless stated.
>
> Upside is measured against **₹147.33**, the December 2025 pre-IPO placement price adjusted for the 5:1 bonus. It is a reference point, not a price band. Exit route assumed: 2–3 year hold.
>
> A DRHP is a screen, not a decision. The RHP must carry financials no more than six months old and should therefore include H1 FY27.

## Verdict: Screen only. The evidence is mixed.

**Strengths:** fast and well-documented growth.
- Revenue grew at a 41.9% CAGR over FY24–FY26, from ₹23,505.82 m to ₹47,324.50 m.
- PAT grew at a 151.7% CAGR, from ₹273.62 m to ₹1,733.42 m.
- Tax was paid in cash at 26.9% of cumulative PBT.
- The company supplies listed organised jewellers.
- At the ₹147.33 reference, post-issue P/E on FY26 PAT is about **15.6x, in line with the 15.72x peer median**.

**Concerns:** growth has not yet turned into cash.
- Operating cash flow was negative in each year: −₹115.67 m, −₹30.49 m and −₹2,165.17 m.
- Inventory nearly doubled in FY26.
- Volume sold was broadly flat across the three years.
- The FY26 margin rise coincided with a rising gold price.

On a cash basis (DCF) value is below the reference; on an earnings basis (peer multiples) it is above it. **H1 FY27 operating cash flow and margin in the RHP will show which reading holds.**

## Business model

- **What it sells:** a business-to-business (B2B) original design manufacturer of gold chains and plain gold jewellery in 14, 18 and 22 karat, with over 27,000 designs. In-house brands include Rico, SAR and Anamitra (pp. 189, 107).
- **Who buys it:** in FY26, 1,887 customers.
  - Dealers and wholesalers: 89.55% of revenue.
  - Institutional and corporate clients, including Titan, Kalyan, Senco, P N Gadgil and Thangamayil: 6.83%.
  - Export: 3.79% (p. 107).
  - The dealer network covers 24 states (p. 189).
- **How it earns:** a manufacturing and design margin on gold. FY26 gross margin was 7.84% of revenue (p. 111). Revenue largely passes the gold price through, so reported revenue moves with bullion prices as well as volume. Gold is funded by working-capital debt and, from FY26, gold on lease (₹742.28 m) (p. 64).
- **What it owns:** a single integrated plant at Mahape, Navi Mumbai (88,980 sq ft) with 12,000 kg capacity, covering refining, chain-making, casting, CNC/laser work and polishing. It employs 723 permanent staff and 30 designers (p. 191). In FY25, purchases of stock-in-trade (₹19,129.94 m) exceeded materials consumed (₹16,081.27 m), so part of revenue is traded rather than manufactured (p. 65).
- **Subsidiary:** Aalishaan Jewels LLP (plain gold jewellery) became a subsidiary with a 99% profit share from 01-Apr-2026 (p. 190). It was a related party, and the company's largest customer, in FY26 (p. 32).

## Industry analysis

Source: a report by The Knowledge Company LLP, **commissioned and paid for by the issuer** for this offer (p. 189). Treat its market estimates accordingly.
- **Market size:** the Indian jewellery market is put at ₹7,778.4 bn in FY26, projected to reach ₹15,479.7 bn by FY31 (14.8% CAGR). Organised B2B jewellery manufacturing is put at ₹1,390.6 bn, projected at an 18.5% CAGR to ₹3,252.7 bn (p. 190).
- **Structure:** fragmented. About 65% of the organised B2B market is held by medium and small players, and the top two hold about 16.5%. Royal Chain's share is about 3.4% (pp. 190–191). The organised share of B2B manufacturing is expected to rise from 23.9% to 28.7% by FY31 (p. 190).
- **Listed peers named in the DRHP:** Sky Gold & Diamonds, Shringar House of Mangalsutra and Shanti Gold International (p. 110).
- **Unnamed competitors:** other chain manufacturers, retailers' own manufacturing, and the large unorganised sector.
- **Cyclicality and regulation:** demand and reported revenue track gold prices, wedding and festival seasons, and import-duty and hallmarking rules.

## Moat

| Source | Evidence | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| Scale and capacity | Among the top five organised B2B makers by capacity; 12,000 kg (pp. 189, 191) | Moderate. Capacity is replicable, and FY26 output used about 33% of it |
| Process know-how | Hollow and super-light chain capability; imported Italian chain machinery (pp. 189, 191) | Moderate. The technology can be bought; execution is the edge |
| Customer relationships | Supplies seven named organised retailers; 7 of FY24's top-10 customers were still top-10 in FY26 (p. 107) | Moderate. B2B suppliers can be switched, but qualification takes time |
| Design library | Over 27,000 designs, 30 designers (pp. 107, 191) | Low to moderate |
| Brand | In-house brands (Rico, SAR, Anamitra), but sales are mostly B2B | Low. The end consumer buys the retailer's brand |

Overall the DRHP evidence supports a **narrow, execution-based moat**: scale, process and relationships. It does not show pricing power, and margins depend substantially on gold-price movements.

## Revenue and profit growth drivers

- **Revenue: price and mix, not volume.** Volume sold rose 1.7% from FY24 to FY26 (4,397.22 → 4,473.67 kg). Revenue per kg rose 97.9%, from ₹5.35 m to ₹10.58 m (pp. 65, 190). The DRHP does not split this between the gold price, purity mix and traded bullion.
- **Manufactured mix:** chain output went from 3,413.02 kg to 2,563.08 kg, and ornaments from 1,077.79 kg to 1,441.73 kg (FY24 → FY26, p. 190). The shift is towards non-chain jewellery, supported by Aalishaan from FY27.
- **Profit:** the operating margin expansion came from **gross margin**, which rose from 4.62% to 7.84%. Other costs stayed roughly constant as a share of revenue: employee costs were 0.53% → 0.43% and other expenses 1.38% → 1.32% (pp. 65, 111). The DRHP does not quantify how much of the gross-margin gain came from holding inventory while gold prices rose. Finance costs fell from 1.07% to 0.80% of revenue even as debt rose.
- **Forward drivers named by the company:**
  - the shift of retail to organised formats;
  - the Aalishaan consolidation;
  - debt reduction from the issue (₹6,500 m), which lowers finance cost;
  - spare capacity.

## The offer and objects

- **Size:** ₹10,000 m in total. Fresh issue ₹8,500 m; offer for sale ₹1,500 m by four promoter sellers (p. 61).
- **Eligibility:** Regulation 6(1). QIB portion no more than 50%, non-institutional at least 15%, retail at least 35%.
- **Anchor portion:** up to 60% of the QIB portion, of which 33.33% is reserved for mutual funds and 6.67% for insurers and pension funds (p. 61). About ₹1,800 m of anchor allocation is open to other investors. Allocation is discretionary, and anchor shares are locked in, 50% for 30 days and 50% for 90 days.
- **Objects:** ₹6,500 m repays or prepays borrowings in FY27, including a ₹895.55 m Axis Bank working-capital loan. The balance goes to general corporate purposes (capped at 25%) and offer expenses (pp. 95–99). There is no capex object.
- **Shares and holders:** 125,344,092 shares before the issue, after a 5:1 bonus on 08-Sep-2026. Promoters hold 93.19% (pp. 78, 82).
- **Pre-IPO placement:** 1,221,720 shares at ₹884 (₹147.33 after the bonus) to LMJF Capital Advisors LLP on 02-Dec-2025 (p. 79).

## Valuation

All values are ₹ per share on 18.30 crore post-issue diluted shares at the ₹147.33 reference.

| Scenario | DCF | Relative | Blended 60:40 | vs ₹147.33 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 40.49 | 173.90 | 93.85 | −36.3% |
| Neutral | 69.45 | 173.90 | 111.23 | −24.5% |
| Bull | 130.39 | 173.90 | 147.79 | +0.3% |

- **DCF.** WACC is 15.78%:
  - cost of equity 18.20% (risk-free 6.5% + 1.1 × 7.0% + 2% size premium + 2% company-specific);
  - pre-tax cost of debt 8.15%.

  Terminal growth is 4.50%. The Neutral case assumes revenue growth easing from 18% to 9%, an EBITDA margin of 4.5% and NWC at 19% of revenue. Terminal value is 81.2% of EV.
- **Relative.** Uses the DRHP's peer set (p. 110). The median P/E of 15.72x gives ₹148.83 and the median P/B of 3.92x gives ₹281.99, with a 10% discount applied. EV/EBITDA can't be computed because peer debt and EBITDA are not in the DRHP.
- **Why DCF and relative differ by 60%.** Relative value prices reported FY26 earnings. The DCF prices cash after working capital, which absorbed about ₹0.42 of every incremental rupee of revenue in FY26. Which of the two is closer to right depends on whether cash conversion improves.
- **Reverse DCF.** FY26 free cash flow to the firm was −₹2,738.8 m, so the reference price cannot be expressed as a multiple of current cash flow; it relies on future cash generation. In steady state the price needs an EBIT margin of about 10.4%, against 5.6% in FY26.
- **Sensitivity.** One point of EBITDA margin moves Neutral value by about ₹20.60 per share; one point of WACC moves it by ₹6–7.

## What would change our view

Test each of these against the RHP:
1. Restated H1 FY27 operating cash flow is positive (supportive), or negative again (adverse).
2. Closing inventory days on revenue at 30-Sep-2026 compared with 53.7 at Mar-26 and about 36 at Mar-25.
3. H1 FY27 EBITDA margin, and disclosure of any gold-price holding gain or loss within it.
4. The price band, compared with the Neutral blended value (₹111) and the peer-median P/E (15.72x).
5. Like-for-like revenue growth excluding Aalishaan consolidation.
